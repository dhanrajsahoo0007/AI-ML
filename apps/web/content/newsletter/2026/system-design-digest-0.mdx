Welcome to issue zero. This is the shape every digest will take: one theme, three pieces from the library, and a
question to take into your next design review.

## This week's theme: where the writes go

Most storage choices come down to one question: do you pay for a write now, or later? The two answers sit at the
heart of nearly every database you will be asked about in an interview.

**[B-Trees](/system-design/databases/b-trees)** pay up front. Every write finds its page and updates it in place,
so reads stay cheap and predictable. That is why relational databases have relied on them for decades.

**[LSM Trees](/system-design/databases/lsm-trees)** pay later. Writes land in memory and are flushed as sorted,
immutable files, and background compaction tidies up afterwards. Write throughput goes up; the bill arrives as
read and space amplification.

Whichever you pick, a write-heavy system eventually meets more demand than it can absorb. **[Back Pressure and
Load Shedding](/system-design/patterns/back-pressure)** covers what to do next: bounded queues, telling producers
to slow down, and deciding which work is safe to drop.

## A question to take with you

Your service ingests a burst of events ten times its normal rate for five minutes. Which part of the design
absorbs the burst, which part slows the producers down, and what do you drop first if both fill up?
