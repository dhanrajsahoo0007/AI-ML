## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
AI, ML, and Data Engineer behavioral interviews test whether you treat machine learning as an **empirical engineering discipline** rather than a Kaggle leaderboard game: interviewers probe how you debugged **offline-to-online metric divergence**, caught **silent data leakage or distribution shift**, chose a **simple baseline over a flashy neural architecture**, and built **trustworthy LLM/agent evaluation loops**.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

In behavioral and past-project rounds for Applied AI Engineers, ML Systems Engineers, and Research Engineers at Meta, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, Stripe, and DoorDash, generic software stories aren't enough. The interviewer will ask:
- *"Tell me about a time your offline model metrics improved significantly, but the online A/B test was flat or negative."*
- *"Tell me about a time you discovered data leakage, label noise, or train-serve skew."*
- *"Tell me about a time you advocated for a simpler heuristic, linear baseline, or prompt pipeline over a complex model."*

Candidates who only talk about model architecture tweaks (swapping LoRA ranks or attention heads) fail this round. The strongest AI/ML candidates talk about **looking directly at raw failure examples**, **data pipelines**, **latency/FinOps envelopes**, and **aligning offline proxy metrics with real user utility**.

| **4. LLM Evals & Guardrail Engineering** | Building stratified golden eval sets, pairwise human/judge calibration, and deterministic PII/hallucination circuit breakers | "Vibe-checking" 5 prompts in a notebook and deploying straight to production |

## Worked engineering example (STAR+L)

<Tabs title="AI/ML Signature Stories: Offline-Online Gap vs. LLM Eval Rigor">
  <Tab label="Debugging an Offline-vs-Online A/B Test Inversion">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time a model or ranking improvement looked great offline, but failed in an online A/B test."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"On our e-commerce search ranking squad, I trained a cross-encoder transformer reranker that improved offline NDCG@10 by **+4.8%**: our largest offline gain all year. However, when we ran a 5% online A/B test, gross merchandise value (GMV) per session dropped by **-1.9%**."*
    - **Action:** *"Instead of discarding the model or blaming test noise, I ran a **3-hypothesis forensic decomposition**:
      1. **Train-Serve Skew Check:** I logged live feature tensors on 10,000 shadow queries and compared them against our offline replay join. Parity was 99.9%.
      2. **Latency Elasticity Slicing:** I bucketed the A/B test results by device network speed and p95 latency. I found our cross-encoder added **185 ms at p95**; on mobile cellular connections, every 100 ms of search latency increased query abandonment by 1.4%, completely wiping out our relevance gain!
      3. **Exposure Bias in Offline Labels:** Furthermore, our offline golden set was sampled from historical clicks on top-3 results, penalizing novel long-tail items.
      To fix both bottlenecks, I distilled the cross-encoder into a 2-layer INT8 quantized dual-encoder with dynamic token pruning for queries over 6 words, and gated reranking to the top 25 candidates instead of top 100."*
    - **Result:** *"Reranker p95 latency dropped from 210 ms to **18 ms**, and our follow-up A/B test delivered a statistically significant **+2.3% GMV lift (+$14M annualized)** while preserving +4.1% NDCG@10."*
    - **Learning:** *"Offline ranking metrics assume zero latency cost. In production, latency IS a quality feature: always evaluate offline accuracy gains inside a hard latency-elasticity budget."*
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Catching Silent Data Leakage & Building an LLM Cascade">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you discovered a major flaw in an evaluation benchmark or training pipeline."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"Our applied AI team was fine-tuning an open-weights 13B LLM for automated clinical insurance prior-authorization extraction. A teammate reported 96% F1 on our held-out validation split, but early hospital pilot users complained the model hallucinated policy codes on new patient forms."*
    - **Action:** *"I pulled 100 validation examples and their nearest neighbors in the training split using MinHash LSH and discovered **patient-level temporal leakage**: the dataset had been split randomly by document ID rather than by `(patient_id, clinic_template_id)`. Because patients submitted 4–5 follow-up forms using the exact same clinic header template, the model was memorizing clinic boilerplate shortcuts rather than reading the clinical notes!
      1. **Rebuilt the Eval Harness:** I re-partitioned the benchmark strictly by **out-of-distribution clinic + temporal cutoff**, which revealed our true generalization F1 was only **71%**.
      2. **Simpler Hybrid Architecture:** Rather than scaling up to a 70B fine-tune, I designed a deterministic 2-stage pipeline: an OCR layout-aware regex/rules extractor for structured ICD-10 header tables, paired with a schema-constrained LLM call strictly for unstructured physician narrative sections, backed by a citation-span verifier that highlighted the exact source sentence for every extracted field."*
    - **Result:** *"True out-of-clinic generalization F1 jumped from 71% to **93.8%**, human nurse review time dropped by **64%**, and our citation-span verifier eliminated ungrounded policy code hallucinations."*
    - **Learning:** *"Random row splits are the #1 source of illusory ML progress. Always split evaluation data along the exact generalization boundary your production system will face on Day 1."*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Introspect your own work

<Quiz title="Audit your AI/ML stories for senior engineering depth">
  <QuizItem
    question="1. In your ML/LLM project stories, can you state the exact baseline you compared against before introducing a complex model?"
    hint="Interviewers immediately ask: 'What did a simple heuristic, regex, BM25, or zero-shot prompt score on that same eval set?'"
  >
    Always open your **Action** section with the **Baseline Ladder**: *"First, I stood up a deterministic BM25 / logistic regression baseline in 1 day, which achieved X%. That gave us the exact error budget to justify Y."*
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem
    question="2. When you talk about an LLM or GenAI application you built, how do you prove you didn't rely on 'LLM-as-a-Judge' blindly?"
    hint="Frontier and applied AI interviewers know LLM judges suffer from length bias, self-preference bias, and position bias."
  >
    Explain how you calibrated your automated judge against a **human-labeled golden set of 200–500 stratified examples** (measuring Cohen's kappa / Spearman correlation) and swapped answer positions to eliminate position bias.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>

## Common red flags & how to recover

- **Reciting textbook paper summaries as your own work:** If you used a standard architecture (e.g., RAG with hybrid search or LoRA fine-tuning), don't spend 10 minutes explaining how LoRA works: spend 10 minutes on **your data curation, failure taxonomy, latency optimization, and production guardrails**.
- **Ignoring the failure mode of non-determinism:** Every AI system fails on the tail. Always explain how the UX and backend handle low-confidence predictions safely (e.g., abstention, human-in-the-loop fallback, or deterministic citations).

<InterviewQuestions>
  <InterviewQuestion
    question="What is 'Goodhart's Law' in ML/AI systems, and how do I use it in a behavioral story?"
    level="Senior"
    companies={["Meta", "Google", "OpenAI", "Anthropic", "Netflix"]}
  >
    **Goodhart's Law** states: *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."* In ML/recsys, optimizing pure click-through rate (CTR) rewards clickbait that hurts long-term retention; in RLHF/LLMs, optimizing helpfulness ratings without a conciseness/truthfulness guardrail causes sycophancy and verbose hallucinations. Sharing a story where you caught a model gaming its proxy metric and introduced a **counter-balancing guardrail metric** is a top-tier Senior/Staff signal.
  </InterviewQuestion>

  <InterviewQuestion
    question="How do I answer: 'Tell me about a time an ML/AI experiment failed completely'?"
    level="Mid"
    companies={["Google", "Meta", "Amazon", "DoorDash"]}
  >
    In empirical ML, 70%+ of hypotheses fail in A/B testing. Show that you **designed the experiment to fail fast and cheaply** (e.g., an offline replay or interleaved test in 3 days rather than a 6-week pipeline build), extracted a concrete **negative result insight** about user behavior or feature signal, documented it so nobody repeated the dead end, and pivoted to the winning hypothesis.
  </InterviewQuestion>
</InterviewQuestions>
