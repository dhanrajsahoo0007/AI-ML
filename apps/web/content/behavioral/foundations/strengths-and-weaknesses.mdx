## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  Self-awareness questions are answered with **evidence**: a strength is a claim plus a story that proves it, and a
  weakness is a real fault plus **the habit you changed because of it**.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

An interviewer asks three candidates for their biggest weakness.

- The first says, "I work too hard and I care too much about quality." The interviewer has heard it four times that week and writes "no real answer".
- The second says, "I am not great with deadlines, honestly." It is real, but nothing follows it, and for a role with weekly releases it is a reason to say no.
- The third says, "I hold on to work too long before showing it. Last spring I spent two weeks on a design that a ten-minute review would have redirected. I now post a rough draft within two days of starting anything, and my last three designs changed direction early because of it. I still feel the pull to polish first."

The third answer is the only one that gives the interviewer something to write that helps the candidate.

## The shape of a weakness answer

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/behavioral/weakness-answer-arc"

  minWidth={700}
  caption="A weakness answer takes about a minute, and most of it is the habit you changed."
/>

1. **Name it.** A real weakness, in plain words, without a joke or an apology.
2. **The cost.** One time it hurt your work or someone else's. This is what makes it believable.
3. **The change.** The specific habit, tool or rule you use now. A promise to "be more careful" is not a habit.
4. **Where it stands.** Better, and not finished. Saying it is fully fixed makes it sound like it was never real.

**Choosing the weakness.** It should be real, something you have worked on, and not the main skill of the job. "I avoid conflict in code review" is workable for most engineering roles. "I struggle to debug production issues" is not workable for an SRE role.

## Strengths: a claim, a proof and a limit

"What are you best at?" is answered the same way in reverse.

- **The claim**, as something you do, not something you are: "I am good at finding the cause of problems that cross several services."
- **The proof**, in two sentences: one piece of work with its result.
- **The limit**, in one sentence: when this strength gets in your way. "It means I can go too deep before asking for help." The limit is what makes the claim sound honest, and it often answers the weakness question before it is asked.

Choose strengths the role needs. Read the job description, mark the three things it asks for most, and pick the two you can prove.

## The questions behind the question

| They ask                                           | They want                                                       | Shape of the answer                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| "What is your biggest weakness?"                   | A real fault and proof you act on it                            | Name, cost, change, where it stands                    |
| "What would your manager say you need to work on?" | Whether you have actually been told, and whether you listened   | Quote the feedback, then what you changed              |
| "What would your teammates say about you?"         | Whether your view of yourself matches theirs                    | One strength and one criticism they have really voiced |
| "Tell me about feedback that was hard to hear."    | How you react when corrected                                    | The feedback, your first reaction, what you did        |
| "What are you best at?"                            | Whether the strength is real and useful here                    | Claim, proof, limit                                    |
| "Where do you see yourself in five years?"         | Whether this role is on your path, and whether you have thought | The kind of problems you want, not a job title         |

For the second and third questions, use real words from a real review if you have them. "My last review said I take on too much myself and should hand work to newer teammates sooner" is more convincing than anything you could invent.

## Build your own

1. Reread your last two performance reviews, or ask one colleague: "What is one thing I should do differently?" Write down their words.
2. Pick one item that is real and is not the main skill of the job you want.
3. Write the four parts: name, cost, change, where it stands. Read it aloud in under 70 seconds.
4. Write two strengths as claim, proof and limit.

Put the "cost" moment into the [Story Builder](/behavioral/practice-lab/story-builder?for=growth) as a full story. It doubles as your answer to "tell me about feedback that was hard to hear".

## Red flags

- **The disguised strength.** "I work too hard." **Recover:** if you hear yourself saying it, add a real cost: "and it meant I burned out a teammate by setting that pace".
- **A weakness with no change.** **Recover:** say what you are trying now, even if it is new.
- **A list of three weaknesses.** One is asked for. Three sounds like a confession.
- **"I cannot think of one."** It reads as no self-awareness or no honesty. Prepare one.
- **A strength with no proof.** "I am a great communicator" is an adjective. Give the piece of work.

## Common questions

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="Can I use a technical gap as my weakness?">
    Yes, if it is next to the role and you are closing it: "I have little experience with frontend performance. I took
    our slowest page as a side task last quarter and cut its load time by a third." A gap in the core skill of the job
    is a poor choice.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="What if they ask for a second weakness?">
    Have one ready. It tests whether the first was rehearsed. Use the same four parts, shorter.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="How do I answer 'five years from now' without sounding fake?">
    Describe the size and kind of problem you want to be trusted with, and say this role is a step toward it. You do not
    need a title or a plan.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
