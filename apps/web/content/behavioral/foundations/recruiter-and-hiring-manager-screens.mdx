## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  The recruiter screen and the hiring manager screen are not warm-ups: the first sets **the level and the pay range you
  will be considered for**, and the second decides **whether a team wants you**, before any scored interview happens.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

A senior engineer takes a recruiter call from his car. Asked what level he is aiming for, he says "whatever you think fits". Asked about pay, he names his current salary. Asked about timing, he says "no rush".

Four weeks later he passes the loop and gets an offer one level below what he wanted, built on the number he named, with no urgency on the company's side. Every one of those outcomes was set in the first 25 minutes.

Nothing he said was wrong. He treated a conversation that sets terms as small talk.

## What each conversation decides

1. **Recruiter screen.** Checks that you are a possible fit, and records the role, the level and often a pay expectation. The recruiter is on your side once you are in process: they are measured on hires.
2. **Hiring manager screen.** A manager checks your work and your reasons. It is half behavioral interview, half a conversation about the team.
3. **The loop.** The scored interviews. The rest of this section is about these.
4. **After the loop.** At some large companies you are matched to a team after the interviews, in short calls with managers. Then the recruiter returns with the numbers.

Not every company has all four. Small companies often merge the first two.

## The recruiter screen: questions and answers

| They ask                                              | What it decides                  | How to answer                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Walk me through your background."                    | Whether to pass you on           | Your 90-second introduction                                                |
| "Why are you looking, and why us?"                    | Motivation notes for the file    | One specific reason, moving toward something                               |
| "What level are you targeting?"                       | Which loop you get               | Name the level and one sentence of evidence for it                         |
| "What are your compensation expectations?"            | The range you are tested against | See below                                                                  |
| "Where else are you interviewing, and how far along?" | How fast they need to move       | The truth, in general terms: "two other processes, one at the final stage" |
| "What is your timeline and notice period?"            | Scheduling                       | A real date. If you have a deadline from another company, say so.          |

**On pay expectations.** In some places employers must share the range, and in some they may not ask your current pay; the rules differ by country and state, so check yours. In general:

- You do not have to give a number first. "I would like to learn more about the level and the scope first. What range is budgeted for this role?" is a normal answer, and many recruiters will give the range.
- If you are pressed, give a researched range for the level, not your current salary, and say it depends on the whole package.
- Do not lie about your current pay or about other offers. Recruiters sometimes ask to see an offer letter.

**On level.** If you say "whatever fits", you will usually be tested at the lower of the two levels you could be. Say the level you want and why: "Senior. I have led work across three teams for the last two years."

## The hiring manager screen

The manager is asking one question: "Would this person do well on my team?" Expect:

- **A walk through one project in depth**, with follow-ups on what you did yourself and what you would change.
- **Why this team.** A reason about the team's work, not the company in general.
- **What you are looking for in your next role.** Say the kind of problem and the kind of team.
- **How you work.** How you handle disagreement, how you like to get feedback, what you do when you are stuck.
- **Your questions.** This is the best place to ask them. See [High-Signal Questions to Ask Your Interviewer](/behavioral/foundations/questions-to-ask-interviewers).

Treat it as a scored behavioral interview. Use full stories with results. The manager often writes feedback that the loop's interviewers read.

## Team matching

Where it exists, team matching is a series of short calls with managers after you pass the interviews. Two things to know:

- **It runs both ways.** You are choosing as well. Ask what the team will ship in the next six months, how on-call works, and why the role is open.
- **It can take time, and it can end without a match.** Ask your recruiter how long it usually takes and what happens if your interview results expire. Keep other processes going until you have a written offer.

## Red flags

- **Taking the call unprepared.** **Recover:** if a recruiter calls without warning, ask to schedule 30 minutes for the next day.
- **Leaving the level to them.** **Recover:** email the recruiter after the call: "On reflection I am targeting senior, and here is why."
- **Naming your current salary.** **Recover:** you cannot unsay it, but you can add the range you expect for the new level.
- **Stopping other processes** during team matching or before a written offer.
- **Being rude or dismissive to the recruiter.** They write notes that the hiring manager reads, and they argue for you at the offer stage.
