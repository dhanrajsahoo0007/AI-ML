## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  Your introduction is **90 seconds in three parts: now, before, next**. Its job is to hand the interviewer two or three
  things worth asking you about, so the rest of the conversation starts on your best work.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

Two candidates with the same background answer "tell me about yourself".

The first starts at university, walks through four jobs in order and reaches the present at minute four. The interviewer, who has the résumé open, has learned nothing new and has 41 minutes left.

The second says what she owns today, names two pieces of work with a result each, and says why she applied. It takes 85 seconds. The interviewer's next question is about the second piece of work, which is the story she most wanted to tell.

Both were honest. The second one chose what the interviewer would ask next.

## The three parts

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/behavioral/intro-ninety-seconds"
  stepper
  minWidth={700}
  caption="Each part of the introduction leaves the interviewer something to ask about. The middle part is the one you choose on purpose."
/>

1. **Now, about 20 seconds.** Your role, your team, and what you are responsible for, with one number that gives the size. "I am a backend engineer on the payments team at a 300-person company. I own the service that settles about 2 million transactions a day."
2. **Before, about 45 seconds.** Two pieces of work, one sentence each, each with a result. Pick them for the role you want: if the role is about reliability, one of them is a reliability story. This is the part you design.
3. **Next, about 25 seconds.** Why this role and this company, tied to something in the first two parts. One specific reason is better than three general ones.

Leave out your degree unless you graduated in the last two years, every job before the last two, and anything the interviewer cannot ask a useful question about.

## A weak answer, then the fix

<Tabs title="The same person, twice">
  <Tab label="Weak answer">
    *"So, I studied computer science and graduated in 2017. My first job was at a consultancy where I did a bit of
    everything, mostly Java. Then I moved to a startup, which was great experience, I learned a lot there. We used
    microservices and Kubernetes. After that I joined my current company in 2021, where I have worked on several
    projects across different teams. I am a hard worker and a fast learner, and I am passionate about technology. I am
    now looking for a new challenge."*

    - It is in time order, so the least relevant part comes first.
    - No piece of work is named, so there is nothing to ask about.
    - "Hard worker", "fast learner" and "new challenge" could be said by anyone.

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Fixed">
    *"I am a backend engineer on the payments team at a logistics company. I own the settlement service, which handles
    about 2 million transactions a day. Two pieces of work I would point to: last year I led the move of that service
    off a nightly batch onto a stream, which cut settlement delay from 9 hours to 4 minutes. Before that, I found and
    fixed a reconciliation gap that had been losing us about 11 thousand dollars a month. What I enjoy most is making
    money systems correct and fast, and your team runs that problem at a hundred times my current scale, which is why
    I applied."*

    - It starts with the present and takes about 40 seconds to reach the reason for applying.
    - Two pieces of work, each with a number, each a question waiting to be asked.
    - The reason for applying follows from the work.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Build your own

1. Write one sentence on what you own today. Add the number that shows its size.
2. List five pieces of work. Circle the two that are closest to the role you are applying for.
3. Write one sentence for each, ending with the result.
4. Write one sentence on why this role, starting from something in step 1 or 3.

Say it out loud with a timer. If it passes 100 seconds, cut the first sentence of the middle part: it is usually context. Each of your two pieces of work should also exist as a full story in the [Story Builder](/behavioral/practice-lab/story-builder?for=motivation), because the next question will be about one of them.

## Red flags

- **Reading your résumé in order.** The interviewer has it. **Recover:** stop at the present and say "the two things I would point to are".
- **Running past two minutes.** A long introduction takes time from the questions that are scored. **Recover:** close with your reason for applying and stop.
- **A reason that fits any company.** "A great culture and interesting problems" says you have not looked. **Recover:** name one thing the team works on.
- **Personal history.** Hobbies and family are fine if asked. The opener is about your work.

## Common questions

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="Should I change the introduction for each company?">
    Keep the first part. Change which two pieces of work you choose and rewrite the last part for each company. That
    takes ten minutes and it is the part interviewers notice.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="What if they ask 'walk me through your résumé'?">
    It is the same question with a different order. Go through the résumé forwards, but spend one sentence on each older
    job and keep most of your time for the last two.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="I am changing fields. What goes in the middle?">
    The two pieces of work closest to the new field, even if they were side projects, and one sentence on what carries
    over from the old one.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
