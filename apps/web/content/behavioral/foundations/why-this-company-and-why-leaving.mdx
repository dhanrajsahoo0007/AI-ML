## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  A good answer to "why us" joins **one specific thing the team does** to **one thing you have done**, and the same link
  answers "why are you leaving": you are moving **toward** that work, not away from a complaint.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

A recruiter asks two candidates why they want to join a company that builds developer tools.

The first says the company has a great culture, smart people and a product he admires. Every candidate that week said the same three things.

The second says: "I spent last year cutting our CI time from 40 minutes to 12, and I found I liked that more than the product work. Your build system team wrote about remote caching in March, and that is the problem I want to work on full time." The recruiter writes down "specific, has read our work, relevant experience".

The second answer took 20 seconds and needed about 30 minutes of reading beforehand.

1. **Their work.** Find one concrete thing: an engineering blog post, a talk, an open-source project, the scale of a system, a problem named in the job description. The mission statement does not count, because everyone quotes it.
2. **Your work.** Pick the piece of your own work that is closest to it.
3. **The link.** One sentence on what you want to do next and why this team is where it happens.
4. **Leaving.** Say it as the same link from the other side. You are leaving because you want more of that work than your current job has.

Where to look, in about 30 minutes: the company's engineering blog, the team's open-source repositories, recent conference talks by its engineers, the job description itself, and the company's published values or principles.

## Why are you leaving?

The interviewer is listening for two things: will you speak badly of this company one day, and are you running from a problem you helped cause.

| Your real reason                | Say                                                                                     | Do not say                                   |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **A bad manager**               | "I want a team where design reviews are a habit. I have missed that."                   | Anything about the manager as a person       |
| **No growth**                   | "I have owned the same system for three years and I want a larger one."                 | "They would not promote me"                  |
| **Pay**                         | "I want a role with more scope." Leave pay for the offer stage.                         | "I am underpaid"                             |
| **A layoff**                    | "My team was cut in the March reduction, along with about 200 others."                  | An apology, or a long explanation            |
| **A short stay (under a year)** | "The role changed after I joined: it became on-call support. I should have asked more." | Blaming the company for misleading you       |
| **A gap in your history**       | "I took eight months to care for a parent. I kept current by doing X."                  | A vague "personal reasons" with nothing more |

A layoff needs one sentence, said plainly. It is common and interviewers know it. A short stay needs one sentence on what happened and one on what you check now before you accept a job, because the second sentence shows it will not repeat.

## Build your own

1. Spend 30 minutes on the company's engineering blog, talks and job description. Write down one specific thing.
2. Write the piece of your work that is closest to it, with its result.
3. Join them in one sentence that starts "I want to".
4. Write your reason for leaving as the same sentence from the other side. Check it has no person and no complaint in it.

Keep the piece of work from step 2 as a full story in the [Story Builder](/behavioral/practice-lab/story-builder?for=motivation). "Tell me more about that" is the usual next question.

## Red flags

- **A reason that fits any company.** "Great culture, smart people, interesting problems." **Recover:** add one thing only this team does.
- **Praise for the product as a user.** Liking the app is not a reason to build it. **Recover:** move to the engineering problem behind the feature you like.
- **Speaking badly of your employer.** Even when it is deserved. **Recover:** say what you want more of.
- **A different reason in each round.** Interviewers compare notes. Decide your reason once and keep it.

## Common questions

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="What if I am applying to many companies and do not have a strong reason for this one?">
    You still need 30 minutes of reading per company. A modest, specific reason ("your team owns its own deploy tooling,
    and I want to work somewhere that does") is enough. A grand, general one is not.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Should I mention that I was laid off?">
    Yes, in one sentence, if you are asked why you left. Give the size of the cut if you know it. Then move to what you
    want next.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="They asked 'why not stay and fix it?'">
    Say what you tried. One concrete attempt, and what happened, shows you did not leave at the first difficulty.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
