## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
**Invent and Simplify** is the ability to delete moving parts by reframing the problem rather than adding infrastructure, while **Think Big** connects a bold, 10x multi-year architecture vision to a pragmatic week-two first milestone.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

When an engineering team is asked to support a new product requirement, their default instinct is almost always **additive**: add another microservice, add another Kafka topic, add another caching tier.

Two years later, a single user click traverses 19 microservices, nobody can run the stack on a laptop, and on-call engineers spend half their week debugging distributed serialization mismatches.
Then a Senior Engineer steps back and asks: *"Why do we have three separate stateful services passing JSON over HTTP when all three read the same 40 MB configuration dataset that changes once an hour?"* They replace three microservices and a Redis cluster with an immutable SQLite snapshot pushed to S3 and memory-mapped locally inside the gateway, deleting 14,000 lines of code, cutting <Term id="p99" /> latency from 45 ms to 0.2 ms, and saving \$8,000/month.

That is **Invent and Simplify**: true invention is measured not by how many boxes you draw on the whiteboard, but by **how many boxes you safely erase**.

## Worked engineering example (STAR+L)

<Tabs title="Simplification vs. 10x Platform Vision">
  <Tab label="Invent & Simplify (Deleting Moving Parts)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you invented a simple solution to a complex technical problem."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"Our analytics ingestion pipeline used a 12-node Spark Streaming cluster + Redis dedup store to compute real-time 15-minute merchant fraud counters at 3,000 events/sec. It cost \$11k/month and paged on-call twice a week whenever Spark checkpoint state corrupted."*
    - **Action:** *"During a post-mortem, I questioned why we needed exact event-ID deduplication and heavy JVM streaming for a sliding 15-minute fraud heuristic. I proved on 30 days of historical fraud labels that a probabilistic HyperLogLog + 1-minute tumbling window in plain Go workers had 99.8% recall parity with exact state. I built a 400-line stateless Go consumer backed by Postgres unlogged tables in 5 days."*
    - **Result:** *"We decommissioned the entire Spark Streaming cluster, cut monthly infra spend from \$11k to \$650/month (94% reduction), reduced end-to-end lag from 18 seconds to 400 ms, and had zero on-call pages over the next 9 months."*
    - **Learning:** *"Always audit whether a business problem actually requires exact distributed state before reaching for a stateful streaming engine."*
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Think Big (Turning Point Fixes into a Platform)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you proposed a bold vision that looked beyond the immediate request."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"Product asked my team to build a custom GDPR data-deletion workflow for our Orders service in 6 weeks. While scoping it, I noticed 7 other domain teams had the exact same compliance mandate on their Q3 roadmaps, each planning to build bespoke S3/Postgres scrubbers."*
    - **Action:** *"I wrote a 4-page PR/FAQ proposing a unified, declarative **Privacy Deletion Orchestrator** where any service simply registered an idempotency webhook and a retention schema in YAML, while our central engine handled audit logs, retries, cryptographic erasure proofs, and SLA tracking. To avoid delaying our Q2 Orders deadline, I built the orchestrator with Orders as Tenant #0 in 5 weeks."*
    - **Result:** *"Orders passed compliance on time, and all 7 sibling teams onboarded in under 3 days each instead of spending 6 weeks apiece, saving ~40 engineering-weeks across the org."*
    - **Learning:** *"The best way to sell a Think Big platform vision is to fund Milestone 1 out of a concrete single-team deliverable."*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Introspect your own work

<Quiz title="Spot hidden simplification & vision opportunities">
  <QuizItem
    question="1. If you were forced to delete 30% of the services, queues, or tables in your current architecture tomorrow, which ones would you merge or remove?"
    hint="Where is your team paying a distributed-systems tax for data that fits in memory?"
  >
    That thought experiment is the fastest way to uncover your next *Invent and Simplify* story.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem
    question="2. Look at the last feature you built. Are 2 or 3 other teams in your company solving a 80%-similar problem in parallel?"
    hint="L6+ engineers look horizontally across sibling teams' roadmaps."
  >
    Extracting a shared primitive from parallel team pain is how Senior/Staff engineers practice *Think Big*.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>

## Common red flags & how to recover

- **Confusing "Invent" with "Not-Invented-Here Syndrome":** Building your own custom database or message broker when a standard managed service fits the bill is a red flag. Explain why off-the-shelf options didn't fit your specific constraint.
