## The questions, by principle

### Customer Obsession

Start from what the customer needs and work backwards from there, even when an internal shortcut would be easier.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you delivered something different from what the customer asked for.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time a customer asked for something you thought was unreasonable. What did you do?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you pushed back on a technically convenient shortcut because it would have hurt the end user or customer.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you anticipated a customer or developer pain point they hadn't explicitly asked for yet, and built a solution for it.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you went well beyond what was expected of you for a customer.

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/customer-ownership) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/customer-obsession-and-ownership)

### Ownership

Act for the whole company and the long term. A problem is never "not my job".

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you handed over work you owned to someone else. How did you make sure it went well?
- **Mid.** Describe a time you diagnosed and fixed a problem that lived outside your team's codebase or charter because it was blocking a larger outcome.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time something you were responsible for broke in production. How did you handle it?
- **Senior.** Describe a time you sacrificed a short-term team win or easy metric boost to protect long-term system health or company value.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you took the lead on a project. What did you do that a team member would not have done?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/customer-ownership) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/customer-obsession-and-ownership)

### Invent and Simplify

Look for new ideas everywhere, and make things simpler than you found them.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you automated a task to save your team time.
- **Mid.** How have you used generative AI to improve your own work or your team's work? Give me one specific example.
- **Senior.** Describe a time you replaced a complex, expensive, or over-engineered design with a significantly simpler one under tight resource constraints.
- **Senior.** Pick a product or feature you know well. If you ran it, what would you improve?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/invent-think-big-frugality) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/invent-simplify-and-think-big)

### Are Right, A Lot

Use good judgment, look for views that differ from yours, and try to prove yourself wrong.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a project where you found out partway through that the goal was wrong. What did you do?
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time your judgment was wrong. How did you find out, and what did you do?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you actively sought out data or perspectives that proved your own initial technical hypothesis wrong.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time a colleague disagreed with your design. How did you know your design was the right one?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/dive-deep-judgment) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/dive-deep-and-are-right-a-lot)

### Learn and Be Curious

Keep learning, and go into areas you do not know yet.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly to finish a task.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time you stepped far outside your primary technical stack or domain to debug a problem or ship a feature.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time you took a risk and it did not work out. What did you learn?
- **Senior.** What have you taught yourself recently, and why did you choose it?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/people-curiosity-responsibility) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/frugality-and-curiosity)

### Hire and Develop the Best

Raise the bar with each hire, and help the people around you grow.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you taught a teammate something or helped them get better at their work.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time you got a group to work well together on a project. What did you do?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you raised the engineering bar on your team through mentorship, hiring calibration, or fixing a systemic operational risk.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a hire who did not work out. How did you handle the low performance?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/people-curiosity-responsibility) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/hire-develop-and-best-employer)

### Insist on the Highest Standards

Hold a quality bar that others may find too high, and fix problems so they stay fixed.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you finished a piece of work and felt you should have done more.
- **Mid.** How do you make sure that what an AI tool generates for you is correct before you use it?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you insisted on holding a launch or rejecting a design because the quality, reliability, or operational readiness was not high enough.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you refused to accept the way something had always been done.

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/action-results-standards) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/highest-standards-vs-speed)

### Think Big

Set a direction large enough to change the result, and say it clearly.

- **Junior.** Tell me about an ambitious goal you set for yourself. Did you reach it?
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time you pushed your team to take a bigger risk than they were comfortable with.
- **Senior.** Describe a time when you noticed multiple teams solving the same narrow problem and proposed a larger, unified vision.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a long-term plan or vision you proposed. How did you get it started?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/invent-think-big-frugality) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/invent-simplify-and-think-big)

### Bias for Action

Move quickly when a decision can be reversed. Many decisions can.

- **Junior.** Tell me about something you started on your own, before your manager asked for it or approved it.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time something broke in production and you had to act before you knew the full cause.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you had to make an important technical decision quickly with less than 70% of the data you wished you had.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you chose to move fast and accept a known risk. How did you limit the damage in case you were wrong?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/action-results-standards) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/bias-for-action-and-deliver-results)

### Frugality

Do more with less. Limits on money, people or time can lead to better designs.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you solved a problem with what you already had, when the easy answer was to spend money or ask for more.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time you had to deliver with fewer people, less budget or less time than the work needed.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you found money or effort being wasted and put a stop to it.

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/invent-think-big-frugality) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/frugality-and-curiosity)

### Earn Trust

Listen, speak plainly, and say openly where you got it wrong.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you took on more than you could deliver.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time you made an architectural or estimation error that affected another team, and how you communicated it to maintain their trust.
- **Mid.** Tell me about the hardest critical feedback you have received. What did you change, and how do you know the change worked?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a team or stakeholder who did not trust you or your team at the start. How did you change that?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/backbone-trust) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/have-backbone-and-earn-trust)

### Dive Deep

Know the details, check them often, and be suspicious when a number and a story disagree.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a problem where the first explanation was wrong and you had to keep digging to find the real cause.
- **Mid.** Which technical problem have you found hardest to solve? Walk me through how you got to the answer.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time when high-level dashboards looked healthy, but you noticed an anomaly in the details that uncovered a serious bug or architectural flaw.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time an AI tool gave you a result that was wrong. How did you find out, and what did you do next?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/dive-deep-judgment) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/dive-deep-and-are-right-a-lot)

### Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit

Challenge a decision you think is wrong, with respect. Once it is made, support it fully.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time your team chose an option you had argued against. What did you do after the decision?
- **Mid.** Tell me about a technical choice where you and a teammate wanted different things. What did you do, and how did it end?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you strongly disagreed with a technical or product decision made by your manager or a senior engineer. How did you handle it, and what happened?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time a stakeholder asked for something you believed was wrong, and you said so.

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/backbone-trust) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/have-backbone-and-earn-trust)

### Deliver Results

Focus on what matters most and finish it, on time and to the right quality, despite setbacks.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time the deadline was fixed and the work did not fit. What did you leave out, and how did you decide?
- **Mid.** Tell me about a time when a key dependency, partner team, or technical assumption failed halfway through a critical project, and you still delivered results.
- **Mid.** Tell me about a commitment you made and did not meet on time. What happened?
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you had several important things to deliver and could not do them all. Which did you pick, and why?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/action-results-standards) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/bias-for-action-and-deliver-results)

### Strive to be Earth's Best Employer

Make the team safer, fairer and more productive for the people in it, and care about their growth.

- **Junior.** Tell me about a time you noticed a teammate was having a hard time. What did you do?
- **Mid.** Tell me about a change you made, without being asked, that made your team fairer or easier to work in for other people.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time you noticed your team's operational load, on-call rotation, or developer experience was hurting morale, and what mechanism you built to fix it.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a delivery that put your team under heavy pressure. What did you do to look after the people?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/people-curiosity-responsibility) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/hire-develop-and-best-employer)

### Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility

Think about the effects of your work beyond your customers, and act to make them better.

- **Mid.** Tell me about a time you changed a design or a decision because of its effect on people the original plan had not considered.
- **Senior.** Tell me about a time when what was good for the business conflicted with what was good for users, society or the environment. What did you do?
- **Senior.** Imagine you are asked to grow usage of a product you work on. Before you ship a change that does it, what would you check apart from the usage number?

[Practise these with answers and follow-ups](/interviews/amazon-leadership-principles/people-curiosity-responsibility) · [Read the page](/behavioral/amazon-leadership-principles/broad-responsibility-at-scale)

## Common questions

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="Do I need a different story for each of the 16?">
    No. One story often shows two or three principles. What you need is to know, for each principle, which story you
    would use and which part of it you would stress.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Will the interviewer say which principle they are asking about?">
    Usually not. The wording tells you: "outside your area" points to Ownership, "with less" to Frugality, "disagreed"
    to Have Backbone. The exercise above trains this.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Are all 16 asked of engineers?">
    Amazon says the principles apply to every role. In the candidate reports we collected, the two newest principles
    come up far less often for engineers than the other fourteen, so prepare them last.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
