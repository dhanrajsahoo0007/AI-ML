## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
**Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** tests whether you proactively engineer for second-order societal, privacy, security, accessibility, environmental, and AI-safety impacts before a 1-in-10,000 edge case harms thousands of real people at scale.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

At a 50-user startup, a 0.01% edge case happens once a year. At Amazon, Google, or OpenAI scale—serving 100 million requests an hour—a 0.01% edge case happens **10,000 times every single hour**.

Suppose an engineer builds an LLM customer-support summarizer that logs raw prompt context to an internal analytics table so data scientists can debug bad summaries. At small scale, it looks like harmless debugging telemetry. At scale, those raw prompts contain unredacted credit-card numbers, medical disclosures, and auth tokens accessible to 400 internal analysts.
An engineer who embodies **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** doesn't wait for a compliance audit or a data breach to care: during the initial RFC, they build a deterministic PII/secret scrubbing pipeline with differential access controls and a 7-day auto-TTL—leaving the system better, safer, and more trustworthy than they found it.

| **Resource & Carbon Footprint** | Eliminating wasteful zombie compute jobs, runaway retry storms that DDoS partner systems, or unnecessary multi-TB data duplication |

## Worked engineering example (STAR+L)

<Tabs title="Privacy/Security vs. AI Fairness & Scale">
  <Tab label="Catching Cross-Tenant PII Leakage">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you identified a secondary impact or ethical/security risk of your team's work and took action."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"We were launching an AI auto-complete feature for enterprise support agents. To speed up response time, the initial design cached prompt-completion pairs in a shared Redis cluster keyed by the first 64 characters of the prompt."*
    - **Action:** *"During a pre-launch code review, I realized that two agents at different companies typing a standard greeting ('Hi, I am following up on your account number...') could collide on the 64-character prefix and receive cached completions containing another tenant's customer name or invoice details. Even though launch was 4 days away, I flagged the cross-tenant PII risk, wrote a reproduction test proving the collision, and re-keyed the cache with an HMAC of `tenant_id + user_role + full_prompt_hash` plus an automated regex PII scrubber before any entry was written to Redis."*
    - **Result:** *"We shipped on the original launch day with zero cross-tenant exposure, and passed our SOC2 Type II audit the following month."*
    - **Learning:** *"I added a mandatory multi-tenant cache-key linter rule across our monorepo requiring `tenant_id` in every cache namespace."*
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Preventing Downstream Ecosystem Harm">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you realized a technical design would negatively impact an external partner or community at scale."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"Our webhook delivery service retried failed merchant endpoints up to 20 times with fixed 5-second intervals. When small indie merchants experienced a brief database hiccup, our 50,000-webhook retry wave acted like a DDoS attack that kept their servers down for hours."*
    - **Action:** *"I analyzed webhook failure logs and saw our aggressive retries were causing 40% of small-merchant outages. I redesigned our retry policy to use exponential backoff with full jitter, added per-destination concurrency caps and circuit breakers that paused delivery when a merchant's endpoint returned 503 for 60 seconds, and exposed a self-serve 'replay when ready' button in the merchant dashboard."*
    - **Result:** *"Webhook recovery success rate rose from 74% to 98.6%, outbound retry bandwidth dropped by 62%, and small-merchant support tickets about webhook floods fell to zero."*
    - **Learning:** *"At scale, an unthrottled client retry loop is indistinguishable from an attack; every outbound caller must respect the receiver's capacity."*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Introspect your own work

<Quiz title="Second-order responsibility audit">
  <QuizItem
    question="1. If a malicious user or compromised API key sent 10,000 crafted requests to your service tonight, what is the worst thing that could happen to other users' data or availability?"
    hint="Think about tenant isolation, PII in logs, and noisy-neighbor starvation."
  >
    Auditing your service's blast radius under abuse is one of the highest-leverage exercises a Senior+ engineer can run.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem
    question="2. Does your product or model work equally well for users on a 3G mobile connection, using a screen reader, or speaking with a non-US accent/locale?"
    hint="Slicing error rates by locale, device tier, or accessibility mode often uncovers massive blind spots."
  >
    Check your metrics across the bottom 5% device/network/locale cohorts—not just the median.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
