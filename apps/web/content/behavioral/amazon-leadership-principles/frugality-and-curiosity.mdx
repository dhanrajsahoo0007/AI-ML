## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  **Frugality** means you get more done with less, and you treat a limit on money, people or time as a reason to design
  better. **Learn and Be Curious** means you keep learning things outside your area, and you **use** what you learn on a
  real problem.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

Give a team eight engineers and no budget limit, and a lookup service tends to become eight small services that cost \$40,000 a month to run.

Give the same problem to two engineers with a cap of \$1,500 a month. They cannot afford to run eight services, so they study the workload first. They find that 2 GB of data answers 97 percent of requests. Neither of them has worked with memory-mapped files, so one spends three days reading how they behave under load and testing them on a copy of production traffic. They keep the hot data in memory on two small instances and serve 15,000 requests a second. The rare request for cold data takes 200 ms where it used to take 20.

The limit did two things. It forced a simpler design, and it forced someone to learn something. That is why these two principles sit well together in one story.

1. **Frugality.** A log cluster kept everything on fast disks. Keeping only 12 hours there took the cost from \$38,000 to \$14,000 a month.
2. **Learn and Be Curious.** A backend engineer learned phone audio code in two days and used it to fix a battery problem.
3. **Where they pull apart.** Learning costs time and sometimes money, with no promise of a return. Frugality asks what you got for it.
4. **A strong answer holds both.** Give the learning a limit, such as two days, and measure what it paid back.

### Frugality

In our words: achieve more with less. A constraint makes people resourceful and inventive, and there is no credit for a bigger team, a bigger budget or a bigger fixed cost.

| A strong story shows                                                       | What weakens it                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| The saving came from a better design: tiers, batching, a cheaper algorithm | The saving came from switching off staging or alerts |
| A cost per unit, before and after: per request, per tenant, per GB         | "We saved a lot"                                     |
| Reliability and speed held, or you say exactly what got worse              | Cheaper and slower, and nobody measured it           |
| You were told no to more budget and found another way                      | Asking for more people was the first move            |

### Learn and Be Curious

In our words: you are never finished learning. Keep improving yourself, be curious about what else is possible, and go and find out.

| A strong story shows                                                  | What weakens it                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| You learned something outside your area and used it on a real problem | A list of courses, books and blogs |
| How you learned: what you read, the experiment you ran, who you asked | "I picked it up quickly"           |
| What changed in production because of it                              | Nothing shipped                    |
| You passed it on: a guide, a talk or a pairing session                | The knowledge stayed with you      |

The same story with the design in it:

- **Situation and task.** _"Our log search cluster cost \$38,000 a month. Finance had frozen any expansion, and log volume was growing 20 percent a quarter."_
- **Action.** _"I profiled 30 days of queries. 91 percent of searches touched only the last 6 hours of logs. The other 13 days sat on fast disks for audits that happen a few times a month. I proposed two tiers: 12 hours on the fast disks, and everything older compressed in object storage behind a slower query path. Before building it, I showed the audit team what a 40-second search would look like, and they agreed. I moved one log source first and compared results for a week."_
- **Result.** _"Over the next year volume grew 2.4 times and the monthly cost fell from \$38,000 to \$14,000. Recent searches got about 30 percent faster because the indexes were smaller. Searches on old logs went from 3 seconds to about 40."_
- **Learning.** _"Storage proposals on our team now state a cost per GB queried."_

### Learn and Be Curious

**Question:** _"Tell me about a time you had to learn something unfamiliar quickly to solve a problem."_

<Tabs title="Learn and Be Curious">
  <Tab label="Weak answer">
    *"I love learning. I read engineering blogs every morning, and I did two online courses on machine learning last year. When our audio feature had a battery problem, I picked up the mobile side quickly and fixed it."*

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Fixed">
    - **Situation and task.** *"I am a backend engineer. Our on-device wake word feature was draining Android batteries by 4.8 percent an hour. The launch limit was 1 percent, and the one audio specialist on the team was on leave."*
    - **Action.** *"I had never worked on audio or on phone power use. I gave myself two days to learn before I touched the code. I read the source of the voice detection library we used, set up a power profiler on a test phone, and asked two firmware engineers how a phone decides to wake its fast processor cores. The profiler showed our bridge code copying an audio buffer every 10 ms, and each copy woke a fast core. I changed it to share one buffer and to hand over 80 ms of audio per wake-up. One of the firmware engineers reviewed it."*
    - **Result.** *"Drain fell to 0.6 percent an hour with no loss in detection, and we shipped on the date. My first attempt had been a lower sample rate. It cut detection accuracy by 6 points, and I reverted it."*
    - **Learning.** *"I wrote a three-page guide to power profiling for backend engineers. Two teammates have used it since."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

What changed:

- The weak answer lists what the candidate reads. The fixed one names a problem, a two-day limit and three sources.
- The learning was used: a profiler finding, a code change and a number.
- It includes a first attempt that failed, and it ends with the knowledge passed on.

## Build your own

1. Find out what your team's main service costs each month, and what the largest single cost is. Write one change that would cut it.
2. Think of a time you were told no to more people or budget. Write what you did next.
3. Name one layer below your daily work that you treat as a black box: the database's storage engine, the garbage collector, the network stack. Write a problem that knowing it would have solved faster.
4. Find the last thing you learned that changed something in production. Write how long the learning took and what it paid back.

## Red flags

- **Cheap, and someone else pays.** Staging switched off, alerts removed, on-call stretched. Recover by choosing a saving that came from design, and say what stayed the same.
- **"I read a lot."** Reading is not a result. Recover by naming one problem where something you learned changed what you built.
- **Resentment about a no.** Recover by telling what the limit made you look at, and what you found.
- **A saving with no number.** Recover with the cost before and after, per month or per unit.

## Common questions

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="I have never owned a budget. How do I show Frugality?">
    Time and people are budgets too. A story where you delivered with two engineers in place of five, or reused an
    existing component in place of building one, fits. It also helps to learn what your service costs to run. Many
    engineers have never looked, and a first look often turns up a story.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Is asking for more resources a red flag?">
    No. Sometimes more capacity is the right answer. What is listened for is the order: you measured where the cost went
    and tried the cheaper options first, and your request came with that evidence.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Does a course or certificate count for Learn and Be Curious?">
    Only as the start of a story. Say what you did with it afterwards: the problem you applied it to and what changed.
    If nothing changed yet, pick a different example.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
