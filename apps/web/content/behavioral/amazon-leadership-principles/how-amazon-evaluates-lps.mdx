## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
Amazon does not test all 16 Leadership Principles in every round; each interviewer is assigned 2–3 specific principles, and the **Bar Raiser** probes whether you can resolve the natural tension when two principles pull in opposite directions.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

Most engineers prepare for an Amazon loop by treating the 16 Leadership Principles like vocabulary flashcards, memorizing one isolated story for *Bias for Action* and another for *Insist on the Highest Standards*.

Then they enter the fourth round with the **Bar Raiser**: an independent interviewer from outside the hiring org who holds veto power over the Hiring Manager. The candidate tells a story about shipping a service in four days (*Bias for Action*). Instead of applauding, the Bar Raiser asks: *"You shipped in four days without load testing: how did you make sure you weren't violating Insist on the Highest Standards and putting customer data at risk?"*

Ten minutes later, the candidate tells a story about spending three weeks refactoring a module for clean architecture (*Highest Standards*). The Bar Raiser immediately probes the opposite pole: *"Why wasn't that gold-plating when the customer needed a fix that week (Deliver Results)?"*

This is the secret to Amazon's behavioral loop: **principles are easy in isolation; seniority is proven when two Leadership Principles collide**.

## Worked engineering example (STAR+L)

<Tabs title="Balancing opposing LPs in a single story">
  <Tab label="One-Dimensional Story (Fails Tension Probe)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you took a calculated risk to move fast (Bias for Action)."*
    > "We needed to launch a new recommendations widget before Prime Day, so I skipped writing the shadow evaluation pipeline and deployed the model directly to production. It increased clicks by 4%."
    - **Why the Bar Raiser flags this:** This shows *recklessness*, not *Bias for Action*. There was no blast-radius cap or guardrail protecting *Customer Obsession* or *Highest Standards*.
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Tension-Aware Story (Clears L6 Bar)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you took a calculated risk to move fast (Bias for Action)."*
    > **Situation & Task:** "Two weeks before our Q4 peak freeze, we discovered our checkout address-autocomplete API was timing out for 6% of rural postal codes, costing ~\$18k/day in abandoned carts. A full index rebuild would take 4 weeks."
    > **Action:** "I classified this as a **two-way door** if we could isolate the blast radius. Instead of touching the primary Lucene index, I built a read-only prefix-tree fallback cache populated from the top 50,000 validated rural postal codes in 48 hours. To uphold *Highest Standards* while moving fast, I wrapped the fallback behind a circuit breaker that only triggered when primary <Term id="p99" /> exceeded 150 ms, and logged any address mismatch for asynchronous reconciliation."
    > **Result:** "We shipped in 3 days. Timeout rate dropped from 6% to 0.08%, recovering ~\$350k in Q4 GMV with zero bad shipping labels."
    > **Learning:** "I turned that circuit-breaker + static-fallback pattern into a reusable library now used by 4 checkout dependencies."
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Common red flags & how to recover

- **Trying to guess which LP the interviewer is asking and reciting the LP name:** You don't get extra points for saying *"I am very Customer Obsessed"* out loud. Show the behavior through concrete actions and metrics.
- **Repeating the same flagship story in Rounds 1, 2, 3, and 4:** Keep a tally on your scratchpad of which story you used with each interviewer.
