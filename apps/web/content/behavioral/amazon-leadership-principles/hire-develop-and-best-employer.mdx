## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  **Hire and Develop the Best** means you raise the level of the people around you, in who you help hire and in how you
  coach, until they can do the work without you. **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** means you make the team a safer
  and more productive place to work, and you keep the standard while you do it.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

A mid-level engineer joins a payments team from a frontend background. Her first three backend pull requests each come back with more than 30 comments, most about transactions and concurrency. Her output stalls and she stops asking questions.

One senior engineer on the team helps by taking over. He rewrites her changes over a weekend, and the release goes out on time. A month later she is exactly where she was.

A second senior engineer starts by finding out why she is stuck. She is writing 800 lines before anyone has agreed which rows are locked and what happens on a retry. He sets up four weeks of support that steps back as it goes. Before each task she fills in half a page: what locks, what retries, what can fail. They write the first one together. He comments on the second. He only reads the third.

Her review time falls from six days to under one. The next quarter she leads a reconciliation project alone, and the half-page note becomes a team habit.

Both engineers met the deadline. Only one of them changed what the team can do.

1. **Hire and Develop the Best.** Coaching that ends with the other person working alone.
2. **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer.** Something that was wearing the team down, measured and removed.
3. **Where they pull apart.** A high bar with no support burns people out. Support with no bar lets the work slide.
4. **A strong answer holds both.** The standard stays where it was, and people get the help they need to reach it.

### Hire and Develop the Best

In our words: raise the bar with every hire and promotion, recognise strong people and help them move to where they can do most, and take your part in coaching others seriously.

| A strong story shows                                                    | What weakens it                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| You found out why the person was stuck before you helped                | You did their work for them           |
| Support that steps back: together, then reviewed, then alone            | "I told them to ask me anything"      |
| Something the person did alone afterwards                               | The story ends with your own result   |
| A habit or a document that helps the next person too                    | Coaching that lives only in your head |
| You improved how your team interviews, for example with a scoring guide | "I have done a lot of interviews"     |

### Strive to be Earth's Best Employer

In our words: work to make the workplace safer, more productive and fairer. Lead with empathy, and care whether your colleagues are growing and ready for what comes next.

| A strong story shows                                                  | What weakens it                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| You measured what was wearing people down: pages, hours, waiting time | "Morale was low"                                  |
| You removed the cause and kept the standard                           | Lower targets, or unreliability that is tolerated |
| People can raise a mistake without fear, and you can say how you know | "We have a blameless culture"                     |
| People stayed, grew or were promoted                                  | No sign of what changed for them                  |

## A weak answer, then the fix

### Hire and Develop the Best

**Question:** _"Tell me about a time you helped someone who was struggling."_

<Tabs title="Hire and Develop the Best">
  <Tab label="Weak answer">
    *"We had an intern last summer and I was her mentor. She was a bit slow at first, so I gave her small bugs and answered her questions on chat. When her project fell behind, I finished the last part myself so that she had something to present. She got good feedback."*

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Fixed">
    - **Situation and task.** *"Last summer I mentored an intern on our mobile team. Her 12-week project was an offline mode for the notes screen. By week 4 she had merged nothing, and her updates said 'still exploring'."*
    - **Action.** *"I asked her to show me where she was. She had three half-built approaches and did not know how to choose between them. Her coding was fine. The gap was scoping. We split the project into four milestones, and before each one she wrote three lines: what is in, what is out, and how she would test it. I went through the first plan with her line by line, commented on the second and only read the third. When milestone 3 slipped, I did not write the code. We cut the handling of two edited copies and she wrote it up as future work."*
    - **Result.** *"She shipped offline reading and editing to 5 percent of users in week 11. Conflict handling was not done. She presented the cut and the reason herself, and she joined us full time the next year."*
    - **Learning.** *"The three-line plan is in our intern guide now. Two other mentors used it this summer."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

What changed:

- The weak answer blames her pace. The fixed one finds the real gap: scoping.
- The mentor did not take the keyboard. The scope was cut, and she owned the cut.
- The story ends with what she did, and with something the next mentor can use.

### Strive to be Earth's Best Employer

**Question:** _"Tell me about a time you made your team a better place to work."_

<Tabs title="Earth's Best Employer">
  <Tab label="Weak answer">
    *"Our team was burnt out from on-call. I organised a team lunch and told my manager that people were unhappy. I also covered a few extra shifts myself so that others could rest. Morale improved after that."*

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Fixed">
    - **Situation and task.** *"Our six-person infrastructure team was getting 19 pages a week outside working hours. Two senior engineers had told me they were looking at other teams."*
    - **Action.** *"I sorted 60 days of pages by cause and by whether a person had to act. 58 percent needed no action: a short CPU spike, or an upstream retry that fixed itself. 27 percent came from one old scheduled job with no retry. I took those numbers to our manager and product manager and asked for two weeks without feature work. I deleted or re-tuned the alerts that needed no action, added a retry with backoff to the job, and wrote one rule for new alerts: a page at night must say what to do."*
    - **Result.** *"Night pages fell from 19 a week to between 1 and 2. Both engineers stayed. One feature slipped by two weeks, which the product manager had agreed to in advance."*
    - **Learning.** *"In each weekly review we treat a night page that needed no action as a bug, to be fixed within two days."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

What changed:

- "Burnt out" became a count: 19 pages a week, sorted by cause.
- Covering shifts treats the symptom and makes one more tired person. Removing the alerts treats the cause.
- The cost is stated: two weeks of feature work.

## Build your own

1. Think of the best mentor you have had. Write the one habit of theirs that let you ask basic questions and still feel held to a high bar.
2. Think of the last question a newer teammate asked you. Write whether you gave the answer, or the answer and how you found it.
3. Count something that wears your team down: night pages, flaky builds, hours waiting for review. Write what one week of work would remove.
4. Find a person who can now do something alone that they first did with you. Write what you did in between.

## Red flags

- **Taking the keyboard.** "I rewrote it for them so we hit the date." Recover by telling a story where you cut scope or paired, and the person finished it.
- **A teammate described as lazy or weak.** Recover by naming the missing skill or the unclear expectation, and what you did about it.
- **Empathy as a lower bar.** "I told them not to worry about the tests." Recover by showing that the standard stayed and the support went up.
- **The hero on call.** Covering every bad shift yourself. Recover by showing how you removed the pages.

## Common questions

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="I have never managed anyone. Do these principles apply to me?">
    Yes. Amazon says its principles apply to every role. Engineers show these two by onboarding new teammates,
    interviewing, giving reviews that teach, mentoring interns and fixing what makes on-call painful.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Will I be asked about Strive to be Earth's Best Employer?">
    Some prep guides say engineers are rarely asked about it by name. That is not confirmed, and Amazon lists it with
    the other fifteen. A team health story also answers questions about ownership and standards, so it is worth
    preparing one.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="The person I coached did not improve. Can I use the story?">
    Yes, if you are honest about it. Say what you tried, how you knew it was not working, what you changed, and what you
    would do earlier next time. Do not blame the person.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
