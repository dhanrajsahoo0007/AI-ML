## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
**Hire and Develop the Best** tests whether you act as a force multiplier who coaches peers and raises the hiring bar through structured mechanisms, while **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** tests how you eliminate unsustainable on-call toil and build psychological safety without lowering performance standards.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

An L6 candidate is asked: *"Tell me about a time you helped a struggling engineer on your team."*
The candidate replies: *"A junior engineer on my team was falling behind on their sprint tasks before a deadline, so I took over the hardest tickets, rewrote their PRs over the weekend, and we hit the launch date."*

In the debrief room, the Bar Raiser marks a **No Hire** on *Hire and Develop the Best*. Taking the keyboard away from a struggling teammate hits a 1-week deadline at the cost of keeping that teammate dependent forever.
Compare that with a true **force multiplier**: they diagnose *why* the engineer is stuck (they didn't understand how distributed transactions worked in the legacy billing service), pair with them on the first PR (*I do / We do*), review their 1-page mini-design before they write code on the second PR (*You do with safety net*), and watch them lead the third feature independently two months later.

## What interviewers are really testing

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/behavioral/leveling-ladder" stepper caption="From L5 to L6+, your value is measured not just by the code you write, but by how many L3/L4 engineers you elevate to the next level." />

| Principle | What Bar Raisers probe | Red flag to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hire and Develop the Best** | Diagnosing root causes of a peer's struggle; scaffolded mentorship (*Shadow → Reverse-Shadow → Solo*); calibrating interview loops with objective rubrics | Doing the work *for* the mentee; giving vague encouragement instead of actionable code/design coaching |
| **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** | Auditing on-call burnout, alert fatigue, and developer friction; creating safe, blameless post-mortem environments where junior engineers speak up | Confusing "empathy" with lowering the engineering bar or tolerating chronic unreliability |

## Worked engineering example (STAR+L)

<Tabs title="Coaching a Peer vs. Fixing On-Call Burnout">
  <Tab label="Hire & Develop the Best (Scaffolded Coaching)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you mentored someone and how you measured their growth."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"A mid-level engineer who joined our payments team from a frontend background had their first three backend PRs sent back with 30+ comments each for concurrency and transaction bugs. Their confidence was tanking and velocity had stalled."*
    - **Action:** *"In our 1:1, I realized the problem wasn't coding ability—they were writing 800-line PRs before aligning on database locking invariants. I set up a 4-week scaffolded plan: before writing code on any ticket, we spent 15 minutes reviewing a half-page 'State & Concurrency Box' together (which rows lock, what happens on retry). On the first ticket, we paired on the integration test; on the second, I reviewed only the half-page doc; by the third, they ran the design check themselves."*
    - **Result:** *"Their PR review turnaround dropped from 6 days to under 24 hours, they led our Q3 ledger reconciliation service solo with zero production bugs, and they were promoted to Senior SDE 10 months later."*
    - **Learning:** *"We adopted the half-page 'State & Concurrency Check' for all L4/L5 engineers before starting multi-table mutations."*
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Earth's Best Employer (Eliminating On-Call Burnout)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you improved your team's work environment, morale, or operational sustainability."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"Our 6-person infrastructure squad was experiencing severe burnout—averaging 19 off-hours PagerDuty wake-ups per week—and two senior engineers were considering transferring."*
    - **Action:** *"I categorized 60 days of pages by root cause and actionability. I found 58% of wake-ups required zero human action (transient 30-second CPU spikes or upstream retries that self-healed), and 27% came from a single legacy cron job with no automatic retry. I presented the data to our PM and EM, won a 2-week 'Page Zero' engineering tax, deleted 14 non-actionable threshold alerts in favor of 5-minute <Term id="slo" /> burn-rate windows, and added idempotent auto-remediation to the cron worker."*
    - **Result:** *"Off-hours pages dropped from 19/week to 1.2/week within one sprint, sprint velocity increased by 22% the following quarter because on-call engineers weren't sleep-deprived, and zero engineers left the team."*
    - **Learning:** *"We instituted a rule at every weekly ops review: any alert that pages a human at night without requiring a human action is treated as a bug and tuned or deleted within 48 hours."*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Common red flags & how to recover

- **Throwing a mentee under the bus:** Never describe a teammate you mentored as "incompetent" or "lazy." Diagnose the *systemic or skill gap* with empathy.
