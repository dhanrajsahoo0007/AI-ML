## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** means you challenge a decision you think is wrong, with evidence, and once the
  decision is made you back it fully. **Earn Trust** means you listen, speak plainly, and say your own mistakes out loud
  before anyone else has to.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

A checkout team is three weeks from a partner launch. The principal engineer decides to keep session state in the old cache cluster. Moving to the new one would cost the three weeks.

A backend engineer on the team disagrees. The old cluster does not replicate, and her numbers show that losing one node drops 8 percent of active carts. She writes this on one page with a second option, a four-day bridge that writes to both clusters, and sends it before the review.

In the review the principal engineer and the director weigh the partner's contract penalty against the risk. They keep the old cluster. She says she still sees the risk and that she will build it their way.

Then she does the part that matters. She tells the team the decision as "ours" and gives the reason. She spends two days adding a signed cookie that carries the cart id, so a lost node does not lose the cart. In week two a node fails, and 99.7 percent of checkouts survive. The next quarter, the principal engineer asks her to lead the migration.

1. **Have Backbone.** She said it to the person who owned the decision, before the decision, with a number and an option.
2. **The decision goes against her.** She can state the reason fairly: the partner date outweighed the risk.
3. **Commit.** She explained the decision to her team as theirs, and did work that made the chosen plan safer.
4. **The risk arrives.** Her guard is what held the decision up.
5. **Earn Trust.** The trust came from step 3, more than from being right in step 1.

The two principles need each other. Backbone without trust makes you the person nobody wants in the room. Trust without backbone makes you agreeable while the team walks into a mistake you saw.

### Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit

In our words: say so when you disagree, with respect, even when it is uncomfortable or tiring. Do not give way just to keep the peace. When the decision is made, commit to it completely.

| A strong story shows                                                 | What weakens it                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| You told the decision owner, before the decision, with evidence      | Silence in the meeting and complaints afterwards |
| You can state the other side's reason as they would                  | The other person "did not understand"            |
| After the decision, you did work that helped the chosen plan succeed | Slow work, or "management made us do this"       |
| At least one story where you lost                                    | Only stories where you won                       |

### Earn Trust

In our words: listen carefully, speak candidly and treat people with respect. Be openly critical of your own work, even when that is awkward.

| A strong story shows                                             | What weakens it                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| You named your own mistake first, with the times and the cause   | The mistake came out when a customer noticed |
| The disagreement stayed on the system and the trade              | Blame, or remarks about a person             |
| You gave the affected team something: control, a check or credit | An apology and nothing else                  |
| A real mistake with a real cost                                  | "My weakness is that I care too much"        |

The same story, committed:

- **Situation and task.** _"Our fraud model needed new features. My manager decided to compute them in a nightly batch. I believed we needed streaming, because 30 percent of fraud on new accounts happened within an hour of signup."_
- **Action.** _"I put that number in a one-page note with two options and their costs, and sent it to him before the design review. In the review he chose batch. The team had six weeks and nobody on it had run a streaming system in production. Those were fair reasons. I said I still saw the risk and that I would build the batch design. Then I asked what could cover the first hour inside his plan, and added an hourly run for accounts less than a day old. When a teammate called the decision a mistake, I gave the reasons for it as ours."_
- **Result.** _"We shipped in six weeks. The hourly run caught about half of the first-hour fraud that a nightly job would have missed. The other half got through, and it cost about \$40,000 in the first quarter."_
- **Learning.** _"I took those numbers to planning without the words 'as I said'. Streaming for new accounts went onto the next quarter's plan, and he asked me to lead it."_

### Earn Trust

**Question:** _"Tell me about a time you lost someone's trust. How did you earn it back?"_

<Tabs title="Earn Trust">
  <Tab label="Weak answer">
    *"During a gateway migration there was a brief issue with logins on iOS. It came from a default in the proxy configuration that the documentation did not explain well, and the review missed it too. We rolled it back and things were fine. I think the mobile team understood."*

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Fixed">
    - **Situation and task.** *"I was the SRE leading a gateway migration. I had told the mobile lead that our new rate limit rules would only observe traffic. One rule I had copied had enforcement switched on for a login route. It rejected 4 percent of iOS login retries for 22 minutes."*
    - **Action.** *"I rolled the rule back within three minutes of seeing the spike. Then I wrote in the shared channel that my configuration error had caused the rejected logins between 14:10 and 14:32, and that it was rolled back. I did not mention the documentation or the review. That afternoon I showed the mobile lead a validator I had written, which rejects any observe-only rule that has enforcement on. I also gave her team a switch that turns our rules off without asking us."*
    - **Result.** *"We moved the other 12 routes over two weeks with no incidents. She asked for me as the backend partner on her next launch."*
    - **Learning.** *"I tell the affected team myself, in the first five minutes, with the times and the cause."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

What changed:

- The weak answer says "there was an issue". The fixed one says "my configuration error", with the minutes.
- The documentation and the review are left out. They may be true, and they sound like excuses.
- The other team got control: a switch they can use without permission.

## Build your own

1. Think of the last time a decision went against you. Write what you said to your teammates about it the next day.
2. Write the other person's reason in one sentence that they would sign.
3. Think of your last mistake at work. Count the minutes between finding it and telling the people it affected.
4. Find a time you stayed quiet and later wished you had spoken. Write the one-page note you would send now.

## Red flags

- **"Just as I said it would."** Any hint of waiting for the plan to fail. Recover by telling what you did to make it work, and leave the forecast out.
- **A mistake that is a boast.** "I work too hard." Recover with a real technical or communication mistake, its cost, and the check you added.
- **Silent in the room.** Disagreement that only appears in private messages. Recover with a story where you raised it with the decision owner, in a meeting or in writing.
- **The other side has no reason.** Recover by giving their strongest argument before your own.

## Common questions

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="Should I commit to a decision I think is unsafe or unethical?">
    No. Our advice is to keep these stories apart. Disagree and Commit fits trade-offs: a design, a scope, a priority. A
    security hole, a privacy breach or something illegal is a different kind of problem, and the right action there is
    to raise it through security or legal until it is fixed.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="I am quiet in meetings. How do I show backbone?">
    Backbone does not need volume. A one-page note or a benchmark sent before the meeting often does more than a debate
    in it. A story where your written evidence put a risk in front of the decision owner is a full answer.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Do I need a story where I lost the argument?">
    Prepare one. A question can ask directly about a decision that went ahead against your view, and a story where you
    won cannot answer it.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
