## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  Behavioral preparation is the same four jobs at any timescale: **choose your stories, write them, say them out loud,
  and fit them to the company**. The time you have decides how many stories and how many rounds of practice.
</KeyIdea>

## Which plan

| Time before the loop | Plan        | Stories ready | Practice out loud               |
| -------------------- | ----------- | ------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Under a week         | **3 days**  | 4 or 5        | 2 mock loops                    |
| One to three weeks   | **2 weeks** | 8             | 4 mock loops, one with a person |
| A month or more      | **6 weeks** | 8 to 10       | Weekly loops, two with a person |

About 30 to 45 minutes a day is enough for the two-week and six-week plans. If you are also preparing coding and system design, do the behavioral work on the days you are tired of the other two: it uses a different kind of attention.

Your ticks are saved in your browser, so you can come back to the page each day.

## The 6-week plan

For a planned search with several companies. It adds what the shorter plans skip: a second story per topic, feedback from other people, and a version of each story for each company.

- **Weeks 1 and 2.** Do the first week of the two-week plan at half speed. Write ten stories, not eight, so you can drop the two weakest.
- **Week 3.** One mock loop every two days. After each one, rewrite the story you rated lowest.
- **Week 4.** Two mock interviews with people. Ask each for one thing to stop doing and one to keep.
- **Week 5.** For each target company, reread its page and decide which story you would lead with for each thing it scores. Amazon needs the most work here, because each interviewer covers different principles.
- **Week 6.** One loop every other day, then nothing on the last day.

Use the two-week checklist above for weeks 1 to 4 and tick it more slowly.

## What not to do

- **Do not memorise scripts.** Memorised answers break on the first follow-up question. Know the facts of each story and the order you tell them in.
- **Do not prepare twenty stories.** You will not remember them under pressure. Eight that you know well cover almost every question.
- **Do not skip saying them out loud.** A story that reads well can take four minutes to say. The mock loop timer shows you this in private.
- **Do not prepare only successes.** Failure and conflict questions come up in nearly every loop, and they are the ones people have no story for.
