## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  A timer shows you how long you talk. Only another person can **interrupt you, look unconvinced and ask the follow-up
  you did not prepare for**, so do at least one mock with a person before a loop that matters.
</KeyIdea>

## Who to ask

In order of usefulness:

1. **Someone who interviews** at your target company or one like it. They know what a real follow-up sounds like.
2. **An engineer one level above you.** They can hear whether a story is sized for the level you want.
3. **Any engineer.** They can still hear a rambling setup, a missing result and "we" where "I" belongs.
4. **A friend outside engineering.** Good for one thing: if they cannot follow the story, it is not clear enough.

Offer to swap. Being the interviewer for someone else is the fastest way to learn what a weak answer sounds like.

## The 45 minutes

| Minutes  | What happens                                                                |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0 to 3   | The interviewer introduces the round. You give your 90-second introduction. |
| 3 to 35  | Five questions. Each one: your answer, then two or three follow-ups.        |
| 35 to 40 | You ask your questions, as you would in the real round.                     |
| 40 to 45 | Feedback. The interviewer reads their notes for each answer.                |

Do not stop between questions to discuss an answer. Feedback comes at the end, as it would in a written debrief.

## The script for your interviewer

Send this section to the person helping you.

- **Pick five questions** from [Interview Practice](/interviews), or run the [Mock Loop](/behavioral/practice-lab/mock-loop) on a second screen and read its questions out. Do not show the candidate the list.
- **Let the first answer run for two minutes.** If it passes three minutes, interrupt: "Let me stop you there. What did you do next?" Real interviewers do this.
- **Ask two or three follow-ups on every answer.** Choose from these:
  - "What was your part in that, exactly?"
  - "Why that option and not the other one?"
  - "How did you measure that?"
  - "What did the other person think of how it went?"
  - "What would you do differently now?"
  - "What happened after you left the project?"
- **Push once on a vague answer.** If you hear "we improved it a lot", ask "by how much?" and write down whether a number came back.
- **Take notes on what was said**, not on how you felt about it. "Said 'we' in every action" is useful. "Seemed unsure" is not.

## The scoring rubric

Score each answer from 1 to 5. It is the same scale the practice tools on this site use, so your scores can be compared.

| Score | Name           | What the interviewer heard                                                                  |
| ----- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **Blanked**    | No story, or a general statement about how you usually work.                                |
| **2** | **Rambled**    | A real story, but a long setup, an unclear action, or it answered a different question.     |
| **3** | **Covered it** | Clear actions in the first person. No number in the result, or the follow-ups went nowhere. |
| **4** | **Strong**     | Clear actions, a measured result, and specific answers to the follow-ups.                   |
| **5** | **Ready**      | All of that inside two minutes, with a learning that changed how you work.                  |

The interviewer also writes down three things for each answer:

- **Whose actions were they?** Count "I" and "we" in the action part.
- **Was there a number?** Write the number down. If you cannot, there was none.
- **What level did it sound like?** One person on a defined task, one person who found and fixed the problem, or someone who changed how a team or several teams work.

## Reading the feedback

- **A low score on one story** means that story needs work. Rewrite it.
- **The same note on several stories** ("long setup", "no number", "we") is a habit. Fix the habit and all your stories improve. Most people have one such habit, and they cannot hear it themselves.
- **"I could not follow the technical part"** from an engineer means the story has too much detail for a first answer. Move the detail into the follow-ups.
- **A level lower than you are aiming for** is about the stories you chose, not how you told them. See [Junior-to-Senior Leveling](/behavioral/leveling-and-tracks/junior-to-senior-leveling) and choose stories in which other people changed what they did because of your work.

## If you cannot find anyone

Record yourself answering three questions on your phone and listen the next day. You will hear the long setup and the filler words at once. It does not replace follow-up questions, so add them yourself: after each answer, ask the six follow-ups from the script above and answer them out loud.
