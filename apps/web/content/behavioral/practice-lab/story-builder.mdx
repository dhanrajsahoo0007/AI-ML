## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  Write each story once, in the four parts an interviewer listens for, and let the page tell you whether it fits in
  **two minutes** and whether **most of it is what you did**.
</KeyIdea>

## How to use it

1. Press **New story** and give it a short name you will recognise.
2. Fill the four boxes. Write the way you would speak. Full sentences are fine; so are notes.
3. Watch the two bars under the boxes. **Yours** shows how your words are split. **Target** shows the split that works for a first answer: about 15% setup, 60% action, 15% result and 10% learning.
4. Read the list under **What a listener would notice** and fix what it flags.
5. Tick the kinds of question the story can answer. The [Story Matrix](/behavioral/practice-lab/story-matrix) uses those ticks to show your gaps.

## What the checks mean

| Check                | Why it matters                                                                                                 |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Speaking time**    | A first answer of about two minutes leaves room for follow-up questions. The estimate uses 140 words a minute. |
| **Share of action**  | Interviewers grade what you did. A story that is mostly setup gives them little to write down.                 |
| **"I" against "we"** | "We" hides your part. Say what you did, and name what other people did.                                        |
| **A number**         | A result with a number can be compared and checked. "It went well" cannot.                                     |
| **Learning**         | The last part shows whether you changed how you work. It is the part most candidates leave out.                |

The checks count words. They cannot tell whether a story is true, relevant or well chosen, so read them as a first pass and practise the story out loud afterwards.

## Where your stories are kept

Your stories are saved in this browser as you type. Clearing your browser's site data removes them, so keep a copy of anything you cannot rewrite.
