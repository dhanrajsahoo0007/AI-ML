## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  At all three companies the behavioral round is described as **about one hour with a hiring manager or another
  leader**, and it mixes your stories with **technical questions about your past work**. Only Databricks publishes that
  in detail. Snowflake publishes values and no loop, and Datadog publishes its stages and no values.
</KeyIdea>

### Databricks

Databricks publishes an engineering interview guide (April 2025). It lists the stages in order: a **recruiter screen** of 30 minutes, a **technical screen** of one hour, team matching, a **panel of four to six interviews** of one hour each, a hiring committee, references, and the offer.

The behavioral round is called the **Cross-Functional or Hiring Manager interview**. An engineering leader runs it, and that person may be your direct manager. The guide lists what it covers: your career path, why you are looking, why Databricks, significant projects and how they tied to company goals, problem solving and decision making, working across teams, overcoming challenges, and leadership for your level. It says to expect technical depth when you explain a project.

### Snowflake

We could not open any Snowflake page that describes the loop, so everything here is reported. interviewing.io describes a 30-minute recruiter call, an optional 30-minute hiring-manager screen about fit with the role, the team and the values, a two-hour technical phone screen, and an onsite of about four hours. The onsite is reported to open with a **30-minute presentation of a past project** to several engineers, followed by coding, a team-specific expertise round and a **behavioral round** of one hour each, and a closing conversation with a recruiter or a senior leader.

One source says the behavioral round is usually run by a project manager. That is not confirmed.

### Datadog

Datadog's candidate experience page lists the stages and no lengths: a first screen with a recruiter, the hiring manager or both; interviews with the recruiter, the hiring manager or team members, with **one step expected in person**; a take-home project for some roles; and an executive interview for some roles. It names no behavioral or values round.

What is reported: interviewing.io, writing from conversations with Datadog engineers, describes a 30-minute recruiter call, a one-hour technical phone screen, and an onsite of coding, system design and behavioral rounds of one hour each. It says a hiring manager or director runs the behavioral round and mixes standard behavioral questions with technical questions on your past work. Little else is reported for Datadog.

## What they score

Only Databricks publishes the topics of its round. The table is ours. It starts from those topics, which also fit the prompts commonly reported for Snowflake and Datadog.

| Topic                         | A strong story shows                                                   | What weakens it                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Why you are moving**        | One honest reason, and a link from your past work to this team         | Complaints about your employer, or no reason      |
| **A significant project**     | The company goal it served, your design choices and what you measured  | A tour of the architecture with no decision in it |
| **Decision making**           | The options you had, and the data that settled it or changed your mind | "It was obvious"                                  |
| **Working across teams**      | What the other team needed, and what you gave up to get agreement      | The other team was "slow"                         |
| **Overcoming a challenge**    | A mistake or setback that was yours, the fix, and the check you added  | A failure with no cost, or one caused by others   |
| **Leadership for your level** | People who changed what they did because of you                        | A title, with no example                          |

## How the decision is made

- **Databricks** publishes the order: a **hiring committee** reviews the panel's feedback, then **references** are taken, then the offer. interviewing.io adds that a VP of Engineering has the final say and that references are usually one manager and two senior colleagues. Neither detail is in the official guide.
- **Datadog** says on its candidate page that the **interviewing team meets to choose** the candidate. interviewing.io describes a hiring committee followed by team matching. The two accounts differ. Rely on the official page.
- **Snowflake** publishes nothing on this that we could open. interviewing.io reports that the interviewers meet after the onsite and decide within a few days, and that some candidates went through team matching.

## The level bar

None of the three publishes a level rubric.

- **Databricks:** the guide says leadership is judged relative to your career level. It gives no level names.
- **Snowflake:** interviewing.io reports the names IC3 and IC4 for mid to senior and IC5 and above for principal, and an extra cross-functional round for principal candidates.
- **Datadog:** interviewing.io reports a presentation in place of one coding round at staff level and above.

## The published values

### Databricks

The Databricks culture page lists six principles. The interview guide does not say the behavioral round is scored against them, so treat the table as a check on your stories. "Ownership" appears in prep lists and is not one of the six.

| Principle                            | Where it could show in a story                                  |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **We are customer obsessed**         | A trade-off you made because of what a customer needed          |
| **We raise the bar**                 | A quality standard you set, and what it cost to meet            |
| **We are truth seeking**             | You changed your position when the data went against you        |
| **We operate from first principles** | You dropped the usual approach after working out the real limit |
| **We bias for action**               | You shipped a small version in days to learn something          |
| **We put the company first**         | You gave up a team goal for a company one                       |

### Snowflake

Snowflake's Global Code of Conduct lists eight values: **Put Customers First**, **Integrity Always**, **Think Big**, **Be Excellent**, **Get It Done**, **Own It**, **Make Each Other The Best** and **Embrace Each Other's Differences**. Some third-party lists give seven. No source we found ties interview questions to the eight. Use them as a check that your stories cover customers, ownership and how you treat colleagues.

### Datadog

Datadog publishes no list of values that we could find. Its careers site describes its people as solving tough problems, innovating pragmatically and growing together. Do not prepare against a values list from a prep site.

## Worked answers

Both prompts are commonly reported and not confirmed by a first-hand account.

<Tabs title="Two answers for a hiring-manager round">
  <Tab label="Changing your mind after new data">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about changing your mind after new data."* Reported for Databricks.

    - **Situation and task.** *"I am a data engineer. We were laying out a 2 TB events table, and for a week I argued in design review that we should partition it by day. A teammate thought most queries filtered by customer."*
    - **Action.** *"I stopped arguing and pulled 30 days of real query logs. 71 percent of queries filtered by customer ID with no date range at all. I told the review I had been wrong, and we clustered by customer first and date second."*
    - **Result.** *"The median query went from 14 seconds to 5. Storage cost went up 8 percent, which we accepted."*
    - **Learning.** *"I now sample the real query log before I propose a layout. My first position came from how I query the table, and I am one user."*

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="A regression your own code caused">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about a production regression caused by your own code."* Reported for Datadog.

    - **Situation and task.** *"I work on reliability for a metrics collection agent. My change to how it batches data raised memory use by 180 MB per host. I had tested on hosts with few containers."*
    - **Action.** *"The change was on 5 percent of hosts when another team's alert fired, three hours in. I rolled back in 12 minutes, then posted what I knew in their channel before I had the full cause."*
    - **Result.** *"Most hosts recovered with no loss. Two were killed for running out of memory and lost about four minutes of metrics, which could not be recovered. I told the owners of those two hosts which window was missing."*
    - **Learning.** *"Our canary checked error rate and not memory. I added a memory limit to the canary check and a test host with 300 containers."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Red flags

- **A project tour with no decision.** All three rounds are reported to go technical. Recover by picking one design choice, the option you rejected and the number that decided it.
- **No reason for the job search.** Databricks lists this as a topic. Recover with one honest sentence about what you want next, and nothing negative about your employer.
- **Leaving references to the last day.** At Databricks, references are a published step before the offer. Recover by asking a former manager early.

## Common questions about this round

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="Will the behavioral round include technical questions?">
    Plan for it. Databricks' guide says to expect technical depth when you explain a project. For Datadog, reports
    describe the interviewer asking you to sketch a system you built and explain your design choices.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Is the Snowflake project presentation part of the behavioral round?">
    It is reported as a separate 30-minute round in front of several engineers. Prepare one project for both: a
    30-minute version for the presentation and a two-minute version for a story.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Does Datadog have values I should learn?">
    It publishes no list that we could find. Read the careers site, and spend the time on your project stories.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
