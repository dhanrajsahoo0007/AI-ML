## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  Microsoft's careers pages describe **two to four conversations of up to an hour each**, and say the process looks at
  **growth mindset, diverse and inclusive, One Microsoft and customer obsession**. Microsoft recommends answering in
  **STAR(R)**, where the last R is **reflection**, so every story needs a sentence on what you would do differently.
</KeyIdea>

What Microsoft says about its own process (see Sources):

- **An optional short screening conversation** comes first.
- Most interviews are then **two to four conversations of up to an hour each**, by phone, on Teams or in person. You meet potential teammates and cross-functional colleagues, and the hiring manager is one of them.
- Questions are of two kinds: **behavioral** (past examples, such as applying feedback, working with others or adapting your approach) and **role-related scenarios**. Some roles add coding, a portfolio or work samples.
- Students get an initial interview, then a final virtual interview day of one-to-one meetings.

What candidates commonly report for engineering roles (not confirmed by Microsoft):

- A recruiter call, a phone screen or online assessment, then a loop of four to five rounds of 45 to 60 minutes: two or three coding rounds, one system design round, and sometimes a domain round.
- **Behavioral questions are often folded into each technical round.** The hiring manager round mixes a deep dive on one project with behavioral questions.
- Many candidates still call the last round the **"as appropriate" or AA interview**: 30 to 60 minutes with a senior manager or leader, reported to focus on growth mindset and adaptability, sometimes with technical questions on weak spots from earlier rounds.

## What they score

Microsoft publishes no scoring rubric and no list of "core competencies". The lists of six or seven competencies on prep sites disagree with each other and appear on no official page. What the careers pages do name is the six areas below. The signals are our reading.

| Area Microsoft names      | A strong story shows                                                                       | What weakens it                                             |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Growth mindset**        | An assumption of yours that was wrong, the evidence, and what you do differently           | No failures, or failures caused by other teams              |
| **Diverse and inclusive** | A habit that brings other people's views in, such as written comments before a meeting     | "I treat everyone the same" with no example                 |
| **One Microsoft**         | You built on another team's work, or changed their code with them, when a fork was quicker | "It was faster to build our own than to talk to them"       |
| **Customer obsession**    | You saw the customer's problem first-hand and it changed the design                        | Only internal metrics, with no customer in the story        |
| **Values**                | Respect, integrity and accountability shown in an action, such as owning a missed date     | Blame, or a fix that was quietly shipped and never reported |
| **Manager expectations**  | For manager roles: how you model, coach and care, with one person as the example           | General statements about your "leadership style"            |

## How the decision is made

Microsoft's careers pages do not say who decides. Three sources fill in part of the picture:

1. **The final interview.** A 2023 post on Microsoft's developer blog explains the term "as appropriate". It was the last interview of the day, with a more senior person in the hiring group, and it was first held only when the earlier feedback made it worthwhile. That person reviewed the other interviewers' recommendations and could end the day early. Later the role rotated among interviewers and the final interview became mandatory. The post says the term is no longer official.
2. **Feedback.** interviewing.io, citing Microsoft interviewers, says practice varies by organisation: written feedback, a live debrief, or both, with rubrics in some groups only.
3. **One report.** A 2024 account on Blind describes the final interviewer moving a candidate for a manager role to a weaker rating and proposing a non-manager role.

Several common claims have no solid source: that the final interviewer has a veto, that they set your level, that they are always above a certain rank, or that the round happens only if the earlier rounds went well. The last one contradicts the blog post. Prepare for the final interview as a full round whatever your day has been like.

## The level bar

Microsoft does not publish its level numbers. A salary site lists software engineer levels commonly written as 59 to 70, with 59 as the entry level and 63 as senior. Treat the other number bands you see as third-party.

## Microsoft's culture and values in the interview

Microsoft's culture page and corporate values page list the following. Expect them to be heard in your stories more often than asked about by name.

| Published                                            | Where it shows in a story                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Growth mindset**                                   | The reflection at the end: what you learned and what you changed    |
| **Customer obsessed**                                | The reason you chose the problem                                    |
| **Diverse and inclusive**                            | Who you asked, and whose view changed the plan                      |
| **One Microsoft**                                    | The other team in the story, and what you left behind for them      |
| **Respect, integrity, accountability**               | How you describe colleagues, and how you describe your own mistakes |
| **Create clarity, generate energy, deliver success** | For leads: how you set direction when the goal was unclear          |
| **Model, coach, care**                               | For managers: one person you developed, and how                     |

## The fifth step: reflection

Microsoft's interview tips add an R to STAR: situation, task, action, result, **reflection**. A reflection is one or two sentences on what you would do differently and what you have done differently since. "I learned communication is important" is not a reflection. "I now send the design to the dependent team before I start building, and I did that on my next two projects" is one.

## Worked answers

<Tabs title="Two STAR(R) answers">
  <Tab label="A team that could not help you">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about working across teams, including a clash over dependencies or scope."*

    - **Situation and task.** *"My backend team needed per-customer rate limits in the shared API gateway. The gateway team, in another time zone, had a full roadmap for two quarters. Two engineers on my team wanted to fork the gateway."*
    - **Action.** *"I asked the gateway team's lead for 30 minutes to understand what was blocking them. They wanted a plugin hook too, but had nobody to write the compatibility tests. I offered to do the work in their repository under their review. I wrote a two-page design in their format, joined their channel for two sprints, and built the hook and the tests."*
    - **Result.** *"We shipped our rate limits three weeks later than a fork would have allowed. There is no fork to maintain, and two other teams used the hook within the quarter."*
    - **Reflection.** *"I underestimated their review time by half, and I told my manager late. Next time I would agree the review schedule before I commit to a date."*

    **Why it works:** the other team's constraint is explained fairly, the delay is stated, and the reflection is specific.

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="A technical disagreement">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about a technical disagreement and how you handled it."*

    - **Situation and task.** *"I am a frontend engineer. A colleague and I disagreed about our new settings page: I wanted to render it on the server, and he wanted a client-side app so we could reuse his form components."*
    - **Action.** *"We had argued twice in review comments and got nowhere. I suggested we each write down the one number that would change our mind. Mine was his bundle size on a mid-range phone. His was how many of his components would need rewriting. We spent a day measuring both. His approach loaded in 2.9 seconds against my 1.4, and 9 of his 14 components would have needed changes for mine."*
    - **Result.** *"We rendered the page shell on the server and kept his components for the forms. Load time came out at 1.8 seconds, slower than my design and much better than his. We both signed the decision note."*
    - **Reflection.** *"I had treated it as a contest for two weeks. I now ask the 'what number would change your mind' question in the first conversation."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Red flags

- **No real failure.** "I cannot think of one" or a weakness that is a boast. Recover by naming a wrong assumption, however small, and what it cost.
- **Criticising an old system or a partner team.** Much Microsoft work is on long-lived products with customer commitments. Say why the old design made sense when it was built.
- **Building your own by default.** A story that starts with "we wrote our own framework" invites the question of what you looked at first. Say what you evaluated.
- **Coasting in the last round.** Candidates report technical questions in the final interview on topics that went badly earlier. If you ran out of time on something, bring it up yourself and finish it.

## Common questions about this round

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="What does an 'as appropriate' interview on my schedule mean?">
    It is the candidates' name for the final interview with a more senior person. Microsoft's own developer blog says
    the interview used to be held only when earlier feedback justified it, later became mandatory, and that the term is
    no longer official. Do not read it as a sign that you have passed.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Is there a separate behavioral round?">
    Often not. Candidates report behavioral questions inside technical rounds and in the hiring manager round. Have a
    two-minute version of each story ready, because you may get ten minutes for it and not forty-five.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Should I study the six core competencies I see online?">
    No official Microsoft page lists them, and the lists disagree. Prepare for the areas Microsoft does name: growth
    mindset, diverse and inclusive, One Microsoft and customer obsession.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
