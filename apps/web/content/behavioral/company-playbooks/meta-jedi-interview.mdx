## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
Meta's 45-minute **Jedi** behavioral round is the primary **leveling gate** between E4, E5, and E6: it grades whether you find your own high-impact problems, resolve interpersonal conflict directly without escalating to managers, and ship fast through ambiguity.
</KeyIdea>

## The story

At Meta, passing the two coding rounds and the system design round is not enough to secure a Senior (E5) or Staff (E6) offer. In post-loop calibration, the committee looks closely at the **Jedi** (behavioral) scorecard.

Suppose an E5 candidate tells three polished stories where their Engineering Manager handed them a well-defined project spec, they wrote clean C++/Python code, and they escalated a cross-team disagreement to their manager to resolve. Even if their system design round was strong, the calibration committee will down-level the offer to **E4**. Why? Because at Meta, **E5 is the terminal "self-directed" level**: an E5 engineer is expected to discover problems, align peers across teams directly, and drive impact without waiting for manager air-cover.

## What interviewers are really testing

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/behavioral/faang-loop-comparison" stepper caption="Where the Jedi round sits in the Meta loop compared to Amazon, Google, and Frontier AI labs." />

Every Meta Jedi interviewer grades your stories against **five core signal pillars** mapped to Meta's core values (*Move Fast*, *Focus on Long-Term Impact*, *Build Awesome Things*, *Live in the Future*, *Be Direct and Respect Your Colleagues*, *Meta, Metamates, Me*):

| Jedi Pillar | What E5 (Senior) / E6 (Staff) looks like | What causes a down-level to E4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Resolving Conflict** | Addressing technical or cross-functional tension 1:1 with data and empathy; building consensus | Escalating every disagreement to a manager or avoiding friction |
| **2. Growing Continuously** | Soliciting critical feedback proactively; admitting a real blind spot and changing a habit | Giving a fake weakness (*"I work too hard"*) or blaming others for failures |
| **3. Embracing Ambiguity** | Turning a vague metric drop or unowned pain point into a scoped roadmap and shipped win | Waiting for a PM or TL to write detailed tickets |
| **4. Driving Results** | Moving fast with prototypes/canaries to prove impact; cutting low-leverage work | Spending months building infrastructure with no measurable user/system outcome |
| **5. Communicating Effectively** | Crisp, structured answers; translating deep technical trade-offs for product/design peers | Rambling 4-minute monologues with no clear "I" actions |

## Worked engineering example (STAR+L)

<Tabs title="Meta Jedi Core Prompts: E4 vs. E5/E6 Framing">
  <Tab label="Be Direct & Respect Your Colleagues (Feedback)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you had to give difficult feedback to a teammate."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"On our Ads ranking squad, a senior peer was merging hotfix PRs directly to production branches during experiments to move faster, which broke our offline-online feature logging parity twice in one month."*
    - **Action:** *"Instead of complaining to our EM, I scheduled a private 15-minute 1:1 the same day. I started by acknowledging why they were bypassing the staging queue—our CI pipeline took 55 minutes and blocked urgent experiment tweaks (*Respect Your Colleagues*). Then I showed the exact data impact: two weeks of corrupted training labels that invalidated three teammate experiments (*Be Direct*). Together, we built a 4-minute 'fast-lane' shadow-validation workflow for config-only experiment changes so nobody needed to bypass CI."*
    - **Result:** *" logging parity incidents dropped to zero, experiment iteration speed actually improved by 10x (55m to 4m), and our working relationship grew stronger."*
    - **Learning:** *"When a smart engineer bypasses a process, fix the slow process while holding the line on the data invariant."*
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Move Fast & Focus on Impact (Bottom-Up Initiative)">
    **Prompt:** *"Tell me about a time you identified an opportunity nobody asked you to work on and drove it to impact."*

    - **Situation & Task:** *"While debugging a feed latency spike, I noticed our service was fetching full 40-field user profile blobs on every request even though 92% of feed rankings only used 4 fields."*
    - **Action:** *"Nobody owned the cross-team payload contract. I spent one afternoon building a lightweight projection prototype on 1% shadow traffic to prove the bandwidth savings. Armed with real numbers, I wrote a 1-page RFC, built an auto-generated GraphQL/Thrift field-mask codemod for the 3 calling teams, and migrated the hot path over two weeks."*
    - **Result:** *"Cut network egress between Profile and Feed by 78% (14 Gbps saved at peak), shaved 19 ms off feed <Term id="p99" />, and freed \$220k/yr in memcached RAM."*
    - **Learning:** *"In a bottom-up engineering culture, a 1-day shadow benchmark persuades partner teams 10x faster than a slide deck."*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Common red flags & how to recover

- **Waiting for permission:** Saying *"I waited for my manager to tell me which option to pick"* is the #1 reason candidates get capped at E4.
- **Building in a silo:** Meta is intensely collaborative across Product, Data Science, and Engineering; solo-hero stories where you ignored partners score poorly.
