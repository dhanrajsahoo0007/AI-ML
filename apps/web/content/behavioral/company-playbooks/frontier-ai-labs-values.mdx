## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  The three labs publish very different amounts about their interviews, and the best-attested behavioral prompt among
  them is Anthropic's: **tell me about a time your values were tested**. Prepare one true story for it, including how
  you felt then and how you feel now, and treat most other claims about these loops as unconfirmed.
</KeyIdea>

## The values round

  spec="animated/behavioral/ai-lab-values-round"

Two candidates, one interviewing for a staff role and one for a senior role, describe a separate culture or values round at Anthropic. Both report a prompt close to "tell me about a time you did or built something that went against your values". A second reported form is "can you push back when something conflicts with your values? Tell me about a time you did."

Walk through the diagram:

1. **The prompt** asks for an event, not for your opinions about ethics.
2. **The pressure.** Say who wanted what, and what you stood to lose by refusing.
3. **What you did, and what it cost.** The first form of the prompt asks about a time you went along. An honest answer includes that.
4. **"How did you feel at the time?"** is a reported follow-up.
5. **"How do you feel about it now?"** and **"What would you change?"** are the others. The candidates say they were warned that the questions might be uncomfortable.

No first-hand report of this kind could be found for OpenAI or Google DeepMind. It is still worth preparing for all three.

## What they score

None of the three labs publishes a behavioral rubric. This table is built from the prompts reported most often across them. The signals are our reading.

| Reported theme                                | A strong answer shows                                                         | What weakens it                                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Values under pressure**                     | One real event, what you did, and an honest account of how it sits with you   | "I have never compromised my values"               |
| **Why this lab, and your doubts about it**    | A reason tied to the lab's stated mission, and one real reservation           | Praise only, or a reason that fits any AI company  |
| **Being sure and being wrong**                | A result or belief you held firmly, the evidence against it, what you changed | A mistake that was really someone else's           |
| **Tough feedback, given and received**        | The words used, your first reaction and the change                            | Only feedback you gave, never feedback you took    |
| **Disagreement over direction**               | The other person's case stated fairly, and how the choice was made            | The other person "did not understand the research" |
| **Your own contribution to a team effort**    | The line between what you did and what the team did                           | "We" for everything, or "I" for everything         |
| **How you think about the risks of the work** | A specific risk in something you built, and what you did about it             | Safety as someone else's job, or as a slogan       |

## The loop at each lab

### Anthropic

**Official.** Anthropic's careers page says interviews are held over Google Meet, technical roles use live coding tools, and interviews also cover your experience and motivation. It does not list the rounds or name a culture interview. It says no interview feedback is given and that you can apply again after 12 months.

**Reported.** interviewing.io, from conversations with Anthropic engineers, describes a 30-minute recruiter call, a coding challenge of 60 to 90 minutes, then an onsite of about five one-hour rounds: a hiring manager call, coding, system design, a second coding round and a company-values round. One candidate says the values round was run by non-technical interviewers, with one more person shadowing. interviewing.io says the decision is a consensus of the panel, with the hiring manager deciding when there is none. Anthropic has not confirmed any of this.

**Published values.** Act for the global good. Hold light and shade. Be good to our users. Ignite a race to the top on safety. Do the simple thing that works. Be helpful, honest, and harmless. Put the mission first. No source ties a given interview question to a given value, so do not try to guess which one is being tested. Have a story that fits two of them and one honest doubt about the mission, because candidates report being asked for theirs.

### OpenAI

**Official.** We could not open OpenAI's interview guide or careers pages, so nothing in this section is confirmed by OpenAI, and we print no list of its values.

**Reported.** interviewing.io, from conversations with current and former OpenAI engineers, describes a 30-minute recruiter call, a one-hour technical phone screen and a one-hour system design screen. The onsite is reported at 4 to 6 hours: a 45-minute behavioral call with a senior manager, a 45-minute presentation, an hour of coding, an hour of system design, and a second behavioral round of 30 minutes on working with teams. Some candidates also get a coding round that uses AI agents. The same source says hiring is decentralised. All the OpenAI prompts we have come from aggregators, not from first-hand reports.

### Google DeepMind

**Official.** DeepMind's careers page and interview guide describe four stages: initial interviews (a 30-minute recruiter call, sometimes the hiring manager too), skills interviews (two or three calls on the role's competencies, including potential peers), final interviews (team leads and leadership, including your likely manager), then decision and offer. Many interviews are competency based and the guide recommends STAR. Candidates are assessed against clearly defined criteria in each interview. Most interviews are virtual and the process takes 4 to 10 weeks. DeepMind and Google run separate processes.

**Not published.** The guide names no separate behavioral or values round and gives no round lengths beyond the recruiter call. It says DeepMind values curiosity and intellectual honesty, and it lists no named values. Prep sites describe one behavioral round. Claims that DeepMind runs a Google-style round or a Google committee review conflict with the official guide.

## Worked answers

<Tabs title="Two answers for a values round">
  <Tab label="A time you went along">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about a time you did or built something that went against your values."*

    - **The event.** *"Two years ago I built a cancellation flow with seven screens. I thought a customer who wants to leave should be able to do it in one."*
    - **The pressure.** *"Churn was our top metric that quarter and the growth lead owned the design. I was eight months into the job. I objected once in the design review, was told it was decided, and I built it."*
    - **What it cost.** *"Churn fell by 6 percent for a quarter. Complaints about cancelling went from about 10 a month to more than 90, and two of them quoted my screens."*
    - **How I felt then.** *"Uneasy, and a bit relieved that it was not my call. That relief is the part I do not like."*
    - **How I feel now, and what I would change.** *"I think I gave up too early. I had one conversation and no data. Six months later I brought the complaint numbers to the same lead with a two-screen design, and we shipped that. Today I would bring the evidence the first time and ask for a test."*

    **Why it works:** it answers the question asked, including the going along, and the feelings are specific.

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Sure, and wrong">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about a time you were sure you had the answer and turned out to be wrong."*

    - **Situation.** *"I am a machine learning engineer. I fine-tuned a support model and our evaluation score rose from 71 to 83 percent. I told the team it was ready and wrote the launch note."*
    - **What happened.** *"A colleague asked how the test set had been built. I checked. About a fifth of the test questions were near-copies of training examples, because both came from the same month of tickets. On a clean test set from a later month the score was 74."*
    - **Action.** *"I withdrew the launch note the same day and said why in the team channel. I rebuilt the split by date, and added a check that fails the evaluation if any test item is too similar to a training item."*
    - **Result.** *"The launch moved by three weeks. The model we shipped scored 78 on the clean set."*
    - **Learning.** *"I was sure because the number agreed with what I wanted. I now ask someone else to try to break an evaluation before I report it."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Red flags

- **No story.** "I have never compromised" ends the conversation. Recover by offering a small, real one.
- **A lecture.** Opinions about AI ethics in place of an event. Recover with "Let me give you the actual case."
- **Borrowed views on safety.** Repeating a lab's public position back to it. Say what you think, including where you are unsure, and tie it to something you built.
- **Overclaiming a result.** In a lab interview, a number you cannot defend does more damage than a modest one.

## Common questions about this round

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="Is the values round where most candidates are rejected?">
    Prep sites repeat this about Anthropic and attribute it to recruiters, but we found no primary source. Do not treat
    it as fact. Prepare for the round as seriously as for the technical ones.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Will every lab ask about my views on AI safety?">
    That is not confirmed for any of the three. Prompts about why safety matters to you and how you think about the
    risks of the technology are reported for Anthropic and OpenAI by secondary sources. Have a considered view and one
    example from your own work.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Is a Google DeepMind interview the same as a Google interview?">
    No. DeepMind's guide says the two processes are run by different teams. Use DeepMind's own four stages to plan.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
