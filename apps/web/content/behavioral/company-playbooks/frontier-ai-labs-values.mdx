Behavioral and mission alignment interviews at frontier AI labs (**Anthropic**, **OpenAI**, **Google DeepMind**, and leading AI infrastructure startups) look fundamentally different from legacy Big Tech loops. Because model capabilities, evaluation benchmarks, and post-training recipes shift every few weeks, these labs select for **high-agency generalists** who combine **empirical scientific rigor**, **extreme velocity under uncertainty**, and **principled AI safety and deployment judgment**.

<KeyIdea title="Empirical Rigor + High Agency + Safety as an Engineering Discipline">
Frontier AI labs reject two opposite archetypes: the reckless "ship-at-all-costs" cowboy who ignores eval regressions, data leakage, or misuse vectors, and the slow academic who cannot ship reliable production systems. Winning candidates treat **evals, red-teaming, and alignment guardrails as first-class systems engineering invariants** that enable—rather than block—rapid deployment.
</KeyIdea>

### The Four Core Signals in Frontier AI Interviews

1. **Mission Alignment & Safety Pragmatism (Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind):**
   - Interviewers ask: *"Why do you want to work at a frontier AI lab right now, and how do you think about the tension between rapid capability deployment and safety/alignment?"*
   - They look for nuanced, grounded engineering thinking—such as defense-in-depth classifiers, Responsible Scaling Policies (RSPs), automated red-teaming evals, and staged deployment monitoring—not generic sci-fi platitudes.
2. **Thriving in Zero-Playbook Ambiguity:**
   - Roadmaps at AI labs are invalidated every 60 days as new pre-training or reasoning paradigms land. You must show stories where you threw away sunk-cost code without ego the moment a simpler model-native approach emerged.
3. **Obsession with Evals & Data Hygiene:**
   - When an LLM or ML system misbehaves, do you tweak prompts blindly ("vibe-driven development"), or do you inspect 100 raw failure trajectories, build a deterministic eval harness, check for train-test contamination, and quantify confidence intervals?
4. **Low-Ego Research ↔ Engineering Collaboration:**
   - Research Scientists and Research/Systems Engineers work in tight, flat pods. Territorial friction between "research" and "production engineering" is an immediate no-hire.
