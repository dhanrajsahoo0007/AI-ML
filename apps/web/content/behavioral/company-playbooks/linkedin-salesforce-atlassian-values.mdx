## In one sentence

<KeyIdea>
  All three companies publish **five values** and ask for past stories that are heard against them. **Atlassian** is the
  only one that publishes how its values interview runs: 45 minutes, often with someone from outside your team. For
  LinkedIn and Salesforce, the shape of the round comes from reports.
</KeyIdea>

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn's careers site publishes its culture and values. It does not describe the interview loop.

What is reported and not confirmed by LinkedIn: interviewing.io, writing from conversations with current and former LinkedIn interviewers, describes two recruiter calls, a one-hour technical phone screen and an onsite of four one-hour rounds: coding, system design, a technical communication round on one past project, and a **behavioral round**. That round is described as a conversation with ad hoc questions tied to the values. Team matching with hiring managers follows.

### Salesforce

Salesforce's candidate resources page says you meet **several interviewers**, such as the hiring manager, a team leader, a future peer or a key stakeholder. It tells you to expect "tell us about a time when" questions on five themes: working in a team, adapting to change, driving equality, solving a problem and innovating. It names no separate values round and gives no lengths.

What is reported: interviewing.io describes a 30-minute recruiter call, a one-hour technical phone screen, and an onsite of two coding rounds, a system design round and a behavioral round of one hour each.

### Atlassian

Atlassian's engineering interview guide (dated August 2023) lists five interviews:

- **Coding**, **craft** and **system design**, 60 minutes each.
- A **management interview**, 60 minutes, run by an engineering manager. It is structured and built on scenarios, and it covers four areas: driving outcomes, applying and sharing lessons, managing conflict, and initiative and leadership. Atlassian's interview page says it is usually one-on-one with the hiring manager or a more senior manager.
- A **values interview**, 45 minutes: a set of behavioral questions on how your thinking and actions line up with the five values. The interview page says the interviewer is **often someone from outside the team**, for example from Sales, HR or Customer Support.

## What they score

None of the three publishes a scoring guide. The table is ours. It groups the published values into five signals, so one story can serve at more than one company.

| Signal                   | Value names that point to it                                                                                     | A strong story shows                                        | What weakens it                          |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **The user comes first** | "We put members first" (LinkedIn), "Customer Success" (Salesforce), "Don't #@!% the customer" (Atlassian)        | What you gave up for the user: a date, a metric, a feature  | "We always think about the user"         |
| **Openness**             | "We are open, honest and constructive" (LinkedIn), "Trust" (Salesforce), "Open company, no bullshit" (Atlassian) | Bad news told early, to the person it affects               | News softened, delayed or told to others |
| **Team before self**     | "We act as One LinkedIn", "We trust and care about each other" (LinkedIn), "Play, as a team" (Atlassian)         | A team goal you put ahead of your own, and what it cost you | Every win in the story is yours          |
| **Inclusion**            | "We embody diversity, inclusion and belonging" (LinkedIn), "Equality" (Salesforce)                               | One change to how a team hires, meets or reviews work       | A statement of belief with no action     |
| **Starting a change**    | "Innovation" (Salesforce), "Be the change you seek" (Atlassian)                                                  | A change you began that other people took up                | An idea you had that nobody adopted      |

## How the decision is made

- **Atlassian** publishes this. A **hiring committee that works independently of the interviewers** makes the final review. It sees only information from the process, such as interview feedback and your CV.
- **LinkedIn** does not publish it. interviewing.io reports that each round is graded on a 4-point scale where 3 is a pass, and that a hiring committee then decides hire and level. LinkedIn has not confirmed this.
- **Salesforce** does not publish it. One source says the coding rounds carry the most weight and that team matching comes before the offer numbers when you apply to an org. Treat both as unconfirmed.

Prep sites say Atlassian's values interview can veto an offer. Nothing Atlassian publishes says so.

## The published values

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn's careers page lists five: **We put members first**, **We trust and care about each other**, **We are open, honest and constructive**, **We act as One LinkedIn**, and **We embody diversity, inclusion and belonging**.

Many prep lists still use older names such as "take intelligent risks", "demand excellence" and "act like an owner", or the phrase "next play". They are not on the current page. Use the current names.

### Salesforce

The Salesforce values page lists five: **Trust**, **Customer Success**, **Innovation**, **Equality** and **Sustainability**. The candidate resources page names four and leaves out Sustainability. "Ohana" appears in third-party guides and is not on the values page.

### Atlassian

Atlassian's interview guide names the five values and gives example questions for each.

| Value                            | Where it shows in a story                                                   |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Open company, no bullshit**    | A hard conversation you started, or a conflict you settled face to face     |
| **Build with heart and balance** | A decision about priorities, and a time you or your team missed the mark    |
| **Don't #@!% the customer**      | A choice you made for the user that cost the team something                 |
| **Play, as a team**              | You helped a teammate who was behind, or put the team's goal ahead of yours |
| **Be the change you seek**       | A change you pushed for, and what you learned from pushing                  |

## Worked answers

<Tabs title="Two answers for a values interview">
  <Tab label="Pushing for a change">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about a time you pushed for a change."* Atlassian's guide gives this as an example question.

    - **Situation and task.** *"I am a mobile engineer. Every release of our Android app needed a manual checklist of 34 steps. It took one person two days, every two weeks."*
    - **Action.** *"I proposed replacing it with automated checks. Our QA lead said no: two of the manual steps had caught real payment bugs. I asked her which steps she trusted least to a script, and she named six. I automated the other 28 and kept her six as a 40-minute manual pass."*
    - **Result.** *"A release went from two days to about three hours. One of the other two squads adopted the scripts. The third still uses the old checklist, because their app has a hardware step I could not automate."*
    - **Learning.** *"My first proposal treated her objection as resistance. The version that passed started from the two bugs she had caught."*

  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Bad news for a customer">
    **Question:** *"Tell me about delivering bad news to a customer."* This prompt is commonly reported for Salesforce and not confirmed.

    - **Situation and task.** *"I work on a data sync service. I found that our job had been dropping records with accented characters in the name field for nine days. For one large customer that was 1,140 contacts."*
    - **Action.** *"I told our account manager within the hour and asked to join the call with the customer's administrator that afternoon, before the fix was ready. On the call I said what was missing, since when, and that I would have a restore plan by noon the next day. I sent the list of affected records that evening."*
    - **Result.** *"We restored all 1,140 records in two days. It was too late for one thing: their quarterly report had already gone out with the wrong totals, and they had to reissue it. They stayed, and asked for a weekly count check, which we now send to every customer on that plan."*
    - **Learning.** *"The job reported success because it counted rows written and never compared them with rows read. I added that comparison to every sync job we run."*

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Red flags

- **Reciting the value names.** "I really believe in trust" is not evidence. Recover by describing the action and letting the interviewer attach the label.
- **A story only an engineer can follow.** At Atlassian the values interviewer is often from Sales, HR or Customer Support. Recover by retelling the story with no system names in it.
- **An inclusion answer with no action.** Recover with one thing you changed, such as how design reviews are scheduled across time zones, and who it helped.

## Common questions about this round

<Quiz title="Common questions">
  <QuizItem question="Should I name the value in my answer?">
    You do not need to. Tell the story so the action is clear. If you do name a value, use the wording on the company's
    own page, and only after you have described what you did.
  </QuizItem>
  <QuizItem question="Does Salesforce run a separate values interview?">
    Its candidate page does not name one. It says to expect "tell us about a time when" questions from the people you
    meet. Reports describe a one-hour behavioral round in the onsite. Prepare for values questions in every
    conversation.
  </QuizItem>
</Quiz>
