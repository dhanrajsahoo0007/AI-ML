A user posts a German comment with one letter swapped for a Cyrillic look-alike; it is a dehumanising slur.
Kestrel, the platform on this page, gets 140M posts, comments, edits and images a day, and about 1 in 100 breaks a rule, mostly spam.
A person cannot read 140M things, a classifier cannot read a policy, and a model that reads the written policy costs 16 to 556 times a classifier per call.
The hard part is deciding who looks at what, with thresholds that follow the cost of each mistake, keeping the worst content out of view while it waits, and then proving what you missed.

- Interviewers use this question to see whether you cascade from cheap to expensive, set thresholds from the cost of each error, hold the worst harms for a person, treat people as the main cost, and measure misses on views with a labeller that did not make the decisions.
- **Running example:** Kestrel is an illustrative public community app with 40M daily users in five markets (EU 30%, UK 8%, US 25%, India 22%, the rest 15%). It is not one of the Brightpost systems.
- **Labels:** no platform publishes the share of traffic per tier or the latency per stage, so every tier share, base rate, error rate, handle time and latency here is illustrative. Model prices and terms are as of November 2026.
- **Harm units:** the cost of each kind of error is in harm units, a policy device that sets thresholds; it is not money, and no platform publishes such numbers.

## Overview

- **A cascade from cheap to expensive.** Hash lists and Kestrel's own banks settle ≈ 548,240 units a day (0.39%); small classifiers run on every unit and remove ≈ 201,700 at once (0.14%); a policy-reading model takes ≈ 3.68M calls a day (2.6%); people see ≈ 247,900 items (0.18%).
- **Thresholds come from the cost of each error.** Act when P(violates) ≥ τ = false-alarm cost ÷ (false-alarm cost + miss cost), one global value per family (hate τ 0.571). The model is called only where its verdict can flip that decision (hate 0.0417 to 0.942).
- **Local law is a separate, market-scoped withhold.** A comment legal under Kestrel's terms but illegal in Germany is withheld in Germany only, at the EU's own τ (0.333), with the legal ground named.
- **Three paths.** Hashes and classifiers run inside the post request (text ≈ 34 / 97 ms p50 / p95); severity 0–1 band units are **held** out of view until decided; severity 2–3 band units publish with limited distribution while the model decides in ≈ 1.2 / 4.9 s.
- **People are the cost and the safety net.** ≈ 3,965 handling hours a day from an hourly roster give ≈ 1,492 full-time staff, ≈ 6.76M USD a month: 83% of ≈ 8.13M all-in, against ≈ 50,500 USD of model calls (0.6%).
- **Misses are measured on views, by someone else.** Weekly samples of views are labelled by a model from a different family, positives confirmed by people; hate is ≈ 5.3 violating views per 10,000, with a one-sided 95% upper bound of 6.59 on 4 weeks.

<KeyIdea>Spend each check where it changes a decision. Hashes and classifiers settle the clear 97%; the worst harms wait out of view for a person; a model that reads the written policy settles most of the rest; and a sample of views, labelled by someone else, tells you what all of them missed.</KeyIdea>

- **Terms:** a *unit* is one post, comment, edit or image; a *family* is one kind of harm with its own policy and costs; the *band* is the range of classifier probability where the model's verdict can change the decision; a *hold* keeps a unit visible to its author only; *prevalence* is violating views ÷ all views.

## API design

| Call | Who calls it | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/check` (sync, internal) | Post service, inside the create or edit request | Every text unit; returns `allow`, `nudge`, `limited`, `hold` or `block` with families and a `decision_id` |
| `content.created`, `content.edited` (Kafka) | Post and media services | Every unit; an image is checked when the upload lands, before anyone but the author sees it |
| `content.views_crossed` (1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000) | View counter | The reach re-check |
| `POST /v1/reports` (`Idempotency-Key`) | Apps, web | `{ unit_id, reason, reporter_market }`; an EU report is handled as a DSA notice (conservative: Art. 16 covers reports of illegal content) and the notifier is told the decision |
| `POST /v1/notices` | Trusted flaggers; illegality notices with a legal ground | Priority queue; illegality claims screened by the model with the market addendum, then trained legal-ground reviewers |
| `POST /v1/ncii-requests` | Depicted persons or their representatives | An id, a 48-hour clock (24 h for India), a hash of the image and its known identical copies |
| `POST /v1/orders` | Authority portal (authenticated, signed) | Removal orders; an EU terrorism order starts a 60-minute clock |
| `GET /v1/review/next?pool=`, `POST /v1/review/{task_id}/decision` | Review app | Next task by priority; 5-minute lock; `{ action, rule_id, scope, note, version }` with an optimistic lock |
| `POST /v1/appeals`, `GET /v1/actions/{id}/statement` | Users and notifiers | An appeal within 6 months; a statement of reasons per restriction |
| `POST /v1/policies/{family}/versions`, `…/rules`, `…/{v}/shadow`, `…/{v}/rollout?markets=`, `…/{v}/backfill?days=` | Policy team | The policy registry (Deep dive 3) |

The check call answers inside the post request; the model's verdict arrives later on the decision record.

```http
POST /v1/check
X-Request-Id: req_7Hc2
{ "unit_id": "c_90214", "kind": "comment", "author": "u_5531", "market": "DE", "lang": "de",
  "text": "…", "parent_id": "p_88120" }

200 OK   (34 ms)
{ "decision": "limited", "families": ["hate"], "decision_id": "d_4f1a" }
```

The policy model returns fixed keys and no probability; code applies the band.

```json
{ "violates": true, "rule_id": "H2.3", "local_illegal": ["DE"], "escalate": false,
  "rationale": "Compares a protected group to vermin; the parent post is a news article, not a quote.",
  "policy_version": "hate@2026-11-18.2" }
```

- **The model proposes; code decides.** A "yes" inside the band acts, because the posterior after a "yes" is ≥ τ by construction of the band; a "no" allows (2–3) or releases (0–1). `escalate`, a refusal or malformed JSON goes to a person and is counted (0.5% assumed).
- **A held unit is not an oracle.** The author sees the post as posted while the ≤ 10 s model path runs; only a unit a person holds shows "under review", so the author's own view gives nothing away. A second account can still see a held post is missing, so holds are rate-limited per account and device and a red-team family probes it.
- **Statements of reasons are built in code** from the decision record: restriction, scope, facts, whether automation detected and decided, ground, redress. The model's rationale stays internal, because a rationale can be wrong.
- **Idempotency.** Reports deduplicate on `(unit_id, reporter)`; one action per `(unit_id, family, policy_version, action)`; a unit a person decided under the current version is not re-queued by new reports; an edit supersedes and never doubles a strike.
- **Content is data.** The unit, the thread and the OCR text sit in delimited blocks after the cached policy; the report reason is never in the prompt.
- **Errors.** A Bedrock `ValidationException` that reports a CSAM block on an image means the classifier missed it: the image goes to a CSEA specialist, and the report notes that Bedrock may have reported it too. Other validation errors go to engineering; a timeout falls to the fallback (text) or a person (images).

One action row per decision is the record every statement, appeal and transparency report is built from.

```sql
CREATE TABLE action (
  action_id uuid PRIMARY KEY,
  unit_id text, family text, policy_version text, action_seq int,
  action text,                 -- 'nudge' | 'label' | 'interstitial' | 'age_gate' | 'limit' | 'hold' | 'geo_withhold' | 'remove' | 'restore' | 'strike'
  scope text[],                -- markets, e.g. '{DE}' for a national-law withhold
  rule_id text, ground text,   -- 'tos:H2.3' or 'law:DE-StGB-130'
  detected_by text,            -- 'hash' | 'classifier' | 'model' | 'report' | 'trusted_flagger' | 'order' | 'recheck'
  decided_by text,             -- 'automated' | reviewer id
  statement_id text, appeal_state text, created_at timestamptz
);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX one_action ON action (unit_id, family, policy_version, action, action_seq);
```

- **Storage:** decision records 28 GB a day (2.52 TB hot); actions 2.87 GB a day (2.10 TB over 2 years); removed content ≈ 218 GB a day (≈ 39.9 TB over 6 months); model logs 3.7 GB a day.
- **Reviewer access:** each reviewer sees only their pool's families and markets; identities masked by default (an unmask is logged); review on locked-down virtual desktops; restoring a high-profile account needs two people.
- **Access patterns:** decisions and actions by unit id (appeals, statements); samples by week and family (prevalence); tasks by pool and priority (review app); banks by hash prefix (pre-publish).

## Building the architecture, step by step

- We start from one classifier that scores every post and blocks above 0.5, and add a part only when a requirement or a driver forces it.
- Each figure shows the design after that step; the step's new parts are outlined and the moving dots show its flow.

### Step 1: One classifier on every post (F1)

The baseline: the post API asks a decision engine, which scores the text with one classifier and blocks anything above 0.5.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/architecture" step={1} caption="Step 1: the post API sends every unit to a decision engine, which scores it with one text classifier, blocks above 0.5 and writes a decision record. Nothing else checks it." />

- **Adds:** Kestrel's apps, the post API, the decision engine, a text classifier, decision records.
- **Flow:**
  1. A user posts; the post API calls the decision engine inside the request.
  2. The classifier returns one score; above 0.5 the post is blocked, otherwise it publishes.
  3. The decision record keeps the score and the model version.
- **Why this and not that:** start with the simplest design that passes the evals (P1); every later tier exists because this one leaves a measured gap.
- **The gap:** a known illegal image re-uploaded is only "probably bad"; one threshold serves spam and threats alike; the worst content is visible until someone acts.

- **Adds:** hash and rules, PhotoDNA Cloud and a vetted vendor CSEA classifier, hash banks.
- **Flow:**
  1. An upload is hashed: MD5 and SHA against the NCMEC and IWF lists locally, PDQ against GIFCT, StopNCII and own banks, PhotoDNA Cloud in parallel with a 600 ms timeout.
  2. Text goes through exact spam lists, URL lists and term rules.
  3. A CSEA exact match is removed at once and a specialist signs off the report on the match record; any other vetted match goes to a specialist who views it, and a second specialist confirms before the report; own-bank matches (person-confirmed entries) act.
- **Why this and not that:** the cheapest certain signal first (P4), and rules in code (P10). A known CSEA image must be removed and reported, so it cannot wait for a classifier's opinion.
- **The gap:** most violations are new, and a small crop or a rotation past about 5° can move a PDQ hash past its match distance.

### Step 3: Classifiers, global thresholds, three paths (F1, F3, N3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/architecture" step={3} caption="Step 3: a check API runs the text, image, OCR and age classifiers in parallel; one calibrated threshold and band per family decides allow, nudge, remove, hold or band, inside about 34 ms for text." />

- **Adds:** the check API, classifiers (a multilingual text encoder, a multi-head image classifier with an age estimate, OCR).
- **Flow:**
  1. The normaliser folds look-alike characters and drops zero-width ones.
  2. The text encoder, the image classifier, OCR and the age estimate run in parallel and return a calibrated probability per family.
  3. One global τ and band per family decide: allow, nudge, remove (severity 2–3 above the band), hold (severity 0–1 band and above) or band.
  4. A severity 2–3 band unit publishes with limited distribution: visible in its thread, not recommended, not in search or notifications.
- **Why this and not that:** budget latency per stage (P5). The text check costs ≈ 34 / 97 ms and an image ≈ 198 / 500 ms, inside the post request; the model would cost seconds.
- **The gap:** ≈ 2.52M band units a day, and a classifier cannot read a policy that changed this morning.

What happens to a unit depends on its family's severity, not only on its score.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/holds" stepper caption="Three paths after the pre-publish check: severity 0–1 band units are held out of view (the author still sees them as posted), severity 2–3 band units publish with limited distribution while the model decides." />

**How to read it:**

- Step 1 is the three paths from one score: allow below the band, remove above it for severity 2–3, and the band in between.
- Step 2 splits the band by severity: a held threat is visible to nobody but its author; a hate-band comment is visible in its thread with limited distribution.

**The numbers:**

- Held: ≈ 562,800 units a day (0.40%). A threat at P = 0.30 risks 0.30 × 400 = 120 harm units if published and costs ≈ 0.70 × 0.05 = 0.035 to hold, so the cost rule holds it.
- Limited: severity 2–3 band units are ≈ 1.99M a day (1.4% of units). Holding them would make every hate-band comment "pending" for seconds, for errors that cost little.

### Step 4: The policy-reading model where it can change a decision (F4, N4)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/architecture" step={4} caption="Step 4: band units queue for a policy-reading model that gets one family’s cached policy, the unit and its thread; code applies the verdict. A self-hosted model takes text when the provider fails." />

- **Adds:** the hold and band queue, the policy-reading model (small and mid), the self-hosted fallback model, the policy registry.
- **Flow:**
  1. A band unit is queued with its family, market and language group.
  2. The model gets one family's cached policy, the market addendum and five examples (1,100 tokens), then the unit, the thread and signals (380 tokens), and returns a JSON verdict.
  3. Code acts: a "yes" removes (2–3) or keeps the unit held for a person (0–1); a "no" allows, or releases a held unit in ≈ 2.0 / 6.3 s.
  4. CSEA images and NCII are never sent; if the provider is down, text goes to the self-hosted fallback.
- **Why this and not that:** route by difficulty (P3); keep the cached prefix stable (P7); constrain the output (P9). Outside the band a classifier is the better judge of clear cases.
- **The gap:** held units need a person, and the model is wrong on 2–8% of hard units.

The cascade narrows the day's traffic to the few units that need a reader.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/cascade" stepper caption="The cascade a day on Kestrel’s illustrative volumes: 140M units narrow to about 2.6% that reach the model and 0.18% that reach a person; reports, re-checks, appeals and QA join from the side." />

**How to read it:**

- Step 1 is the funnel, the cascade only: hash lists and banks, classifier removals, held and band units, then people.
- Step 2 adds the side inputs (reports, reach re-checks, appeals, QA and prevalence labels), which are not funnel stages because they arrive by other routes.

**The numbers:**

- 140M units → hashes 548,240 → classifier removals ≈ 201,700 → held ≈ 562,800 and band ≈ 2.52M → people from the cascade ≈ 129,100.
- Model calls: band 2.52M + re-checks 193,800 + reports 940,800 + local law 18,800 ≈ 3.68M a day, peak ≈ 106 a second.

### Step 5: People by severity, reach and uncertainty (F5, N3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/architecture" step={5} caption="Step 5: held positives, escalations, disagreements and vetted matches go to review queues: specialist hubs, 16 language pools for threats and general pools, ordered by severity, reach and uncertainty." />

- **Adds:** review queues, reviewers (hubs and pools), the review app.
- **Flow:**
  1. Held positives, units above the release line, escalations, disagreements, the CSEA band and vetted matches enter a queue.
  2. The router picks the pool (a specialist hub or the author's language pool) and orders by severity × reach × uncertainty.
  3. The review app shows content blurred, greyscale and muted, with click to reveal; the decision becomes a label.
- **Why this and not that:** keep a human escape hatch (P15). A threat must not wait behind spam, and a Hinglish post needs a Hinglish reader.
- **The gap:** users get no reason and no appeal, and nobody owns the legal clocks.

### Step 6: Enforcement, reasons, appeals, reports and legal clocks (F6, F7, F8, F9)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/architecture" step={6} caption="Step 6: enforcement applies an action with its scope, builds a statement of reasons from the action ledger, takes reports, notices, requests, orders and appeals, and keeps CSEA and NCII evidence in vaults." />

- **Adds:** enforcement and notices; users, flaggers, victims and authorities as callers; the action ledger; the CSEA and NCII vaults.
- **Flow:**
  1. A decision becomes an action on the ladder with its scope; a statement of reasons is built from the record and the EU's go to the DSA database in 15-minute batches.
  2. Reports are routed by reason before any model call; an appeal goes to a different person, who sees the model's rationale only on request.
  3. Confirmed CSEA goes to NCMEC; an NCII request starts a 48-hour clock; an EU terrorism order goes to an on-call specialist (≤ 60 minutes).
- **Why this and not that:** carry provenance (P14) and enforce rules in code (P10). A prompt cannot guarantee a legal duty.
- **The gap:** nobody knows what was missed, and a policy change is a gamble.

An appeal is also how the system learns where it was wrong.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/appeals-loop" stepper caption="An appeal goes to a person who did not decide first; an overturn restores the post, removes the strike, unbanks the hash entry, re-reviews its matches and adds a near-miss to the gold set." />

**How to read it:**

- Step 1: an appeal goes to a reviewer in the right pool who did not make the first decision.
- Step 2: an upheld appeal ends there, and notifiers can appeal "no action" too; an overturn starts the loop: restore, remove the strike, unbank, re-review every unit matched to that bank entry, add a near-miss to the gold set, feed retraining.

**The numbers:**

- ≈ 55,370 appeals a day at 60 s each (≈ 923 hours) plus ≈ 8,381 notifier appeals; ≈ 33.8% overturned (hate 29.0%, graphic 24.1%, spam 39.0%).
- Those rates follow from precision and assumed appeal rates (25% of wrong actions, 8% of right ones); at 4% of right ones hate's overturn rate is ≈ 43.5%, at 12% ≈ 22.3%.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Bank every automated removal and one wrong removal becomes a wrong removal of every look-alike upload. Hate banks hold person-confirmed entries only; an overturn unbanks the entry and re-reviews everything it matched.
</Callout>

### Step 7: Measure what was missed; change policy safely (F10, F11, N1, N2)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/architecture" step={7} caption="Step 7: a sampler labels a uniform sample of views and calibration bins, and a policy gate runs term rules, gold-set gates, a shadow, a canary and a backfill before any policy change goes live." />

- **Adds:** the prevalence and calibration sampler, the policy gate, gold sets and labels.
- **Flow:**
  1. A uniform sample of views is snapshotted, screened for CSEA, labelled by a model from a different family, and its positives confirmed by people.
  2. Prevalence is estimated on 4-week windows with intervals; calibration labels per score bin set the band edges.
  3. A policy change runs term rule → paired gate → shadow → canary → rollout → backfill.
- **Why this and not that:** evaluate continuously and degrade gracefully (P16). The enforcing model cannot see its own misses.
- **The gap:** adversaries adapt within days.

### Step 8: Adversarial adaptation; close the loop (F12, N6)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/architecture" step={8} caption="Step 8: a red team runs ten attack families through the whole cascade each month; misses from prevalence, appeals and reports feed fixes in hours and classifier retraining in weeks." />

- **Adds:** red team and retraining.
- **Flow:**
  1. Each month, ten attack families × 100 units run through the whole cascade; prevalence positives, overturned appeals and confirmed reports are tagged by evasion family.
  2. Normaliser, term-rule and detector fixes ship in hours; banks grow and overturns unbank; classifiers retrain on new labels in weeks.
  3. Every successful attack becomes a regression test in the gate.
- **Why this and not that:** treat content as untrusted (P13) and close the loop (P15); posts are written by people who know a model reads them.
- **The gap:** none; the gates decide each release.

The sequence follows one German hate comment from post to appeal, then a held threat.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/publish-sequence" stepper caption="One German hate comment: limited in about 34 ms, removed after the model’s “yes” in about 1.2 s, a statement of reasons in the next DSA batch, and an appeal upheld by a different reviewer; a held threat goes straight to the urgent pool." />

- **How to read it:** step 1 is the check inside the post request (limited at ≈ 34 ms) and the model's "yes" at ≈ 1.2 s; step 2 the removal, the statement in the next DSA batch and the next day's appeal; step 3 a held threat going straight to the urgent pool.

The German comment (a Cyrillic look-alike and a zero-width joiner inside a slur, under a news article about refugees), p50; the story is invented:

1. **0–5 ms:** the post service calls `POST /v1/check`.
2. **5–7 ms:** the normaliser folds the look-alike and drops the joiner. On the raw text the hate head gives P = 0.02, below the band's lower edge (0.0417): it would have been allowed.
3. **7–32 ms:** rules and exact lists find nothing; the encoder gives hate P = 0.30 on the folded text. Global hate τ = 8 ÷ 14 = 0.571, band 0.0417–0.942: in the band.
4. **≈ 34 ms:** decision `limited`; the request returns.
5. **≈ 0.2–1.2 s:** the small model reads the hate policy, the German addendum and five German examples (cached), the comment, the headline and two previous comments → `violates: true`, rule H2.3, `local_illegal: [DE]`.
6. **≈ 1.2 s:** the posterior after a "yes" is 0.30 × 0.92 ÷ (0.30 × 0.92 + 0.70 × 0.03) = 0.929 ≥ 0.571 → removed worldwide under terms rule H2.3, the German ground recorded as a second ground; strike 1.
7. **Within 15 minutes:** the statement of reasons (removal, own initiative, detected and decided by automated means, rule H2.3, redress) reaches the user and the DSA database in a batch of ≈ 4,487.
8. **Next day:** the user appeals; a German-pool reviewer who did not decide first sees the comment, the parent, the rule and the user's statement, and upholds. The call cost ≈ 0.000094 USD.

The threat ("tonight I end it, nobody will miss me", threats and self-harm, P = 0.30):

1. **≈ 34 ms:** P = 0.30 is above the release line 0.170, where even a model "no" would leave the posterior above τ = 0.0123, so the post is held and goes straight to the urgent pool with no model call.
2. **At once:** the author sees support resources and a helpline for their country (a design rule, not a cited standard).
3. **≤ 10 minutes p95:** a reviewer in the author's language pool reads the post and recent posts; imminent risk → a welfare or emergency referral; no risk → released with a resources label.

## Component choices and why

- Every box in the final diagram: what we picked, why, and why the usual alternatives lose. G ids refer to the drivers table; F and N to the requirements.

### Hashes: exact lists locally, PDQ for shared and own banks, PhotoDNA with a timeout

- **Choice:** NCMEC and IWF exact lists (MD5, SHA) matched in memory; PDQ against GIFCT, StopNCII and own banks, querying the 8 rotations and mirrors of each upload; PhotoDNA Cloud with a 600 ms timeout.
- **Why:** a known CSEA image must be removed and reported (G2, F2, F9); the IWF list carries no PDQ hashes, so perceptual CSEA matching uses PhotoDNA Cloud. Each list comes by vetting or licence: NCMEC registration, an IWF licence, GIFCT membership, StopNCII partnership, PhotoDNA for qualified users.
- **Why not cryptographic hashes alone:** any change to the file defeats them.
- **Why not perceptual hashes as automatic proof:** crops and rotations evade them and collisions can be crafted, so a non-exact vetted match always goes to a specialist; StopNCII asks partners to review matches too.
- **Why not bank every automated removal:** a wrong removal banked repeats on every look-alike. Hate banks hold person-confirmed entries; graphic and spam also take automated removals that survived 30 days without a successful appeal.
- **Why not PhotoDNA without a timeout:** its throughput, rate limits and region for EU images are not verified (an open item), and the post request cannot wait on them.

### Classifiers: one multilingual text encoder, a multi-head image model, a vendor CSEA classifier, OCR

- **Choice:** a ≈ 300M-parameter multilingual encoder with a head per family; a multi-head image classifier with an age-estimation head; a vetted vendor classifier for novel CSEA; OCR into the text classifier.
- **Why:** ≈ 20 ms a unit inside the request at ≈ 0.000006 USD, calibrated per family and language group (G3, G4, G11); 14 GPUs ≈ 25,200 USD a month across two regions.
- **Why not a hosted moderation API:** a fixed category list whose scores change on upgrades, and every unit sent to another vendor (G8, G9).
- **Why not Llama Guard 4 on every unit:** a 12B model for 140M units a day, with recall of 41–69% at false-positive rates of 3–11% on Meta's own sets (model card, 2025).
- **Why not buy image moderation:** a per-image API at ≈ 126,000 USD a month (Rekognition list tiers, Region not stated, as of November 2026) replaces only the image heads, which cost ≈ 4,100 a month on our GPUs.
- **Why not train our own CSEA classifier:** we cannot hold CSEA material to train on.

### Thresholds: one global τ per family from error costs; local law as a geo-withhold

- **Choice:** τ = cfp ÷ (cfp + cfn) on a calibrated probability, one value per family worldwide (cfp is the false-alarm cost, cfn the miss cost); a second, market-scoped withhold where national law forbids more than Kestrel's terms.
- **Why:** one terms-of-service rule must give one outcome whoever posts it, and illegality is national, so it restricts views only in that market with the legal ground named (G2, G8, F3).
- **Why not per-market thresholds on a global removal:** the same words would be removed worldwide at 0.333 when posted from the EU and kept until 0.571 from anywhere else.
- **Why not one threshold for all harms:** a missed CSEA image costs 400 × a wrong one (20,000 vs 50 harm units) and a missed spam post half a wrong spam removal.

### The band: where the verdict can flip the decision; the verdict is binary

- **Choice:** the band's lower edge is where a model "yes" lifts the posterior to τ, the upper edge where a "no" drops it to τ, using the model's measured error rates per classifier-score bin.
- **Why:** outside the band the model cannot change the decision, and the classifier is the better judge there; inside, the verdict decides (G4, F4).
- **Why not act on the model's stated confidence:** it is not a calibrated probability.
- **Why not one fixed band (say 0.3–0.7) for all families:** it would send clean units to a model with a 3% false-alarm rate where that is a loss, and skip units where a "yes" would act.

### Policy-reading models: small for hate, graphic, spam; mid for terrorism, threats, grooming text; a self-hosted fallback

- **Choice:** a small multimodal model (Claude Haiku 5.5 class) and a mid model (Claude Sonnet 5.5 class) on Amazon Bedrock, EU users through the EU cross-Region profile; gpt-oss-safeguard-20b self-hosted on 8 GPUs as the warm text fallback.
- **Why:** one family's policy per call, because extra policies in one prompt cost accuracy (OpenAI's guide, 2025); images need a multimodal model; terrorism and threats need context as much as rules (G7, G11). Bedrock states zero operator access for these models, and model providers cannot see prompts (read 23 November 2026).
- **Why not gpt-oss-safeguard as the primary:** it is text-only. As the fallback it keeps text decisions in-house at its own measured edges (hate 0.0554–0.934 at a 4% false-alarm and 9% miss rate).
- **Why not the model on every unit:** ≈ 1.80M USD a month all small, ≈ 17.2M as designed, and worse than a trained classifier on clear cases (How real systems do it).
- **Why not the mid model everywhere:** it costs ≈ 19 × the small one per text call; small moves to a family only after that family's gate passes.

### Three paths: pre-publish checks, holds for severity 0–1, limited distribution for 2–3

- **Choice:** hashes and classifiers inside the request; the whole severity 0–1 band held; the severity 2–3 band published with limited distribution while the model decides.
- **Why:** the text budget is ≈ 34 / 97 ms (N3); a held threat is visible to nobody, and the cost rule says hold because a miss costs far more than a delay (G2, G3).
- **Why not hold everything in the band:** ≈ 1.99M posts a day "pending" for seconds, turning a cheap error into a product delay.
- **Why not the model before publish for everyone:** seconds on every post, for the 97% the classifier settles.

### People: three specialist hubs, 16 language pools, general pools, an hourly roster

- **Choice:** hubs for CSEA, NCII and terrorism (and possible minors), 16 language pools for threats and self-harm, general pools; priority = severity × reach × uncertainty; an hourly roster with wellbeing limits (Deep dive 2).
- **Why:** a threat must not wait behind spam, and a Hinglish post needs a Hinglish reader (F5, N3, G2).
- **Why not one first-in, first-out queue:** severity is lost.
- **Why not the outsourced pace of 15–20 s an item (a 2025 study):** band units are the hard ones.
- **Why not staff to the daily mean:** urgent pools would miss their p95 every evening, when arrivals peak at 1.75 × the mean.

### Policy as a versioned prompt plus a term rule, gated

- **Choice:** each family's policy, addenda and examples are versions in a registry; a change runs a term rule, a paired gate, a shadow, a canary, a rollout and a backfill (Deep dive 3).
- **Why:** a new coded term scores near zero on a classifier trained before it existed, so only a rule finds it on day 0 (G6, F11).
- **Why not retrain first:** 2–4 weeks with the slur visible.
- **Why not push a prompt straight to production:** one bug can double false positives (Meta, Q4 2025; How real systems do it); the per-slice replay of known-fine band units catches a 2 × slice bug within two hours ≈ 99.96% of the time.

### Prevalence labeller, appeals and statements

- **Choice:** a self-hosted open-weight multimodal model from a different family labels sampled views, people confirm positives; appeals go to a person who did not decide first; statements of reasons are built from the record.
- **Why:** the enforcer's own misses are invisible to it (G1); DSA complaints must not be decided solely by automation (F8); a model rationale can be wrong (G8).
- **Why not the enforcing model as the labeller:** it would grade its own errors as correct.
- **Why not people label every sample:** ≈ 145,000 labels a week would take far longer and cost far more (Pinterest measured ≈ 15 × and over 10 ×; How real systems do it).
- **Why not the model re-judging an appeal:** it repeats its own error.

- Models ≈ 50,500 + classifiers 25,200 + fallback 14,400 USD a month = ≈ 0.0215 USD per 1,000 units (≤ 0.05).
- People ≈ 6.76M + engineering 0.9M (0.6–1.2M) + policy 0.2M + legal 90,000 + label vendors 30,000 + infrastructure 60,000 → ≈ 8.13M, ≈ 1.94 USD per 1,000 units (≤ 2.5). Twice the handle times gives ≈ 2.82 (≈ 2.90 with the high engineering and policy lines) and breaks N4.
- At 10 × the model prices the model line is ≈ 505,000 USD, 6% of the total: the conclusions hold.

- **Model errors on band units** (measured per score bin): false alarms 2–3%, misses 5–8%, escalations 1–3% plus 0.5% refusals. People: 2% / 4%; specialists 1% / 2%.
- **Calibration in the tails:** a stratified sample by score bin, sized so P at each family's lower edge is within ± 50% at 95%: n = 1.96² × (1 − p) ÷ (0.25p), from 240 labels for spam (0.0602) to 7,668 for CSEA (0.002). In all ≈ 115,000 a quarter ≈ 1,264 a day; CSEA and NCII bins go to specialists only.
- **QA:** 0.5% of automated decisions go to a blind second person, 10% of disagreements to an expert; a week gives hate precision ≈ 90.3% ± 0.70 points, graphic ≈ 92.3% ± 0.94, spam ≈ 95.7% ± 0.21.
- **Gold sets:** per model-read family (terrorism, threats, hate, graphic, spam) 2,000 items, a dev half for prompt work and a locked hold-out half for the gate (500 violating, 500 fine near the boundary, by language group, with dialect, reclaimed-term, counter-speech and quotation sets). CSEA grooming text is gated on a specialist-held text set inside the vault, run there and never exported.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/release-gate" caption="Every classifier, edge, prompt, policy or model change passes seven gates; this hate release passes six, then trips the per-market replay of known-fine near-miss units in Germany and is rolled back." />

- **How to read it:** a hate release fans out to seven checks; lime passes, red fails, and one red blocks. It passes six, then the per-market replay of known-fine near-miss units trips in Germany and the release is rolled back.

**The numbers:**

1. **Paired non-inferiority** on the hold-out, per family, for recall and specificity: with ≈ 3% discordant pairs SE = √(0.03 ÷ 500) ≈ 0.0077; the margin (1.645 + 2.31) × 0.0077 ≈ 3.1 points lets an equal release pass all 10 family-metrics 90% of the time, and catches a 3-point drop ≈ 94% of the time.
2. **The ratchet:** a release 1, 2 or 3 points worse passes 85%, 39% or 6% of the time, so each release is also compared with a fixed anchor release, reset quarterly.
3. **Slices** by language and market warn past a 5-point drop; **red-team regression:** every past successful attack still fails, and 0 of 100 injections produce a false allow.
4. **CSEA and NCII routing:** no CSEA or NCII image reaches the model; a replay of 10,000 known hashes loses none.
5. **Shadow** on live band traffic: action rate per family within ± 10% unless intended; **latency and cost** within N3 and 10%.
6. **Live guard, global:** an hourly replay of 200,000 known-fine units (≈ 0.5 USD an hour) for classifier and edge bugs; at today's 0.01% false-action rate that is Poisson(20), and ≥ 35 actions rolls back. False trips ≈ 0.15% of hours; a 2 × bug across all traffic trips within the hour ≈ 81% of the time, but one confined to German hate only ≈ 0.6%.
7. **Live guard, per slice:** 2,000 known-fine near-miss band units an hour per market × model family (25 slices, ≈ 4.7 USD an hour) go through the model: Poisson(60) at its 3% false-alarm rate, a trip at ≥ 84. Across 25 slices that is ≈ 4.8% false trips an hour, so rollback needs two consecutive hours: ≈ 0.23% false a day, and a 2 × slice bug is still caught ≈ 99.96% of the time within two hours.

- **Launch:** 4 weeks of shadow, one family and one market at a time: held units go to people as in production, 2–3 band units stay limited and the classifier decides, and the model's verdicts are logged and compared with people's on a sample. Then model decisions go live on spam → graphic → hate, one market at a time.

| Insider abuse (bribed restores, data theft) | Review tool | Pool × market access, masked identities, logged views, locked-down desktops, two people for high-profile restores | Code + process |

**Legal duties (as read in November 2026; Kestrel is not a very large platform under the DSA):**

- **US CSEA reporting (18 U.S.C. § 2258A):** report apparent CSAM and, since 2024, online enticement and child sex trafficking to NCMEC "as soon as reasonably possible"; a report is treated as a request to preserve its contents for 1 year (REPORT Act, 2024); there is no duty to scan. Kestrel files ≈ 154 reports a day from ≈ 230 confirmed units, bundled per upload event.
- **UK CSEA reporting (from 7 April 2026):** detected, unreported CSEA goes to the National Crime Agency; content reported to NCMEC need not be reported twice, so one path. The 2026 Regulations' timeframes are not read here: "report per the 2026 Regulations".
- **US TAKE IT DOWN Act (from 19 May 2026):** remove NCII within 48 hours of a valid request and make reasonable efforts to remove known identical copies; up to 53,088 USD per violation. India's IT Rules (2021, secondary source) ask for 24 hours on the depicted person's complaint.
- **EU Terrorist Content Online Regulation (applies from 7 June 2022):** remove within one hour of a removal order; preserve removed content 6 months (secondary source).
- **DSA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065):** a statement of reasons for every restriction, demotion included (Art. 17), submitted to the transparency database (Art. 24(5)); complaints open ≥ 6 months and not decided solely by automation (Art. 20); suspected threats to life to the police (Art. 18). That is ≈ 430,800 EU statements a day.
- **Demotion is a restriction:** limited distribution for ≈ 1.2 s and a hold are treated as precautions that end in a decision, pending legal sign-off. If a regulator disagrees, EU statements rise to ≈ 1.20M a day, and the submission path is sized for that.

## Deep dives

### Thresholds from the cost of each error

- **The rule:** on a calibrated probability, act when P ≥ τ = cfp ÷ (cfp + cfn). For hate, cfp 8 and cfn 6 give τ = 8 ÷ 14 = 0.571.
- **The band comes from the checker's own errors:** the lower edge is where a "yes" lifts the posterior to τ, p·cfn·(1 − fnr) = (1 − p)·fpr·cfp + price, where price is the call's cost in harm units (≈ 0; 0.0001 here, so the verdict sets the edge, not the price); the upper edge is where a "no" drops it to τ.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/thresholds" stepper caption="Each family’s threshold comes from the cost of its two errors; the band is where the model’s verdict can flip the decision, and local law is a separate, market-scoped withhold line." />

- **How to read it:** step 1 is the cost table and τ per family, with each family's path (held or limited); step 2 is hate on one probability axis with τ, the band and the EU withhold line drawn separately; step 3 is the German comment (raw 0.02, folded 0.30, 0.929 after a "yes").

**The numbers:**

| Family | τ | Band (lower – upper) | Local-law withhold (τ, band) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CSEA | 0.0025 | 0.002 → specialists (grooming text: model to 0.039) | none |
| NCII | 0.0050 | 0.01 → specialists | none |
| Terrorism | 0.0099 | 0.003 – 0.140 (above: a person directly) | none |
| Threats and self-harm | 0.0123 | 0.005 – 0.170 | none |
| Hate | 0.571 | 0.0417 – 0.942 | EU 0.333 (0.0161 – 0.858); UK and India 0.400 (0.0213 – 0.890) |
| Graphic | 0.429 | 0.0156 – 0.936 | UK under-18s: an age gate by viewer age, not a threshold |
| Spam | 0.667 | 0.0602 – 0.970 | none |

- Hate's lower edge: (0.03 × 8 + 0.0001) ÷ (6 × 0.92 + 0.24) = 0.0417; upper edge: (8 × 0.97 − 0.0001) ÷ (7.76 + 0.08 × 6) = 0.942.
- **Severity 0–1 bands are set by capacity:** a miss costs 80–400 × a false alarm, so the cost rule would send almost everything to a person. The lower edge is what reviewers can carry, and the whole band is held.
- **Local law:** an automated withhold needs a posterior ≥ 0.8 after the model's "yes", which is a classifier P ≥ 0.115; below that a "yes" goes to trained legal-ground reviewers (general pool with market-law training, 45–90 s an item, illustrative; lawyers on escalation). Result: ≈ 2,510 withholds a day, EU automated precision 92.2%, ≈ 3,050 items a day to the legal queue.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Tune thresholds on a test set where 10% is violating, as the concept page's example does, and apply them where the live rate is 0.2%: precision collapses. Calibrate on live traffic by score bin, in the tails where the edges sit.
</Callout>

### People: an hourly roster with skills, languages and wellbeing

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/review-staffing" stepper caption="People are the cost: about 3,965 handling hours a day by queue, severe work in red, staffed from an hourly roster to about 1,492 FTE; handle times and volumes are illustrative." />

- **How to read it:** step 1 is the handling hours a day by queue, severe work in red; step 2 the hourly roster and its result in full-time staff and US dollars; step 3 the threats capacity curve from Deep dive 1.

**The numbers:**

- Hours a day: appeals 923, threats 873 + 219 from reports, hate 400, terrorism 273, escalated reports 267, CSEA 204, CSEA and NCII reports 120, spam 114, graphic 109, notifier appeals 105, legal notices 90, QA 69, the rest ≈ 200: ≈ 3,965 h (severe ≈ 1,919).
- Urgent pools are staffed per hour with an Erlang C queue model for their p95 wait (specialist hubs ≤ 15 min, threat pools ≤ 10 min): peaks of 20, 12 and 21 in-queue reviewers in the three hubs, 93 across the 16 threat pools.
- General work is staffed at the daily mean ÷ 0.85; the evening backlog peaks at ≈ 341 hours of work and clears in ≈ 3.2 hours, inside 24.
- ≈ 6,282 on-shift hours ÷ 8 × 7 ÷ 5 × 1.15 ≈ 1,264 reviewers, + 18% support roles ≈ 1,492 FTE, at 25 USD an hour loaded ≈ 6.76M USD a month.

- **The 4-hour severe cap costs no extra staff here:** severe reviewers fill their other hours with general work in their language, and non-severe work exceeds their spare hours by ≈ 1,066 h a day. It starts to cost when that slack runs out.
- **Handle times are assumptions:** × 1.5 gives ≈ 1,897 FTE; × 2 gives ≈ 2,303 FTE and ≈ 10.5M USD a month. People on the band instead of the model would need ≈ 7,800 FTE.
- **Wellbeing is router rules, not posters:** exposure counters per person per shift and week, rotation after 4 consecutive severe days, model text descriptions first for graphic images where they suffice, opt-out without penalty.

### Shipping a policy change in a day

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/policy-rollout" stepper caption="A new coded slur is live in about 17.3 hours: a term rule routes every match to the model on day 0, gates and a shadow test it, one market canaries it, and a backfill re-checks 30 days of posts." />

- **How to read it:** step 1 is the ≈ 17.3-hour timeline with the term rule live from hour 0; step 2 the gates on the new items, the shadow and the canary; step 3 the backfill of older matches over 7 days.

**The numbers:**

- Write the rule, the term list and 5 examples 2 h → find 400 new gold items 1 h → experts label and adjudicate 2 h (translations into 13 language groups in parallel) → old and new prompt on the hold-out and new items, ≈ 1 minute for ≈ 0.26 USD.
- Gates: new-rule recall 186 of 200 (0.886–0.958 ≥ 0.85); near-miss fine 190 of 200 (0.910–0.973 ≥ 0.90); the paired hold-out gate.
- Shadow 6 h on ≈ 202,800 calls (≈ 19 USD), ≈ 5,070 disagreements; experts read 300 in 1.25 h and 282 are explained (0.907–0.962 ≥ 0.85).
- Canary 4 h in one market with 5% boosted QA (≈ 137 labels, precision 0.90 within 0.838–0.940: a gross-shift detector only), then all markets: ≈ 17.3 h in all.
- Backfill over 30 days of posts: ≈ 400,000 matches (≈ 37.6 USD), ≈ 240,000 removals over 7 days, highest reach first, no strikes for retroactive removals; it adds ≈ 20,400 appeals (≈ 48.6 h a day for 7 days) and ≈ 73,400 EU statements.

- **Day 0 is a term rule** because a new term scores near zero; reviewers get the guideline and a quiz the same day; appeals are judged under the version in force at the action.
- **What cannot ship in a day:** a new family, a new modality, a new classifier head, or a change of legal ground (legal sign-off).

### Measuring what was missed

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/prevalence" stepper caption="What was missed, on views: an independent labeller reads a uniform sample, people confirm its positives, and each family’s 4-week interval sits under its target; calibration labels per score bin set the band edges. - **How to read it:** step 1 is the sampling path (views → snapshot → CSEA screen → labeller → people confirm); step 2 each family's two-sided 95% interval and one-sided bound on 4 weeks against its N1 target; step 3 the calibration bins at each family's edges.

**The numbers:**

| Family | Point (per 10,000) | N1 bound | Labels a week | Windows that pass | 4-week upper bound (two-sided 95%) | Labels a week to see a 25% rise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spam | 6.04 | 9 | 30,000 | 95.7% | 7.58 (4.62 – 7.86) | ≈ 120,400 |
| Hate | 5.29 | 8 | 35,000 | 97.1% | 6.59 (4.03 – 6.83) | ≈ 137,500 |
| Adult and graphic | 0.54 | 1.5 | 40,000 | 96.0% | 0.96 (0.20 – 1.05) | ≈ 1.35M |
| Threats and self-harm | 0.43 | 1.5 | 30,000 | 95.9% | 1.03 (0.16 – 1.14) | ≈ 1.67M |
| Terrorism | 0.042 | 1.5 | 10,000 | 99.1% | 0.89 (0 – 1.10) | not measurable |

- **Estimate:** confirmed positives ÷ n ÷ detection, where detection = the labeller's sensitivity 0.90 × the confirmers' 0.96 = 0.864; the N1 test is the one-sided 95% exact Poisson bound on the count over n × detection at its lower bound (≈ 0.855). A week holds only ≈ 16 confirmed hate positives, so a weekly test would fail 53–68% of weeks; no sampling gain is assumed.
- **Sensitivity** is measured weekly on live positives found by other routes (confirmed reports, trusted flaggers, upheld appeals). Those are easier than what slips past everything, so prevalence is biased low; at a true sensitivity of 0.75 hate's bound would be 7.82, still ≤ 8 with almost no margin.
- **Views, not units:** 293 viral hate misses carry ≈ 12% of hate's violating views, which is why the reach re-check exists.
- **Cost:** ≈ 145,000 labels a week on 2 labeller GPUs (≈ 120 USD a day) and ≈ 290 human confirmations a day; CSEA and NCII are measured by hash re-scans, never sampled into a model.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  This sample can show hate is below 8 per 10,000; it cannot see a 25% rise inside two 4-week windows without ≈ 4 × the labels (≈ 137,500 a week). A rise is a signal to investigate, not proof of a cause.
</Callout>

### The red-team loop

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/moderation/redteam-loop" stepper caption="Ten attack families of 100 units a month run through the whole cascade; a fix turns 23 of 100 successful homoglyph slurs into 3, and every success becomes a regression test." />

- **How to read it:** step 1 runs the attacks through the whole cascade and measures success per family with its interval; step 2 fixes the cause, re-runs and keeps each success as a regression test, with misses from prevalence, appeals and reports joining each week.

**The numbers:**

- Homoglyph slurs: 23 of 100 succeed (15.8–32.2%) → a confusables-table fix → 3 of 100 (1.0–8.5%).
- At 100 a family, a family at 15% has an interval of 9.3–23.3%, so the loop separates families about 15 points apart; smaller differences need more units.
- The ten families: character tricks, splitting, text in images, image edits, new coded terms (incl. model-paraphrased hate), context flips, instructions to the model, code-switching, account tactics and probing, synthetic media.

### Worked example: a coded slur goes viral on a Monday morning

1. **08:00:** prevalence positives and German appeals show a new coded term; it scores 0.01–0.03 on the hate head (illustrative), below the band, so the classifier allows it.
2. **08:00–13:00:** the policy team writes a term rule and examples; experts label 400 items; both gates pass (186 of 200, 190 of 200).
3. **13:00–20:15:** the new prompt runs in shadow on 6 h of live hate-band and rule-match traffic (≈ 202,800 calls, ≈ 19 USD); experts then explain 282 of 300 sampled disagreements.
4. **20:15–01:15:** a 4-hour canary in Germany with boosted QA, then all markets: live at ≈ 01:15 on Tuesday, ≈ 17.3 h after the start.
5. **Tuesday onward:** the backfill removes ≈ 240,000 posts over 7 days, highest reach first, with no strikes; the classifier is retrained on the new labels in 2–4 weeks and the rule retired.

| If the interviewer asks for… | What stays the same | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Video and livestreams | Cascade, bands, holds, people, prevalence | Video hashes, frame sampling and transcripts into the same classifiers; livestreams need an in-stream check every few seconds and a person on call; GPU minutes dominate |

## How real systems do it

- **2019, Facebook PDQ:** Facebook open-sourced the PDQ photo hash and TMK+PDQF for video; a 2019 test found that crops under 5% can push a PDQ hash past the match threshold and rotations beyond about 5° fall away sharply.
- **2023, Discord:** detects known CSAM with PhotoDNA and open-sourced a model for novel CSAM; people review anything that is not an exact PhotoDNA match, and verified CSAM is reported to NCMEC.
- **2025, OpenAI gpt-oss-safeguard:** text-only open-weight models that reason from a provided policy. Its report says classifiers trained on tens of thousands of labels can still do better, and OpenAI uses smaller classifiers to choose what the reasoner sees; judging several policies at once, no model got every policy right on more than 53% of samples (a joint rate, vendor-run).
- **2025–2026, Meta:** prevalence is the share of views of violating content, reported with 95% windows and an upper limit for rare types; enforcement precision was over 90% on Facebook in Q3 2025, and a bug raised false positives by over 100% in Q4 2025.
- **2026, Pinterest:** a multimodal LLM labels daily samples of impressions for prevalence, ≈ 15 × faster and over 10 × cheaper than people at comparable quality (paper, February 2026).
- **2026, Discord Osprey:** a rules engine whose rules are pushed without a deployment, running 2,288 rules over ≈ 400M actions a day (December 2025).
- **Where this design differs:** the LLM sits on a band defined by its own errors rather than on every flagged item, and holds replace "visible while pending" for the worst harms. Kestrel's share of actions with no person (≈ 97.4%) is above the DSA database's 46% fully automated across all platforms (read November 2026); the two are coded differently, and spam's share of Kestrel's actions is the likely reason.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- **Model provider down, slow or at quota:** text band units go to the self-hosted fallback (8 GPUs, 30% of peak text, its own edges); 2–3 bands narrow to their upper halves; held units keep priority. Noticed by the provider's error rate and queue age.
- **PhotoDNA down or slow:** images publish on local checks with limited distribution; the CSEA image band's lower edge drops to 0.0002; every image from the window is re-checked within 15 minutes of recovery, highest reach first.
- **CSEA classifier down:** image posts fail closed ("processing" for the author); after 30 minutes the image queue pages an on-call engineer and a specialist hub lead.
- **A news event** spikes one family (terrorism and graphic × 10, illustrative: terrorism's ≈ 273 specialist hours a day become ≈ 2,730): hash sharing through GIFCT's incident framework (a member service), term rules in hours, specialist hubs borrow from general pools, QA and calibration pause first.
- **A bad release:** the global replay trips at ≥ 35 actions on 200,000 known-fine units and rolls back; a bug in one market and family trips that slice's replay (≥ 84 on 2,000 band units, two hours running), and ratio guards catch drift.
- **Drift:** score PSI (population stability index) and band volume per family × language alarm; new slurs show up in prevalence positives and appeals before the classifiers see them.
- **Reviewer backlog:** severity order holds; spam, QA and calibration wait; 24-hour targets slip before 30-minute ones.

## Interview framework

1. **Minutes 0 to 5:** content types and volume (140M units, 6.0B views, heavy-tailed), seven families by severity, the cost of each error, where Kestrel is regulated, the order when constraints conflict.
2. **Minutes 5 to 10:** numbers: 1,620 units a second, ≈ 1.41M violating units a day (73% spam), a 150 ms pre-publish budget, ≈ 3.68M model calls against ≈ 1,492 FTE.
3. **Minutes 10 to 20:** Steps 1 to 4: baseline, hashes first, classifiers with one global τ per family, holds for severity 0–1, the model where its verdict can flip the decision.
4. **Minutes 20 to 30:** thresholds and bands with local law (Deep dive 1), then people and the hourly roster (Step 5, Deep dive 2).
5. **Minutes 30 to 38:** Steps 6 and 7: the action ladder, statements of reasons, appeals, legal clocks; prevalence and the gates.
6. **Minutes 38 to 45:** one deep dive (policy change in a day, prevalence, staffing or the red team), then video or 10 × volume.

**What interviewers listen for:**

- **Mid:** hashes, a classifier with a threshold, a review queue, appeals, precision and recall.
- **Senior:** the cascade with shares and prices per tier; thresholds per family from error costs; holds against limited distribution and their latency; one policy per model call; people by severity and language; prevalence on views.
- **Staff:** bands from the checker's own errors; global rules against market-scoped law; capacity as the limit on severity 0–1, signed by policy; calibration in the tails; measurement design and its power; gates sized to pass a good release; legal clocks as code paths; the data boundary around CSEA and NCII.

**Red flags:**

- "The LLM moderates everything"; one threshold for all harms; per-market thresholds on a global removal; thresholds tuned on a 10%-prevalence test set; the model's confidence used as a probability.
- The enforcing model grading its own prevalence; severity 0–1 content visible while it waits; CSAM sent to a third-party model; banking automated removals; automated appeals; reviewers at 100%.

  - Kestrel expects ≈ 55,370 appeals a day and ≈ 33.8% overturned (illustrative; measured at launch); an overturn restores the post, removes the strike, unbanks any hash entry and adds a near-miss to the gold set.
  - The trade-off is ≈ 923 review hours a day, so accounts with 3 or more spam strikes get one appeal a week (in the EU through DSA Art. 23: a warning first, then suspension of manifestly unfounded complaints); a model re-judging appeals would repeat its own error.
