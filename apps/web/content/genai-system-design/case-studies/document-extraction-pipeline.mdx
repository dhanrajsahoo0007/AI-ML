A supplier emails a PDF; a week later Brightpost pays it. In between, someone has to read 37 fields, check that they add up and that the supplier and purchase order are real, and post it once.
A vision-language model reads an invoice in seconds. The hard part is knowing which of its answers to trust:
one wrong total pays the wrong amount, one wrong supplier match pays the wrong party, and one retry can pay twice.

- Interviewers use this question to see whether you get confidence from independent evidence instead of the model's word, ground every field to a page and box, count risk in money, size review in staff hours, and post once against an asynchronous ERP.

## Overview

- A **difficulty router** sends each invoice down one of three routes: **A** born-digital with a known template (55%), **B** a novel layout or a scan of print (40%), **C** handwriting on critical fields, degraded scans or unsupported languages (5%, always reviewed, pre-filled).
- **Schema-constrained extraction** returns every field as transcribed text plus the **ids of the OCR words** it read; code turns ids into a page and box and checks that the words spell the value at the field's label.
- **Confidence comes from independent evidence**: a second reader on a **different OCR engine**, a blind image-only tie-break, rules per field family, and a scorer calibrated on labels the scorer did not choose.
- **Errors are counted honestly**: shared errors by cause, document kind and supplier match in the critical set, and a **money rule** inside the straight-through model that raises the threshold with invoice value.
- **People are the cost**: models and cloud services are ≈ 855 USD a month; review is ≈ 709 hours of work, rostered at ≤ 85% utilisation as 6 permanent clerks and 4 flex at month end, ≈ 35,000 USD a month.
- **Effectively once**: a byte hash at intake, a folded business key with a hard ERP duplicate check, an outbox that waits for the ERP's asynchronous acknowledgement, and a daily reconciliation.

## API design

| Call | Who calls it | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mailbox webhook → `POST /v1/ingest/email` | Mail gateway | Every message to `invoices@`; carries `Message-ID`, SPF, DKIM and DMARC results, attachments |
| `POST /v1/documents` (multipart, `Idempotency-Key`) | Supplier portal (MFA), expense app | Returns `document_ids`, `duplicate_of` and `reopened` |
| `GET /v1/documents/{id}` | Portal, expense app, review app | Status: `received → parsed → extracted → validated → auto_approved`, `in_review` or `held` `→ queued_to_erp → posted` or `rejected` |
| `GET /v1/review/next?queue=ap` | Review app | Next task by due date; locks it for 10 minutes |
| `POST /v1/review/{task_id}/decision` | Review app | Fields with text and word ids, optional coding, `approve`, `reject` or `hold`, and the version read; optimistic lock |
| `POST /v1/documents/{id}/reprocess` | Ops | A new schema, model, OCR or scorer version; never re-posts a posted invoice |
| ERP interface `post(invoice, key)` → acknowledgement event | Poster ↔ ERP | Asynchronous (IDoc or open-interface class): accepted, posted with a document number, or rejected |
| Internal `extract`, `second_read`, `tie_break`, `validate`, `score`, `reconcile_statement` | Workflow | Every document |

Upload returns the document id at once; extraction runs in the workflow.

```http
POST /v1/documents
Idempotency-Key: 7c1e…
file=@harbourline-INV-20417.pdf; source=portal

201 Created
{ "document_ids": ["doc_9f2a"], "sha256": "e3b0…", "duplicate_of": null, "reopened": false, "status": "received" }
```

One field of the extraction output: fixed keys from the schema, absence explicit, word ids as integers on the page's word list.

```json
"gross": { "s": "present", "t": "1,488.00", "w": [311], "d": [] }
```

- **Formats:** PDF (including hybrid PDFs with embedded XML), TIFF, JPEG, PNG, HEIC, Peppol UBL and XRechnung XML; encrypted PDFs, portfolios, archives and files over 50 MB or 300 pages are refused with a reply or an AP task.
- **Isolation:** every file is malware-scanned and rendered in an isolated worker with no network and with CPU and time limits; rasters are regenerated on demand, not stored.
- **Transcribe, do not interpret:** `t` is what is printed; readers are compared on raw text first, then one shared normaliser applies the supplier's locale (`1.488,00` from a German supplier becomes 1488.00).
- **Ambiguous dates are flagged, not guessed:** a day ≤ 12 from a supplier with fewer than 5 invoices of history goes to review.
- **No union types, no nulls:** every field is required with `s` set to `present`, `absent` or `illegible`; `d` lists the source fields of a derived value. Lines are an array, extracted in windows of 5 pages with ≤ 2,000 output tokens a window.
- **No decision fields:** the schema has no `approved`, `bank_account` or `notes` field, so nothing the page says can become a decision.
- **Shape failures handled in code:** a `refusal` or `max_tokens` stop gets one retry with a larger limit, then review; enums are compared case-insensitively.
- **Idempotency at intake:** dedupe by `sha256` and by `Message-ID` + attachment index. A hit on a rejected or held document reopens it (the PO may exist now); a hit on a posted one returns it.
- **Review decisions are versioned:** the later of two approvals of the same version gets `409`. Approvers cannot edit the supplier master (a separate role).

Each field version is one row; the outbox's unique index is the second dedupe layer.

```sql
CREATE TABLE extracted_field (
  document_id  text, field text, version int,
  text         text,               -- as printed: '1,488.00'
  value_norm   text,               -- '1488.00' after the supplier's locale
  status       text,               -- 'present' | 'absent' | 'illegible'
  derived_from text[],             -- source fields of a derived value, else '{}'
  word_ids     int[], page int, box numeric[4],         -- box in the de-rotated page's pixels
  primary_text text, second_text text, tie_text text,   -- raw text from each reader
  rules jsonb, p_correct numeric, versions jsonb,       -- model, schema, OCR, scorer
  decided_by   text,               -- 'auto' | clerk id
  PRIMARY KEY (document_id, field, version)
);
-- business key: supplier id | folded invoice number | numbering year | kind
-- e.g. 'SUP-0193|INV20417|Y2026|invoice'
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX one_posting ON posting_outbox (business_key);
```

- **Access patterns:** words by document and page (grounding, the review highlight); field records by document (review, audit); tasks by queue and due date; outbox rows by business key and by `acknowledged = false` older than 30 minutes; labels by release snapshot.

## Building the architecture, step by step

- We start from one model call per PDF and add a part only when a requirement or a driver forces it; each figure outlines the step's new parts.

### Step 1: One model call per PDF (F1, F3)

The baseline: store the PDF, ask one model for JSON, post the JSON.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={1} caption="Step 1: suppliers send a PDF to the intake API; the orchestrator asks one extractor model for JSON and posts it straight to the ERP, keeping the original by its sha256." />

- **Adds:** the intake API, the workflow orchestrator, the extraction model, the document store, the ERP connection.
- **Flow:**
  1. A PDF arrives by email or portal; the intake stores the original under its sha256.
  2. The orchestrator sends the whole PDF to one vision-language model (VLM: a model that reads page images and text) and gets JSON back.
  3. The JSON is posted to the ERP.
- **Why this and not that:** the simplest pipeline that posts an invoice, and the bar every later part must beat on the gold set (P1).
- **The gap:** a resent email posts twice; one PDF can hold three invoices; a statement or a credit note is paid as an invoice.

### Step 2: Ingest once; classify, split, detect e-invoices (F1, F2)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={2} caption="Step 2: receipts arrive from the expense app, and a page classifier labels every page so one file becomes the right number of documents of the right kind." />

- **Adds:** the expense app, the page classifier; dedupe on sha256 and `Message-ID`; e-invoice detection.
- **Flow:**
  1. The intake hashes the file. Seen before? Posted: return it. Rejected or held: reopen it, because the PO may exist now.
  2. Embedded or attached XML (Peppol, XRechnung, Factur-X) is parsed directly and skips extraction.
  3. Otherwise a small model labels every page with a kind and "continues the previous page"; the splitter cuts the file into documents with their own ids.
- **Why this and not that:** idempotency at the door (P10), and kind is a critical field, because a credit note read as an invoice pays instead of deducting.
- **The gap:** scans have no text; pages arrive rotated; hidden text sits in the text layer.

### Step 3: Parse pages into words with boxes (F3, N6)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={3} caption="Step 3: a sandboxed page parser renders and de-rotates every page and checks it for hidden text; OCR writes every word with an id, a box and a confidence to the pages-and-words store." />

- **Adds:** the page parser, OCR (Azure Read), the pages-and-words store.
- **Flow:**
  1. Render each page in the sandbox; take the page angle from OCR, de-rotate, and correct the perspective of photos.
  2. OCR runs at native resolution (a 300 DPI render for born-digital pages) and returns words with ids, positions and confidence.
  3. The PDF content stream is checked for invisible text: render mode 3, white or background fill, under 2 pt, clipped or off the page. Any hit is removed from the model input and raises an alert.
- **Why this and not that:** ground before you generate (P2), and show the model what a person sees, not what the text layer claims.
- **The gap:** one prompt for every page type: easy pages overpay and hard pages fail.

**The numbers:**

- A page is 350 words × 8 tokens = 2,800 tokens of words, plus a 150 DPI image of 2,835 tokens.
- OCR on two pages in parallel takes the slower of two draws: ≈ 2,076 ms at p50 against 1,500 for one page.

### Step 4: Route by difficulty; extract with word ids (F3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={4} caption="Step 4: a difficulty router sends 55% of invoices to route A, 40% to B and 5% to C, and makes the extraction call with the schema and the supplier’s hints." />

- **Adds:** the difficulty router, the schema registry and supplier profiles.
- **Flow:**
  1. Cheap signals: a text layer, a known template (≥ 5 invoices from the supplier), handwriting on a critical field, mean OCR confidence below 0.80, a language the second reader lacks.
  2. Route A: a small model on the text-layer words. Route B: a small VLM on the 150 DPI image and the words. Route C: a mid-size VLM pre-fills a review task.
  3. The model returns transcribed text and word ids per field; code unions the boxes, checks the words spell the value, and checks the box sits in the region of the field's label.
- **Why this and not that:** route by difficulty (P3), constrain the output (P9), and carry provenance from word ids (P14).
- **The gap:** valid JSON can still hold a wrong total, and a reader on the same OCR repeats its misreads.

The router reads signals that cost milliseconds and prices each route.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/router" stepper caption="Cheap signals send 55% of invoices down a 0.0053 USD path; the expensive readers go only where the page is hard, and route C always ends with a person." />

**How to read it:**

- Left, the five signals, read without a model; middle, the router's rules; right, each route with its two readers and its cost.
- Route C is orange because it never auto-posts: the model and the second reader only pre-fill a person's task.

**The numbers:**

- A: 0.0040 common + 0.00127 = 0.0053 USD an invoice; B: 0.0040 + 0.00183 + 0.02 = 0.0258, or 0.0432 with a tie-break.
- C: 0.0040 + 0.0401 + 0.02 = 0.0641 USD, always followed by a person's review.
- Blended: 0.55 × 0.0053 + 0.40 × 0.0258 + 8.0% × 0.0174 + 0.05 × 0.0641 ≈ 0.0178 USD an invoice, half of it the second reader.

### Step 5: Independent second reader, rules, calibrated score (F4, N1)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={5} caption="Step 5: Textract reads the page on its own OCR; the validator checks both readers’ fields against arithmetic and the ERP, and the scorer turns the evidence into a probability per field." />

- **Adds:** the second reader (Textract AnalyzeExpense), the validator, the confidence scorer, the field records.
- **Flow:**
  1. On B and C, Textract reads the page with its own OCR; on A, the template's anchors are the second opinion.
  2. Compare raw text and box overlap per field; on B, a disputed field goes to a blind mid-size read of the image only.
  3. Rules per family: amounts, kind, parties (HMRC and VIES, master, sender domain), PO, invoice-number pattern, dates.
  4. The scorer gives P(correct) per field against its family threshold; value caps, supplier ambiguity and fraud holds apply; the rest auto-approves.
- **Why this and not that:** cascade from cheap to expensive (P4); the rules are code, not a prompt (P10). The Harbourline invoice in the first deep dive walks one disputed total through this step.
- **The gap:** uncertain invoices have nowhere to go, and an ERP that posts later makes a retry dangerous.

### Step 6: Review queue and effectively-once posting (F5, F6, N3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={6} caption="Step 6: flagged invoices go to the review queue and the clerks’ review app; auto-approved and approved ones go to the poster, which posts each once and waits for the ERP’s acknowledgement." />

- **Adds:** the review queue, the review app, the poster (outbox).
- **Flow:**
  1. A flagged invoice becomes a task ordered by due date; the clerk sees the flagged fields beside the highlighted boxes.
  2. The clerk fixes fields (and codes non-PO lines only for new suppliers or low-confidence coding); corrections are re-validated.
  3. Approval and the outbox row commit in one transaction; the poster sends the business key and waits for the ERP's acknowledgement.
  4. It resends only on an explicit rejection, or after the 30-minute interface window and a status read; a daily job reconciles.
- **Why this and not that:** keep a human escape hatch (P15), and make posting idempotent on a business key, not on a request id.
- **The gap:** nobody learns from corrections, and nobody measures what auto-posting gets wrong.

### Step 7: Close the loop with labels the scorer did not choose (F7, N1, N2)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={7} caption="Step 7: corrections flow from the field records into labels and calibrator training, which feeds the 2,000-invoice gold set of double-keyed labels." />

- **Adds:** the label store and calibrator training, the gold set.
- **Flow:**
  1. Labels come from corrections (down-weighted, because the scorer chose them and the clerk saw the model's answer), 1,500 blind audits a month, a random 2% of auto-approved invoices and threshold-band samples.
  2. Each label carries its selection probability, so the calibrator weights it by one over that probability.
  3. A weekly calibrator candidate is gated like a release; supplier hints and templates are updated from corrections.
- **Why this and not that:** close the loop (P15) and evaluate continuously (P16); a scorer calibrated only on what it sent to review learns nothing about what it let through.
- **The gap:** personal data still reaches vendors' prompts, and releases are unchecked.

Calibration is checked at the thresholds, where it decides, not on average.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/calibration" stepper caption="One family’s band labels cannot convict the scorer, so the alarm pools the whole band: 11 errors where 5.5 are expected fires 2.4% of quarters by chance and 93% of the time when the band is three times worse." />

**How to read it:**

- Left: observed accuracy against predicted P(correct) at each family threshold; the whisker is how far a calibrated scorer can wander with this many labels.
- Right: the pooled band. The lime curve is a calibrated scorer's error count; the red one a band three times worse; the dashed line is the alarm.

**The numbers:**

- Band labels: 27,525 auto-approved invoices a month × 11 critical fields × 2% in the band × 10% sampled × 3 months ≈ 1,817 a quarter.
- At ≈ 0.3% predicted wrong that is ≈ 5.5 expected errors; the alarm at ≥ 11 has a 2.4% false-alarm rate and catches a 3× worse band 93% of the time.
- Per family the interval is wide: amounts at 0.995 can show as low as 0.989 by chance, which is why no single family's band can convict the scorer.

### Step 8: Data boundary, guardrails, release gate (N5, N6)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/architecture" step={8} caption="Step 8: a release gate reads the gold set and the calibrator’s weekly candidate and gates the extractor; every model, prompt, schema, OCR or scorer change passes it." />

- **Adds:** the evaluation and release gate; bank-detail masking and the data boundary (enforced in code, not drawn).
- **Flow:**
  1. IBAN, sort-code and account-number patterns are blanked in the image and the word list before any model call.
  2. Model, OCR and second reader run under zero data retention: OCR in Azure UK South, Textract in London, the model through an EU cross-Region endpoint.
  3. The poisoned set, the classifier's confusion matrix and a paired replay gate each model, prompt, schema, OCR or scorer change.
- **Why this and not that:** keep sensitive data inside a boundary (P17) and treat documents as untrusted (P13).
- **The gap:** none; the gate decides each release (details under Evaluation and release gates).

## Component choices and why

- Every box in the final diagram: what we picked, why, and why the usual alternatives lose. Driver ids (G) refer to the drivers table; F and N to the requirements.

### Reading the page: OCR words with positions plus a 150 DPI image

- **Choice:** our OCR words (ids, boxes, confidence) and a 150 DPI copy of the page go to a VLM; route A sends words only.
- **Why:** words give exact digits, ids and boxes to check against (G8); the image gives layout on scans (G1, G11).
- **Why not a text-only LLM everywhere:** it loses layout on scans; kept for route A, where the text layer is exact.
- **Why not image-only end to end:** no word ids, so nothing to check a transcription against.
- **Why not model-drawn boxes:** possible on our own rasters but imprecise and not tied to text; the test that would change this is box IoU ≥ 0.9 against OCR boxes on the gold set.

### Difficulty router on cheap signals

- **Choice:** rules over the text layer, a template fingerprint (a layout hash of label positions after ≥ 5 invoices), handwriting on critical fields, mean OCR confidence and language.
- **Why:** milliseconds, and 55 / 40 / 5 puts most invoices on a 0.0053 USD path (P3, G4, G11).
- **Why not the mid model everywhere:** + ≈ 1,851 USD a month; it pays only for ≥ 11 STP points that remove clerks, or 43% fewer escapes.
- **Why not a learned router first:** rules are auditable until labels show misroutes.

### Page classifier and splitter

- **Choice:** our small model on each page's words: kind and "continues the previous page", 0.0003 USD a page.
- **Why:** kind is critical (G2); it is checked by rules (title, sign of totals, a credit note must reference an invoice) and gated by a confusion matrix on 300 files.
- **Why not Azure's classifier (3 USD per 1,000 pages) or Google's splitter (5):** similar price, less control over the credit-note rule; kept as the fallback.
- **Why not leave splitting to the extraction call:** Google's invoice parser processes only the first invoice in a file (its docs, as of November 2026), which is why splitting is its own step.

### Extraction model: small by default, mid where errors are

- **Choice:** a small VLM on routes A and B; a mid-size VLM for route C pre-fill and tie-breaks.
- **Why:** transcription against a schema is within a small model's reach; the mid model's 0.0401 USD a call goes only where the page is hard (G4).
- **Why not fine-tune at launch:** hints from corrections are cheaper and reversible. Adopt a fine-tuned small VLM after 6 months (≈ 24,000 corrected invoices) only if route B's shared error and flags both fall ≥ 20% on the gold set.

### Output: schema-constrained, all fields required, transcribed text plus word ids

- **Choice:** constrained decoding against a versioned schema; `s` = present, absent or illegible instead of nullable unions; lines as an array in 5-page windows.
- **Why:** shape and types are guaranteed (G8, P9); the provider's grammar allows 16 union-typed parameters, which a nullable-everything invoice exceeds.
- **What it does not give:** correct values; and a refusal or a `max_tokens` stop can still break the shape, so code handles both.
- **Why not provider citations:** incompatible with structured outputs, page-level, and nothing on scans.

### Grounding: word ids checked in code

- **Choice:** each field names its OCR words; code unions their boxes, checks the normalised words equal the value, and checks the box lies in the region of the field's label (template anchor or layout key-value).
- **Why:** "Total" and "Amount due" printing the same number cannot swap (G8, P14). A VLM value that differs from its cited word is a scorer feature, because the OCR may be the one that is wrong.
- **Why not the model's page number:** a page is not a box, and a clerk needs the box highlighted.

### Second reader and tie-break: different inputs, not a second opinion on the same words

- **Choice:** Textract AnalyzeExpense on its own OCR (London, 0.01 USD a page) on B and C; template anchors on A; on B, a blind mid-size read of the image only for disputed fields (8.0% of invoices). Agreement means the same raw text and overlapping boxes.
- **Why:** a different OCR engine and model family rarely repeats a misread (G1, N1); the tie-break is independent of both OCR streams and ≈ 223 USD a month cheaper than tie-breaking every B invoice (P4).
- **Why not Azure's prebuilt invoice model:** it runs on the same Read engine as our words, so OCR errors would be shared; kept as the outage fallback with a stricter threshold.
- **Why not two samples of one model:** they share mistakes.
- **Why not the model's self-reported confidence:** verbalised confidence is overconfident.
- **Why not a third model family for the tie-break:** better for correlation, at a second vendor's cost and contract; the measured tie-break error decides (2% assumed; at 5% the escape rises 0.097% → 0.106%).

### Confidence scorer: per field family, calibrated on labels it did not choose

- **Choice:** gradient-boosted trees with isotonic calibration (Platt scaling is the parametric alternative) over agreement, box IoU, both engines' OCR confidence, grounding checks, rules, route, template match, supplier history and the vendors' own scores.
- **Thresholds (illustrative P(correct)):** amounts 0.995; kind, parties and invoice number 0.998; PO and dates 0.997.
- **Why:** a threshold means something only if P(correct) is calibrated where it decides (G1, P16).
- **Why not a vendor's confidence alone:** it describes the vendor's model, not our pipeline; it is one input.
- **Why not token log-probabilities:** not every API we call exposes them; they would be one more feature.

### Rules per field family, in code, against the ERP at validation time

- **Choice:** amounts (Σ lines = net within 1p a line, quantity × price, net + VAT = gross, VAT per rate, PO price and goods received); kind; parties (HMRC or VIES, master, sender domain, remit-to); PO; invoice-number pattern and sequence; dates.
- **Jurisdiction:** country from the VAT prefix; EU supplies to Brightpost UK are usually reverse-charged; a foreign-currency UK VAT invoice must show VAT in sterling; a simplified invoice is valid only up to £250.
- **Why:** the ERP changes daily (G6) and a check must be deterministic (P10). Catch rates differ: amounts 95%, invoice number 60%.
- **Why not ask the model to check sums:** format restrictions hurt reasoning, and a check that varies run to run is not a check.

### Supplier entity resolution

- **Choice:** VAT number first, then name, sender domain, remit-to address and the PO's supplier; one candidate above threshold, or the invoice is held (1.5% of invoices).
- **Why:** bank details come from the master, so a match to the wrong record pays the wrong party (G2).
- **Why not name matching alone:** "Harbourline Packaging Ltd" and "Harbourline Packaging (Scotland) Ltd".

### Hidden-text detector and bank-detail masking

- **Choice:** the PDF content stream first (render mode 3, background fill, under 2 pt, clipped, off-page), an OCR diff second; bank-detail patterns blanked before any model call.
- **Why:** invoices are written by outsiders (G2, P13); the model never needs bank details, so it cannot be talked into changing them.
- **Why not only "text-layer words OCR cannot see" above a 20-word threshold:** a 6-word payload passes.

### Build or buy

- **Choice:** build the control plane (rules against our ERP, calibration on our labels, review, posting once); buy the reading (OCR and the second reader are already bought).
- **Why not a managed generative extractor as the whole answer:** Azure Content Understanding (≈ 0.0084 USD a page in Microsoft's illustrative example) or Bedrock Data Automation (≈ 0.087 USD an invoice at 37 fields) still need our control plane. Either is a valid primary reader if it passes our gates; its confidence becomes one scorer input.
- **Why not an AP-automation product:** it brings its own control plane, so it is a real alternative. It wins below ≈ 0.72 USD an invoice (our engineering, models and infrastructure minus 0.5 FTE of integration) if it passes our gold set, audit and UK processing.

### Workflow, posting and timing

- **Workflow:** a durable workflow engine resumes after crashes and queues bursts (G12). Why not a queue and stateless workers: partial progress is lost.
- **Posting:** outbox + business key + the ERP's hard duplicate check + acknowledgement + daily reconciliation (G2, F6). Why not read-before-retry alone: an asynchronous ERP can say "not found" for a document it will post later.
- **Timing:** real time in production; Batch only for offline replays. Batch would save ≈ 120 USD a month, but it keeps data 29 days outside ZDR and can take 24 hours (N5).

| Multi-document files | 300 | Including 60 credit notes and 30 statements | Kind and splitting |

- **Match rules per family:** amounts exact to the penny; ids exact after removing spaces and punctuation; dates the exact day; parties by resolved supplier and company code; kind exact; non-PO coding by GL account and cost centre. Intervals by invoice-level bootstrap.
- **The error model (every rate illustrative, measured per route and cause on the gold set):** per critical field, p1 is the primary's error and p2 the second reader's; "shared" is the share of the primary's errors the second reader repeats with the same wrong value.
- **Shared error by cause:** B = OCR misreads 40% × 0.15 (different engines) + field assignment 45% × 0.15 + normalisation 15% × 0.5 = **0.2025**; A (no OCR) = assignment 80% × 0.15 + normalisation 20% × 0.5 = **0.22**.
- **Steady rates:** A p1 0.4%, p2 0.6%; B p1 1.0%, p2 1.5%; the tie-break resolves 70% of disputes, 2% of them wrongly. Rules catch amounts 95%, kind 95%, parties 90%, PO 90%, invoice number 60%, dates 70%.
- **The assumption launch STP hangs on:** a threshold that catches a share c of what is left flags 0.002 × (c ÷ (1 − c))² of correct agreements. Shadow mode measures this curve before anything auto-posts.
- **The money rule is in the model:** invoice values are log-normal (median £1,200, σ = 1.2) in 40 bands; caps first (any over £50,000, non-PO over £5,000: 3.6% of invoices); then each band and route gets the lowest threshold whose escape × loss ≤ 0.36 USD, or is reviewed. 84% of A and B invoices stay at the floor.
- **The floor is solved,** not chosen: the lowest threshold whose escape meets the audit budget below. The 4.2% of auto-approved invoices sampled for labels before posting do not count as STP.

| Result | Steady (12 months) | Launch (A 35%, primary errors × 2) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor (share the score catches) | 0.50 | 0.785 (flags ≈ 2.7% of correct agreements) |
| Invoices with a flagged field | 10.7% | 34.9% |
| Reviewed by a cap or the money rule | 3.6% | 3.6% |

| Auto-approved before sampling | 68.8% | 47.0% |

- **What the audit can show:** 1,500 uniformly drawn blind re-keys a month (4,500 a quarter) decide N1. The quarter passes while k ≤ 7, because the 95% bound at k = 7 is 13.15 ÷ 4,500 = 0.292%; a 90% pass needs a true escape ≤ ≈ 0.1035%, the budget the floor is solved to.
- **Value is a separate sample:** 500 audits a month drawn with probability proportional to value estimate the value-weighted escape and are never pooled into the count bound. 10% of audits are double-keyed and adjudicated.
- **Per-route stop rule (own bound over 0.6%):** route B (≈ 1,900 audits a quarter) is wrongly stopped in ≈ 8% of quarters; route A almost never. Route C never auto-posts.

- **The design holds N1 by spending STP,** never by auto-posting on evidence it cannot afford. At a fixed threshold STP rises as shared error rises (fewer disputes), so the **disagreement rate per route and supplier** is the leading indicator: STP up and disagreement down with no supplier change triggers extra audits.
- **Ablations:** one reader plus rules at the steady floor escapes ≈ 0.27%, 2.6× the budget; a tie-break wrong on 5% instead of 2% moves 0.097% to 0.106%.

One failing check blocks the release, however good the others look.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/release-gate" caption="A credit note read as an invoice pays instead of deducting, so kind has its own gate: one of 90 blocks the release, however good the accuracy numbers look." />

**How to read it:**

- A release candidate (here a new page classifier) fans out to seven checks on the gold set, the multi-document files and the poisoned set; six pass and one fails, so the release is blocked. The measured values drawn are illustrative.

**The numbers:**

1. **Non-inferiority on invoice-level critical accuracy, paired on 2,000 invoices:** with ≈ 2% discordant pairs the SE is √(0.02 ÷ 2,000) ≈ 0.32 points, so the margin is δ ≈ 0.93 points. An equal release passes 90% of the time; a drop of δ is stopped 95% of the time, a drop of 0.41 points only 50%.
2. **Replayed escapes, paired:** new − old ≤ 2 on ≈ Poisson(1) discordant escapes each; an equal release passes 96%.
3. **Kind and splitting:** 0 of 90 credit notes and statements posted as invoices, which bounds the rate at 3 ÷ 90 = 3.3% (rule of three); page-split accuracy ≥ 99%; non-PO coding accuracy ≥ 98%.
4. **Calibration** at each family threshold within its interval.
5. **Poisoned documents:** 0 of 200 change a posted value or bank detail (a per-attempt bound of 1.5%, not a guarantee against an attacker who retries).
6. **p95 per route** within N3; cost per invoice within 10%.
7. **STP on the replay** not lower by more than 1 point, paired (McNemar).

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Gates 1 and 2 are smoke tests: they pass a release as good as today with high probability, but a release that doubles escapes passes gate 2 most of the time. The power has to come from the canary.
</Callout>

- **The canary:** every release (model, prompt, schema, OCR or scorer, including weekly scorer retrains) runs on 10% of suppliers for 2 weeks: ≈ 2,000 invoices and ≈ 166 route-B disputes, so a 30% rise in the disagreement rate is detected about 99% of the time.
- **Canary audits:** 1,000 boosted blind audits stop the canary at k ≥ 3 (a false stop 7% of the time), but catch a doubling of escapes only about 30% of the time. The disagreement alarm is the tripwire; the quarterly audit is the backstop.
- **Online:** STP, flag rate and disagreement rate per route and supplier; per-clerk catch rate on known-error canaries in 1% of tasks; the band alarm; cost and p95 per route.
- **Launch rollout:** 4 weeks of shadow (every invoice reviewed, the model pre-fills, the flag curve and weak-family catch rates measured, 3,000 would-be auto-posts re-keyed blind) → route A → route B → all suppliers.

## Safety and guardrails

- **Money at risk (illustrative):** one escape costs ≈ 2 h of recovery (60 USD) + 5% of the value never recovered. £1,488 → ≈ 157 USD, so the allowed Σ(1 − P(correct)) is ≈ 0.23%; £5,000 → 385 USD → 0.093%; £20,000 → 1,360 USD → 0.026%.
- **Caps:** non-PO over £5,000 and any invoice over £50,000 reviewed; two approvers over £100,000. Expected escape loss ≈ 4,300 USD a month, each value band at its own loss.

## Deep dives

### Confidence you can threshold: independent readers, rules and a calibrated score

- **Shape is not correctness:** constrained decoding guarantees valid JSON and types; a vendor's own docs say to validate values in the application anyway.
- **Self-reported confidence is overconfident,** so the model's own number is never the threshold.
- **Agreement is worth what the shared-error rate says:** two readers on the same OCR agree on its misread. Readers must differ at the input: a different OCR engine, and a tie-break that sees only the image.
- **Rules differ by family:** amounts are caught 95% of the time by arithmetic, invoice numbers only 60% by pattern and sequence, so ids and dates drive the escapes that are left.

### The review queue in staff hours

- **The review times are assumptions** measured in the review app: field review 30 s + 12 s per flagged field, route C and value review 162 s, a business exception 240 s, keying a failure 360 s.
- **Exceptions are not extraction:** 213 of the 709 hours are missing POs and goods receipts that wait on buyers, timed from the blocking record's arrival.
- **Sized at ≤ 85%:** a queue run near 100% has no p95, and one clerk off sick at month end must still fit.
- **Anchoring is measured:** known-error canaries in 1% of tasks give each clerk's catch rate.

**The numbers:**

- Normal day: 1,625 invoices × 50.8 s + 6.9 h of flat work (audits, samples, receipts, mail, statements) ≈ 29.8 h → 6 clerks (83%).
- Month end: 2,800 invoices → 46.4 h → 10 clerks (77%; 86% with one off sick). Launch: 37.5 h → 8 and 59.8 h → 12.
- Roster: 6 permanent + 4 flex for 5 days = 1,168 paid hours ≈ 35,040 USD a month, against ≈ 21,300 USD of work; the difference (≈ 13,800) is the slack that keeps p95 finite.
- One STP point = 1% × 40,000 × 50.7 s ≈ 5.6 h; a permanent clerk carries 21 × 5.1 ≈ 107 h of demand, so savings arrive in steps of ≈ 19 STP points (≈ 5,040 USD a month).

### Ingest once, post once, against an asynchronous ERP

- **"Effectively once"** is at-least-once delivery deduplicated at three layers: a byte hash at intake, a folded business key in the outbox, and the ERP's own hard duplicate check (configured as an error, not a warning).
- **Why read-before-retry fails:** an asynchronous ERP interface accepts a document and posts it later, so a status read inside that window can say "not found" for a document it is about to post.
- **The numbering year:** INV-001 of January 2027 is a new invoice, so the key carries the year counted from the supplier's numbering reset month. The year comes from the date only if the date passes its threshold; otherwise the invoice is held.
- **A near-duplicate hold that does not depend on the date:** same supplier and gross within ± 60 days and the same folded number, a page-1 perceptual-hash match, or the same PO with identical lines (0.5% of invoices).

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/doc-extract/posting-sequence" stepper caption="At-least-once delivery, deduplicated three times: a byte hash at the door, a folded business key in the outbox, and the ERP’s own duplicate check; a timeout never triggers a resend inside the interface window." />

**How to read it:**

- Six lanes from the supplier to the ERP, in four steps: a duplicate upload, an approval with its outbox row, a timeout followed by a late acknowledgement, and a copy printed with a letter O.

**The numbers:** three dedupe layers; a 30-minute wait, the interface's maximum processing time, before any status read; the near-duplicate hold stops 0.5% of invoices, ≈ 200 a month.

### Worked example: a timeout, a late acknowledgement and a copy

The Harbourline invoice again, after review; the times are illustrative.

1. 09:00: INV-20417.pdf arrives by email; sha256 is new, so the intake creates `doc_9f2a`. 09:05: the same file through the portal returns `duplicate_of: doc_9f2a`.
2. 10:12: a clerk approves version 3; the approval and the outbox row commit in one transaction. A second approval of version 3 gets `409`.
3. 10:12: the poster sends key `SUP-0193|INV20417|Y2026|invoice`; the ERP does not reply within the call timeout.
4. 10:12–10:42: no resend, because the interface window is 30 minutes. 10:19: the acknowledgement arrives with the ERP document number; the outbox row is marked posted.
5. 11:30: the supplier sends a copy printed `INV-2O417` with a letter O. New bytes create `doc_a71c`, but the number folds to `INV20417`, the same key; the near-duplicate rule holds it for a person.
6. 23:00: the daily reconciliation matches every approved outbox row to one ERP document; a row with none, or two, goes to the AP lead.

## How real systems do it

- **2025, Uber (its engineering blog):** TextSense runs ingestion, splitting, OCR, LLM extraction, business rules and a side-by-side review UI over invoices in 25+ languages. An off-the-shelf model beat fine-tuned Llama 2 and Flan-T5, whose fine-tunes hallucinated on line items.
- **2025, Uber's results:** 90% overall accuracy, 35% of invoices at 99.5% accuracy, and a 2× reduction in manual invoicing, all self-reported with no volume given.
- **2024, Ramp (published by Modal, its infrastructure vendor):** an LLM turns OCR output into JSON for receipts, with 34% fewer receipts needing manual work.
- **2024–2025, managed review retired:** Google deprecated Document AI's human-in-the-loop in January 2024, and Amazon A2I closed to new customers, so teams build their own review queues.
- **2025, the clouds ship generative extraction:** Bedrock Data Automation went GA in March 2025 and reached London in July 2025; Azure Content Understanding's GA API is `2025-11-01`. Both return confidence and grounding per field.
- **2026, injection reaches invoices:** a November 2026 write-up showed a supplier PDF with white 1-point text claiming new bank details make a naive pipeline record the attacker's IBAN; the secure pipeline found 70 invisible words and held it.
- **Where this design differs, and why that is fine:** Uber can accept 90% accuracy because a person reviews every invoice; we auto-post, so we add an independent reader, an unbiased audit and per-route stop rules. Our ≈ 6.3× fewer hours against Uber's 2× rests on one jurisdiction, 70% PO-backed invoices and templates; without them STP falls towards the launch figure.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- **The second reader is down:** Azure's prebuilt model on the same OCR becomes the fallback, with shared error ≈ 0.5, so the floor rises to 0.69. STP falls 65.9% → 58.2% and field flags rise 10.7% → 20.2%: ≈ 4.7 h more on a month-end day (46.4 → 51.1 h, 85% of 10 clerks).
- **OCR or both readers down:** route A continues on the text layer; B uses Textract's words with the same stricter threshold. With both down everything is reviewed pre-filled, ≈ 126 h on a month-end day, so intake queues and the close date is the escalation, never unchecked posting.
- **The ERP interface is slow or down:** approvals wait in the outbox; nothing resends inside the 30-minute window; the daily reconciliation catches any document posted twice or never.
- **A supplier changes layout or a scanner degrades** (a toner streak turns every 8 into 3 on one engine): the fingerprint moves the supplier from A to B, the per-supplier disagreement rate alerts, the second engine disagrees on the digits, and the line sum catches amounts.
- **Long files** (a 40-page invoice, a 300-page statement): 5-page windows with headers carried forward; Σ lines = net catches dropped or doubled rows; the second reader stops at 10 pages and field review covers the rest.
- **Calibration or reviewer drift:** the band alarm, the random 2% slice and per-clerk canaries notice it; a scorer retrain is gated like any release.
- **A schema or rule change** (a new field, a new VAT rate): a new version; the gold set is relabelled for that field first; old documents keep their version.

## Interview framework

1. **Minutes 0 to 5:** document types, volume and the month-end peak, the 37 fields and the 11 that gate posting, the cost of an error, UK processing, the constraint order.
2. **Minutes 5 to 10:** numbers: 114,000 pages a month, 2,800 invoices on a month-end day, a 13,170-token call, ≈ 0.018 USD an invoice against a ≈ 35,000 USD roster.
3. **Minutes 10 to 20:** Steps 1 to 4: baseline, ingest once and split, words with positions, the router and the schema with word ids.
4. **Minutes 20 to 30:** Step 5 and the confidence deep dive: an independent reader, rules per family, a calibrated scorer; the escape, audit and shared-error arithmetic.
5. **Minutes 30 to 36:** Steps 6 to 8: review at ≤ 85%, posting once, labels, the data boundary and the gates.
6. **Minutes 36 to 45:** one deep dive (confidence, staffing, or posting once), build or buy, then 10M documents.

**What interviewers listen for:**

- **Mid:** OCR + layout + a schema, validation rules, a review queue, a hash for duplicates.
- **Senior:** routes priced per path, grounding by word ids at the label, readers independent at the input, rules per family, review in staff hours, the outbox and the business key.
- **Staff:** shared error by cause and the curve that spends STP to hold N1, an audit sized to pass, gates and a canary with stated power, risk in money, the asynchronous ERP, build the control plane and buy the reading.

- **Red flags:** "the LLM extracts and we post"; thresholding on the model's own confidence; "structured outputs guarantee correctness"; a second opinion on the same OCR called independent; bank details read from the invoice; read-before-retry against an asynchronous ERP; review staffed at 100%.

  <InterviewQuestion question="OCR plus an LLM, or a vision model end to end?" category="Trade-offs" level="Mid">
  - Both, on different routes: OCR words with ids and boxes plus a 150 DPI image to a VLM on scans (route B); words only on born-digital invoices with a known template (route A).
  - Words give exact digits and boxes to check a transcription against; the image gives layout. Image-only output has no word ids, so there is nothing to ground against.
  - The cost: a page is 2,835 image tokens + 2,800 word tokens, and route B's call is 0.00183 USD against 0.00127 for words only.
  - It fails when the VLM copies a misread OCR word, as in the `1,438.00` total; the second reader on a different OCR engine catches it. Model-drawn boxes would replace OCR only if they reach IoU ≥ 0.9 against OCR boxes on the gold set.
  </InterviewQuestion>
  <InterviewQuestion question="The schema guarantees valid JSON. Why is that not enough?" category="Concepts" level="Junior">
  - Constrained decoding guarantees shape and types, not values: `"gross": "1,438.00"` is perfectly valid JSON and pays the wrong amount.
  - Even the shape has exceptions: a refusal or a `max_tokens` stop can break it, so code retries once with a larger limit, then sends the invoice to review.
  - Values are checked elsewhere: a second reader, rules per family (amounts are caught 95% of the time, invoice numbers only 60%), grounding to OCR words, and a calibrated score.
  - The trade-off: transcribed text and explicit `absent` or `illegible` statuses mean code does all normalising. Verify with field-level accuracy per family on the 2,000-invoice gold set, not with a parse-success rate.
  </InterviewQuestion>
  <InterviewQuestion question="How do you prove where every extracted value came from?" category="Architecture" level="Mid">
  - The model cites OCR word ids for each field; code unions their boxes into a page and box, checks the words spell the value, and checks the box sits in the region of the field's label.
  - Provider citations cannot do this: they are incompatible with structured outputs, give page numbers, and cite nothing on scans.
  - Each field record keeps the text, word ids, page, box, every reader's value, the rules, P(correct), the model, schema, OCR and scorer versions and who approved; ≈ 1.72M field rows a month, kept 6 years + the current year.
  - The trade-off: OCR runs on every page, even where the VLM alone would read it. It fails when "Total" and "Amount due" print the same number, which the label-region check catches; verify grounding on the gold set's boxes.
  </InterviewQuestion>
