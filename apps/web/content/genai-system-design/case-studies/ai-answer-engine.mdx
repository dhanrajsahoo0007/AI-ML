Design a search box that returns a short written answer with a numbered source after every sentence.
The corpus is the web: someone else's pages, changing by the minute, some written to fool you, and more of them carrying rules about AI use.
The hard parts are where documents come from, which passages reach the model, making every citation true, and knowing when one pass is not enough.

- Interviewers use this question to see whether you budget latency to the first verified sentence per path, treat a citation as something to check, and can price the build-or-buy choice for the index honestly.

## Overview

- A **query planner** (a small model) classifies the question, rewrites it into 3 searches and picks a route: **quick** (80% of questions), **multi-hop** (5%) or **research** (1% direct).
- **Own the head, rent the tail**: an own index of 2 billion pages cut into **20 billion spans** (passages of about 200 tokens) serves every quick answer; one search-API call joins on the 25% flagged as long-tail or breaking.
- The index is a latency, control, freshness and supplier-risk choice, not a cost one: against an API-only design sending 3 calls an answer it breaks even at about 2.6 USD per 1,000 calls; against 1 call, at 8.1 USD.
- **Spans, not pages**: a funnel of 600 → 400 → 300 → 60 → 12 spans sends 2,700 tokens to the model instead of 16,000 for 8 whole pages.
- **Publisher rules become per-host flags** enforced at retrieval: robots.txt, Content Signals, RSL licences and snippet rules; an opted-out host is filtered at once and its cached answers evicted within 1 h.
- **Cite while writing, check before showing**: each sentence passes a support check, a harm screen and a copy check counted across the whole answer; quality is graded by an **independent** grader, never by the checks that filter.

## Estimates and decision drivers

- This page defines its own generic answer engine: every volume, mix, rate and unit cost below is illustrative unless it names a source.
- Prices are list prices as of November 2026: a mid-size model (Claude Sonnet 5.5 class) 2.00 USD per million input tokens, 0.10 cached, 2.50 to write the 5-minute cache, 10.00 output.
- A small model (Claude Haiku 5.5 class) costs 0.10 input, 0.01 cached, 0.125 cache write and 0.50 output; the minimum cacheable prefix is 512 tokens. Search APIs list at 5 USD per 1,000 calls, with a 1 USD fast tier.
- Parallel stages are budgeted as the slowest of n draws, at p50 and p95, not as one call.

## API design

| Call | Who calls it | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/answers` | Web and app client | Every question; `mode` is `auto`, `quick` or `research`; `thread_id` for follow-ups; the response is a Server-Sent Events (SSE) stream |
| SSE events `plan`, `sources`, `sentence`, `status`, `done`, `error` | Gateway → client | `sources` at ≈ 470 ms on the own path; one `sentence` per checked sentence; `status` during multi-hop and research |
| `POST /v1/research` → `202 { job_id }` | Client | Research runs as a durable workflow |
| `GET /v1/research/{id}/events` | Client | Progress and the report; a reconnect resumes from the last event id |
| `GET /v1/answers/{id}` | Client, shared links | Private to the asker unless shared; ids are 128-bit random |
| `POST /v1/answers/{id}/feedback` | Client | Thumbs, `wrong_citation` (sentence and chip), `outdated` |
| `POST /v1/takedowns`, `POST /v1/delisting` | Publishers, people (web forms) | A copyright takedown; delisting of results about a person; both feed the purge path |
| Internal `search`, `fetch`, `rank`, `verify`, `host_flags(host)` | Orchestrator, research agents | Every answer |

The answer endpoint streams sources first, then one event per sentence that passed its checks.

```http
POST /v1/answers
Idempotency-Key: 6f0c2a…
Accept: text/event-stream
{ "question": "Is HTTP/3 faster than HTTP/2 on mobile?", "thread_id": null, "mode": "auto", "locale": "en-GB" }

200 OK
event: plan      data: {"freshness": "evergreen", "route": "quick", "intent": "informational", "queries": 3}
event: sources   data: {"sources": [{"n": 1, "url": "https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9114", "title": "RFC 9114", "published": "2022-06"}, …]}
event: sentence  data: {"i": 0, "text": "HTTP/3 carries HTTP over QUIC, which runs on UDP rather than TCP.", "cites": [1, 2]}
event: done      data: {"answer_id": "a_91c", "dropped_sentences": 1, "low_confidence": false}
```

- **Sources before sentences:** a sentence may cite only numbers already sent in `sources`; links come only from `sources`.
- **Plain text plus chips:** the client strips model-written URLs, markdown links and images, so an injected span cannot make the client fetch `evil.example/?q=…`.
- **Nothing is retracted:** a sentence is sent only after its checks pass; a dropped sentence is never shown.
- **Idempotency:** the `Idempotency-Key` on `/v1/answers` and `/v1/research` returns the same stream or job on a retry. A research run is charged to quota at start and refunded if it fails before the report.
- **Research quota (illustrative):** 3 runs a day anonymous, 30 signed in; new accounts and IPs get lower limits.
- **Fetch rules:** `GET` only, to public IPs resolved once and pinned, every redirect re-checked (no DNS rebinding, no private ranges); no cookies or credentials; ≤ 2 MB; a 500 ms deadline.
- **Host flags before fetching:** flags are cached for ≈ 50M hosts (the crawl frontier plus every host seen in API results), active hosts re-checked daily (≈ 10M robots fetches a day).
- **A host with no fresh flags:** its robots.txt and signals are fetched inline inside the 500 ms deadline; the page is skipped only if that fails (target ≤ 10% of routed fetches skipped, tracked per answer).
- **Identity:** a declared user agent, published IP ranges and signed requests (Web Bot Auth); research agents fetch only URLs seen in results or on fetched pages.
- **Vendor calls carry the query only:** no user id, thread or IP. Contracts require zero data retention, storage rights for anything kept, terms that allow AI answers, and API results pass the same host-flag filter.
- **Errors:** `429` with `Retry-After` on quota; an `error` event mid-stream ends the answer with what was already shown and a retry link; research failures before the report refund the quota.

## Data model

| Data | Store type | Why (driver ids) | Partition key | Consistency | Rejected alternative and its cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Span index: postings, IVF lists over binary codes in memory, INT8 vectors on SSD | Search engine with hybrid retrieval (Vespa class): a main segment (65,536 IVF lists a shard) and a small fresh segment (in-memory HNSW graph) | 20B spans, ≈ 1,110 queries a second; k = 100 INT8 rescores a shard ≈ 39,600 SSD reads a second a node (G1, G3) | `doc_id` hash; 100 shards × 3 replicas in 3 zones | Fresh segment readable ≈ 1 min after fetch; merged hourly | HNSW over all 20B: 2.6–5.1 TB more memory and slow rebuilds; a separate vector database: two round trips a query |
| Raw HTML (compressed) and parsed pages (text, spans, offsets, content hashes, dates) | Object storage plus a key-value index | Re-parse after a rule change without a re-crawl; offsets to highlight a span; a hash per span to detect change (F3, G6) | `doc_id` | Latest version only (no archive, for copyright) | Parsed text only: every parser fix needs a re-crawl |
| Host flags: `may_index`, `may_answer_from`, `max_snippet`, licence terms, crawl price, flag version | Key-value, replicated to every retrieval node | Enforced at retrieval and on cached answers (F6, N6, G9) | host | Refreshed ≤ 24 h; a flip propagates in minutes | Crawl-time checks only: opted-out pages stay answerable |
| Crawl frontier and schedule (`url`, `next_crawl_at`, `change_rate`, `importance`, `etag`) | A queue per host plus a table | Politeness per host; per-URL scheduling (G6) | host | One writer per host | One global queue: a slow host blocks the others |
| Quality and authority per host and path pattern; link authority; checker support rate | Key-value | Prior for the cheap scorer; parasite pages on reputable domains scored by path (G1) | host + path prefix | Rebuilt daily | A domain allowlist: misses rented subfolders and user content |
| Answer cache with reverse indexes `doc_id → keys` and `entity → keys` | In-memory store (<TechIcon id="oss/redis" /> Redis) | 14% of questions in ≈ 50 ms; eviction on page change, entity news or host opt-out (G5, G6) | key hash | Single-flight per key; followers attach to the in-flight stream | A key that includes the planner's freshness class: every hit waits ≥ 280 ms for the planner |
| Delisting and takedown store | Relational database | Person and URL removals applied at retrieval (F6) | request id | Strong | Removing from the index only: cached answers and API results still show them |
| Threads | Relational database (<TechIcon id="oss/postgresql" /> PostgreSQL), 30-day retention (illustrative) | Follow-ups need the last turn's summary (F2) | `user_id` | Strong per thread | Client-side only: lost across devices |
| Research jobs | Durable workflow engine (Temporal class) with an event log | Runs of 1–4 minutes survive deploys and reconnects (F5) | `job_id` | Exactly-once steps | In-memory loops: a deploy kills every running job |
| Answer traces | Columnar store (<TechIcon id="oss/clickhouse" /> ClickHouse) | p50 and p95 per stage and path, checks, flag versions, cost (F7, G8) | day | Eventual | Logs only: percentiles need full scans |

Each shown sentence is stored with the spans it cites and the host flags in force when it was written.

```sql
CREATE TABLE answer_sentences (
  answer_id text, i int,
  text      text,
  cites     int[],                    -- source numbers [1, 2]
  span_ids  text[],                   -- 'd_77f1:s4' → doc, span and offsets in the page store
  checks    jsonb,                    -- {"support": 0.97, "checker": "0.8b", "harm": "pass",
                                      --  "copy": {"origin": "rfc-editor.org", "run_words": 9, "origin_total_words": 9, "copied_in_a_row": 0}}
  verdict   text,                     -- 'shown' | 'recited' | 'dropped'
  host_flag_versions text[],          -- the flags each cited host had at answer time
  PRIMARY KEY (answer_id, i)
);
```

The span index holds one document per span; host flags and cache entries are small keyed records.

```text
span:   { span_id: "d_77f1:s4", doc_id, host, path, title, heading_path, published, updated,
          text (≈ 200 tokens), bm25 fields, code: bit[1024], vec_int8: int8[1024] (SSD), content_hash, quality }

flags:{host}         -> { may_index, may_answer_from, max_snippet: 120, licence, crawl_price, version, fetched_at }
answer:{key}         -> { answer, intent, freshness: "evergreen", ttl, cited_span_hashes[], entities[] }
                        key = rule-normalised first-turn question + locale (+ a 5 km cell for local questions)
doc2keys:{doc_id}    -> answer keys that cite this page
entity2keys:{entity} -> answer keys that name this entity
```

- **Cache key rules:** local cues ("near me", "open now") add a coarse location cell (≈ 5 km) or skip the cache; relative time ("today", "tonight") adds the user's local date and caps the TTL at the news class.
- **Cache hit check:** an entry whose stored intent is local is never served to a key without a cell.
- **Access patterns:** hybrid top-100 per shard per query; host flags read on every candidate in the prefilter; `doc2keys` and `entity2keys` read on every page change, fast-tier story and flag flip.
- **More access patterns:** traces scanned by day and stage for percentiles; sentences joined to spans for audits and wrong-citation reports.

## Building the architecture, step by step

- We start from one search call and one model call, and add a part only when a requirement or a driver forces it.

### Step 1: Search, then write from the snippets (F1)

The baseline: one search-API call, its snippets in a prompt, and a streamed answer.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={1} caption="Step 1: a gateway, an orchestrator, one search-API call and the answer model. It answers anything, but from two-line snippets." />

- **Adds:** the API gateway (auth, rate limits, SSE), the answer orchestrator, the search APIs, the answer model.
- **Flow:**
  1. The question reaches the gateway and the orchestrator.
  2. One search-API call returns 10 results with snippets.
  3. The snippets go into a prompt; the model streams an answer that cites result numbers.
- **Why this and not that:** the simplest engine that answers anything, and the bar every later part must beat on the gold set (P1).
- **The gap:** one literal search misses vague and multi-part questions, and a snippet is two lines long.

### Step 2: Plan the question; look up the cache in parallel (F1, F2)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={2} caption="Step 2: a small-model query planner writes 3 searches and a route; the gateway looks up the answer cache at the same time, and a hit cancels the planner." />

- **Adds:** the query planner, the answer cache.
- **Flow:**
  1. The gateway looks up the cache on the rule-normalised question while the planner starts; a hit returns the whole answer at ≈ 50 ms and cancels the planner.
  2. On a miss, the planner returns intents, 3 rewrites, a freshness class and a route in ≈ 250 ms.
  3. The 3 searches run in parallel; their result lists merge by reciprocal rank fusion (RRF: each result scores the sum of 1 ÷ (k + rank) across lists).
- **Why this and not that:** route by difficulty from the first call (P3); the cache key cannot depend on the planner, or every hit waits 280 ms for it (P5).
- **The gap:** the model still fills two-line snippets from its own memory.

Rewriting is what turns one vague question into searches that each find part of the answer.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/rag/query-rewriting" caption="No single rewrite finds every chunk; together they put all three relevant chunks in the top 5." />

**How to read it:**

- The question sits on top; each box below is one rewrite with its own search, and the list at the bottom is the merged top 5.
- The "found by" column shows that each relevant result was found by more than one rewrite, which is why merging lifts recall.
- The figure is the RAG section's running example (a password question) and also shows HyDE, a guessed answer used as a query; this planner writes 3 rewrites only.

**The numbers:**

- The planner is a small model with a 600-token cached prefix: ≈ 0.000056 USD and 250 ms at p50, with a 400 ms deadline that falls back to the raw question.
- 3 rewrites × 100 shards × (100 lexical + 100 vector results) = 600 candidate spans once the index exists (Step 7).

### Step 3: Read pages as spans (F1, F3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={3} caption="Step 3: the fetcher and parser read the top pages under a 500 ms deadline and cut them into self-contained spans kept in the page store." />

- **Adds:** the fetcher and parser, the page store.
- **Flow:**
  1. The top 3 pages are fetched in parallel; the slowest of 3 takes ≈ 400 ms at p50, under a 500 ms deadline.
  2. The parser keeps the main text and cuts it into spans of ≈ 200 tokens, each prefixed with its title, heading path and date.
  3. Raw HTML and spans go to the page store with offsets, so a citation can highlight the exact passage.
- **Why this and not that:** ground in passages, not snippets (P2); a span carries its own subject, so it can be read alone.
- **The gap:** 8 pages are 80 spans and 16,000 tokens, far too many to send.

### Step 4: Rank spans in stages (F1, N1)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={4} caption="Step 4: the ranker filters and scores spans cheaply; the cross-encoder reads only the best 60 and keeps 12." />

- **Adds:** the ranker (prefilter and cheap scorer), the cross-encoder.
- **Flow:**
  1. Candidates merge and dedupe: 600 retrieved → 400 unique.
  2. The prefilter drops stale pages, spam, syndicated copies and hosts whose flags forbid answering: 300 left.
  3. A gradient-boosted cheap scorer keeps 60 in ≈ 30 ms; the cross-encoder keeps 12 in ≈ 80 ms, at most 3 per domain and 2 per page.
- **Why this and not that:** cheap scorers first and the expensive model on few (P4); 2,700 tokens reach the model instead of 16,000 (P8).
- **The gap:** citations are still the model's word.

The funnel puts a count and a time on each stage.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/ranking-funnel" caption="Cheap scorers cut 600 spans to 60 in about 110 ms; the expensive cross-encoder only ever sees 60, and 12 reach the model." />

**How to read it:**

- Each band is one stage; its width is proportional to the spans still in play, and the note beside it says what removed the rest.
- The last band is what reaches the prompt: 12 spans, at most 3 from one domain.

**The numbers:**

- A **cross-encoder** reads the question and the span together, so it ranks better than vector distance; it reads 60 × (200 + 30) = 13,800 tokens a pass in 80 ms.
- On all 300 after the prefilter it would take 80 × 300 ÷ 60 = 400 ms, 320 ms more than the stage allows.
- In the prompt: 12 headers × 25 + 12 spans × 200 = 2,700 tokens, against 8 pages × 2,000 = 16,000 (13,300 fewer, ≈ 0.027 USD on a mid-size call).

### Step 5: Cite while writing; check each sentence before showing (F3, N2, N3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={5} caption="Step 5: sentence checks hold each sentence until its support, copy and harm checks pass; only then does it reach the user." />

- **Adds:** the sentence checks; the `sources` and `sentence` events.
- **Flow:**
  1. Spans are numbered; `sources` goes to the client at ≈ 470 ms.
  2. The model ends each sentence with `[n]`; the orchestrator holds it.
  3. Rules (numbers, dates and names must appear in a cited span), a 0.8B support checker (a 7B on the uncertain 20%), a harm screen and the copy state run in ≈ 60 ms.
  4. A passing sentence is shown; a wrong pointer is re-cited to the span that supports it; an unsupported sentence is dropped, and generation restarts from the last shown sentence.
- **Why this and not that:** provenance down to the sentence (P14), and the rules live in code, not in the prompt (P10).
- **The gap:** dependent multi-hop and research questions fail in one pass.

### Step 6: Escalate: multi-hop and research tiers (F2, F5)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={6} caption="Step 6: a research lead and read-only subagents take the questions one pass cannot answer, using the same ranker and checks." />

- **Adds:** the research lead, the research subagents.
- **Flow:**
  1. The planner's label, or a low-confidence end, picks the route.
  2. Multi-hop: 2 retrieval rounds, the second built from the first's answer (≈ 5.4 s, 0.026 USD).
  3. Research: tier S, M or L (46 s, 71 s, 101 s at p50); subagents search and fetch in parallel, and the lead writes a report through the same ranker and checks.
- **Why this and not that:** agents only where they pay (P1); caps in tokens and dollars per tier (P6); research agents can only read (P12).
- **The gap:** every quick answer still waits for a vendor and pays for 3 calls, and news freshness is the vendor's.

### Step 7: Own the head of the web (F4, N3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={7} caption="Step 7: a crawler, an indexer and a 20-billion-span index now serve every quick answer; the search API joins only on the 25% the planner flags." />

- **Adds:** the crawler and scheduler, the indexer (parse rules, dedupe, spans, embeddings), the span index.
- **Flow:**
  1. A per-URL schedule refetches pages by predicted change × importance; 200,000 fast sources are polled every 5 minutes.
  2. Raw HTML is kept; pages are deduplicated and canonicalised, cut into spans and embedded with their document's context.
  3. New spans land in a small fresh segment, readable in about a minute; the main segment merges hourly.
  4. Retrieval runs 3 hybrid queries × 100 shards with a 60 ms per-shard deadline and a hedge at 40 ms.
- **Why this and not that:** own the path most questions take (P1, G3, G12) and cache at every layer (P7); the API stays for the long tail.
- **The gap:** publishers' rules, spam and injection are now ours to handle, and nobody yet knows whether answers improve.

Freshness is a pipeline with a clock on every stage, and the cache has to follow it.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/freshness-path" caption="A polled story is retrievable in about 3.5 minutes; cached answers expire by freshness class and are evicted early when a cited span, a named entity or a host’s rules change." />

**How to read it:**

- The top row is one story's path from publication to retrievable; the cells below are the cache TTLs by freshness class.
- The three boxes over the cache are the events that evict an answer before its TTL ends.

**The numbers:**

- A story waits 0–5 minutes for the next poll (2.5 on average), then ≈ 1 minute to fetch, parse, embed and write: 3.5 minutes at p50, 6 at worst (N4: ≤ 5 / 15).
- TTLs: news 5 minutes, recent 6 hours, evergreen 24 hours; a cached news answer can lag a story by at most 5 + 1 + 5 = 11 minutes.
- Change detection uses a content hash per span, not page bytes, so a new ad or timestamp does not evict anything.

### Step 8: Publisher controls, screening and evaluation (F6, F7, N2, N6)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/architecture" step={8} caption="Step 8: host flags reach the crawler and the span index, a safety screen guards queries and spans, and traces feed the evaluation that gates each release." />

- **Adds:** host flags, the safety screen, answer traces, evaluation.
- **Flow:**
  1. Robots.txt, Content Signals, RSL licences and snippet rules become per-host flags, replicated to retrieval and read by the crawler.
  2. The ranker's prefilter applies them to own-index spans and API results alike; an opt-out purges stored pages and evicts cached answers.
  3. The safety screen checks questions and the 12 chosen spans for planted instructions.
  4. Traces feed an independent grader, a weekly human audit and the gold sets; the release gate reads them.
- **Why this and not that:** retrieved content is untrusted (P13); evaluation runs continuously (P16); wrong-citation reports become test cases (P15).
- **The gap:** none; the gate decides each release (publisher rules in detail under Safety and guardrails).

## Component choices and why

- Every box in the final diagram: what we picked, why, and why the usual alternatives lose. Driver ids (G) refer to the drivers table; F and N to the requirements.

### Index: own head index plus a search API for the tail

- **Choice:** an own index of 2 billion pages (20 billion spans) serves every quick answer; one search-API call joins on the 25% the planner flags as long-tail or breaking.
- **Why:** latency, control, freshness and supplier risk (G3, G6, G12): the first verified sentence comes ≈ 860 ms sooner at p50 than on a snippet-API path (1,353 against 2,215 ms), and spans, ranking and flags are ours.
- **Why not API only:** the right answer at launch and below ≈ 5.3M questions a day at list price; against 1 call an answer it is cheaper even at 5 USD per 1,000 (213,700 against 241,700 USD a day), so the index is bought for the reasons above.
- **Why not a full web index:** hundreds of billions of URLs and exabytes of storage for a tail the API already covers.
- **Why not a grounding product (14 USD per 1,000):** tied to the vendor's own models, and its outputs cannot feed our ranker and checks.

### Search API vendors: two, behind one interface

- **Choice:** two search APIs behind one client, with a contracted burst of 400 calls a second on the primary.
- **Why:** no single vendor or index outage stops answers (N7, G12); the burst covers the rung where the index is down.
- **Why not one vendor:** a single supplier who can retire the API.
- **Why not a scraping (SERP) API:** ≈ 1.3 s at p50 in a vendor-run test, and terms risk.

### Unit of retrieval: spans of about 200 tokens with a title-and-heading prefix

- **Choice:** self-contained spans, each with its title, heading path and date, embedded with its document's context.
- **Why:** 2,700 tokens reach the model instead of 16,000 for 8 pages, ≈ 0.027 USD less on a mid-size answer (G4, G7); a span can be cited and highlighted exactly (F3).
- **Why not LLM-written context per span:** 67B tokens a day × ≈ 1.02 USD per million ≈ 68,300 USD a day, more than the whole index; the cheap prefix is measured on recall@60 instead.
- **Why not whole pages:** cost, prefill time, and models use the middle of a long input worst.
- **Why not sentences:** they lose their subject and multiply the index tenfold.

### Vector index and embeddings: IVF over binary codes, INT8 rescoring, HNSW for the fresh segment

- **Choice:** IVF (inverted-file) partitions over 1,024-bit binary codes in memory (2.56 TB), INT8 vectors on SSD to rescore the top 100 a shard; a small HNSW graph for the fresh segment.
- **Why:** a probe of 64 of 65,536 lists a shard scans ≈ 200,000 codes; nprobe is raised until recall@100 ≥ 0.95 against exact search (G1, G3, G4).
- **Why not HNSW for all 20B spans:** 2.6–5.1 TB more memory for the graph, and slow rebuilds under 33.5M re-embedded pages a day.
- **Why not a disk-resident graph (DiskANN class):** good at this scale but harder with continuous updates; revisit for a tail at 100B pages.
- **Embeddings:** a sub-1B model that embeds a span with its page's context and emits INT8 (4× smaller than FP32) and binary (32×) vectors; binary loses 2–4 points, which INT8 rescoring wins back.
- **Why not a 4B embedding model:** better quality, ≈ 6× the re-embedding GPUs.

### Ranking: prefilter, then a cheap scorer, then a cross-encoder on 60

- **Choice:** rules prefilter, gradient-boosted trees over RRF rank, BM25, vector similarity, freshness, host-and-path quality and link authority (≈ 30 ms), then a sub-1B cross-encoder on 60 (80 ms).
- **Why:** the expensive model sees few candidates and the stage fits the budget (G1, G3, P4).
- **Why not a cross-encoder on all 300:** + 320 ms.
- **Why not an LLM listwise reranker:** ≈ 400 ms and a mid-size call on every answer.
- **Why not a managed reranker:** ≈ 8,970 USD a day against ≈ 2,460 for our cross-encoder GPUs.

### Query planner: a small model with a JSON schema

- **Choice:** one small-model call returns intents, topic tags (health, money, law, person), time scope, freshness, route and 3 rewrites in ≈ 250 ms.
- **Why:** navigational questions get the link; ambiguous ones get the main sense, said as such; local ones use a coarse location only with consent; other languages are retrieved cross-lingually (F2, G3).
- **Why not rules or a classifier:** they cannot rewrite or decompose a question.
- **Why not a mid-size model:** ≈ 2× the latency on every question.
- **Query screen:** a separate sub-1B classifier screens every question in ≈ 5 ms, before the cache lookup, which the 250 ms planner cannot do.

### Answer model: small for single-hop facts, mid-size for the rest

- **Choice:** a small model on 60% (single-hop facts with strong span agreement), a mid-size model on comparisons, multi-part, weak agreement and high-stakes topics; the provider's native citations on.
- **Why:** 0.00472 USD a blended answer against 0.01095 all mid-size, 57% less (G4, P3); native citations guarantee valid pointers into our spans.
- **Why not native citations alone:** a valid pointer is not a supporting passage; entailment is still checked.
- **Why not one frontier model:** cost and first-token time on every question.

### Sentence checks: a cascade

- **Choice:** rules first (≈ 1 ms), a 0.8B checker on every sentence, a 7B checker on the uncertain 20%, plus the copy state and a harm screen.
- **Why:** 60 ms at p50, overlapping the next sentence's generation; the 7B on everything would need 161 GPUs instead of 33 (G1, G3, P4).
- **Why not the top 27B checkers:** ≈ 3× the 7B's compute for 2 points on a public leaderboard.
- **Why not an LLM judge per sentence:** ≈ 200–300 ms a sentence.
- **Why not a citation agent after writing:** it finds a page for each sentence instead of checking the sentence against what the model read.

### Streaming: hold each sentence until checked; restart on a drop

- **Choice:** the orchestrator holds each sentence, sends it once its checks pass, and on a drop restarts generation from the last shown sentence with the claim excluded.
- **Why:** nothing shown is retracted, and a later sentence cannot lean on a dropped claim (G2, N2); the restart reads the cached prompt, ≈ 300 ms to the next token.
- **Why not raw tokens:** ≈ 59% of answers would visibly change.
- **Why not keep generating after a drop:** a later "this is because…" passes on its own and the answer reads as complete.

### Escalation and the research runtime

- **Choice:** three routes (quick, multi-hop, research tiers S, M, L), research on a durable workflow engine.
- **Why:** a dependent hop ("the CEO of the company that bought X") fails one pass and does not need a minute of research; runs of 46–101 s survive deploys and reconnects (F2, F5, G4).
- **Why not one research size:** 4 × 8 is too much for a comparison and too little for a report.
- **Why not research for every question:** 0.45 against 0.0063 USD.
- **Why not in-memory agent loops:** every deploy kills the running jobs.

### Crawl: per-URL schedule, conditional GET, politeness, publisher rules

- **Choice:** recrawl by predicted change × importance; conditional GET; ≤ 2 concurrent requests per host, ≥ 1 s apart or `Crawl-delay`; backoff on 429 and 503 up to 24 h; robots, signals, licences and snippet rules honoured for crawls, live fetches and API results.
- **Why:** 304 responses skip parse and embed for 60% of refetches (G4, G6); publishers set AI-use rules and have sued (F6, N6).
- **Why not fixed tiers alone:** a fast-changing page outside the feeds waits 60 days.
- **Why not daily recrawl:** about 26× our fetches.
- **Why not robots.txt only:** `ai-input=no` and licence terms would be ignored while technically compliant.
- **Dedupe at index time:** near-duplicate grouping (MinHash) and `rel=canonical` keep one original per story; copies inherit its flags, so they cannot fake independent corroboration (G1, G2).
- **Rendering:** a headless browser only for the 10% of pages that need JavaScript, chosen per host by a static-fetch test (1,200 USD a day); rendering everything costs ≈ 10–100× a plain fetch.

### Answer cache: exact match on the rule-normalised question

- **Choice:** <TechIcon id="oss/redis" /> Redis keyed on the normalised first-turn question and locale, TTL by freshness class, single-flight per key, three eviction rules.
- **Why:** 14% of questions in ≈ 50 ms, ≈ 8,800 USD a day saved; single-flight stops a news spike starting ≈ 240 pipelines on one key (G5, G6).
- **Why not a semantic cache:** a paraphrase hit can serve a different question's answer, on a product whose cost of a wrong answer is high (G2).
- **Why not cache follow-ups:** they depend on the thread.

## Latency and cost budget

The waterfall puts the own-index path, the API-routed path, a cache hit and a multi-hop answer side by side.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/latency" caption="The own index reaches the first checked sentence about 860 ms sooner than the API path; the slowest of three fetches, not the average, sets the API path’s budget." />

**How to read it:**

- Each bar is one stage at its start time; the first three lanes share one time scale, and the multi-hop lane has its own, named in its heading.
- On the own path, index and ranking are one bar ending at `sources`; on the API path, fetch and parse are one bar ending before ranking.

**The numbers:** p50 in ms; decode at 80 tokens a second and a 500 ms first token are illustrative for a mid-size model behind an API.

| Stage | Own-index path (75% of quick) | API-routed path (25%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edge, auth, query screen; cache lookup in parallel | 0 → 30 | 0 → 30 |
| Planner (small, deadline 400) | 30 → 280 | 30 → 280 |
| Own index: 3 queries × 100 shards, per-shard deadline 60, hedged at 40, + 20 merge | 280 → 360 | In parallel, off the critical path |
| One API call (p50 513, p95 808 in a vendor-run test) | | 280 → 793 |
| Live fetch: the slowest of 3 (≈ 400; deadline 500) + parse 30 | | 793 → 1,223 |
| Prefilter and cheap scorer (→ 60) | 360 → 390 | 1,223 → 1,253 |
| Cross-encoder (→ 12), then `sources` | 390 → 470 | 1,253 → 1,333 |

| Checks (support, copy, harm) | 1,293 → 1,353 | 2,155 → 2,215 |

- **Max-of-n:** the slowest of n parallel calls at quantile q is one call's quantile q^(1/n). Three fetches at p50: 0.5^(1/3) = 0.794, so 300 + 0.82 × 122 ≈ 400 ms (one fetch: p50 300, p95 500).
- **One API call, not two:** the slowest of 2 calls at p50 is one call's p70.7, ≈ 616 ms instead of 513.
- **Shards:** the index stage is bounded by its 60 ms per-shard deadline; a shard that misses it (target ≤ 1%) is dropped, a monitored loss of 1/100 of spans.
- **p95 per path:** root-sum-square of illustrative stage spreads, mixed with the 7.1% of answers whose first sentence is dropped (+ 673 ms): own path ≈ 1,980 ms (≤ 2,500), API path ≈ 2,910 ms (≤ 3,200).
- **Correlated tails:** planner, cross-encoder, first token, sentence and checks share our provider and GPU fleet and slow together at peak. Fully correlated, the own path is ≈ 2,200 ms and the API path ≈ 3,120 ms, 76 ms inside its target.
- **All stages slow at once:** 3,390 ms without drops, ≈ 3,480 with them; it is not a bound, and it would miss, which is why the API-path p95 is gated on peak-hour live traces.
- **The lever if it fails:** race the call against the second vendor (p50 358, p95 763 in the same vendor-run test). The API stage drops to ≈ 311 / 602 ms and the path to ≈ 2,010 / 2,920, for ≈ 10,000 USD a day at 5 USD per 1,000.
- **Short answer (120 tokens, 5 sentences):** a dropped sentence is not replaced, so each restart adds 300 + 60 = 360 ms. Own path p50 ≈ 2,650, p95 ≈ 3,315 (≤ 3,000 / 3,500); API path ≈ 3,520 / 4,245 (≤ 3,600 / 4,300: 55 ms of headroom).
- **Cache hit:** edge 30 + lookup 10 + send 10 ≈ 50 ms p50 (≤ 200 p95); the planner is cancelled.
- **Multi-hop:** planner 280 + 2 rounds × 1,940 (index 80, cheap 30, cross 80, first token 500, 100 tokens 1,250) + the answer stages 1,073 = 5,233 ms; with the 25% whose first round calls the API, p50 ≈ 5,400, p95 ≈ 6,480 (≤ 6,000 / 9,000).
- **Full 300-token answer:** 4,790 ms before restarts, ≈ 5,080 on average with 0.80 restarts × 360 ms.
- **Research p50:** plan 7.25 s + calls × 5.45 s + synthesis 1.5 s + the report at 80 tokens a second: S 46 s, M 71 s, L 101 s. The lead proceeds at 75 s with the subagents that reported; research stops at 200 s and the partial report is out by 240 s.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Budget three parallel fetches as one fetch and the API path looks 100 ms faster on paper than it ever runs. At p95 the slowest of three is one fetch's p98, which is why the fetch stage has a hard 500 ms deadline.
</Callout>

- **Cost:** 0.0063 USD a quick answer, 0.026 a multi-hop answer, 0.45 a research run on average, ≈ 241,700 USD a day all-in.

## Evaluation and release gates

| Offline set | Size | Source | What it checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gold set | 1,000 questions | Reference answers and gold spans: 400 evergreen facts, 200 comparisons and multi-hop, 400 high-stakes and person questions (a separate slice), refreshed quarterly | Correctness, abstention, citation recall and precision |
| Changed this week | 400 questions | Written weekly from stories and edits whose answer changed | Freshness (N4) |
| Checker set | 3,000 sentences, 1,000 of them unsupported (oversampled) | Labelled by people | Checker false accepts and false rejects |
| Adversarial set | 1,000 cases | Hidden instructions (multilingual, encoded), "recommend X" pages, satire and data voids, syndicated copies, harmful-advice pages, exfiltration through links | Followed injections, harmful sentences, copying |
| Research benchmarks | FRAMES, BrowseComp (1,266 questions), BrowseComp-Plus on a fixed corpus | Public | Research tiers; retrievers compared reproducibly |
| Copyright tests | 3 scripted answers | Written by the team | The copy state behaves as specified |

- **Independent grading (N1, N2):** a grader from a different model family than the answer model and the checkers reads the whole answer and its spans.
- **What it grades:** sentence support with ALCE definitions (recall: the cited spans entail the sentence; precision: no citation is irrelevant), plus answer-level errors the sentence checks cannot see.
- **Answer-level errors:** two true sentences about different products merged into one comparison; a 2019 result presented as current; an orphaned "this is because…".
- **Human audit:** 200 answers a week (≈ 2,200 sentences) anchor the grader (kappa ≥ 0.7) and give the checkers' live false-accept rate; the sentence interval is ± 0.9 points before clustering by answer.
- **Why the grader, not the audit, measures the 3%:** 200 answers give ± 2.4 points and cannot tell 3% from 5%; the grader on 1% of traffic (≈ 86,000 answers a day) gives ± 0.1.
- **Checker rates:** false accepts 5% ± 1.4 points on the 1,000 unsupported sentences.
- **The model's own rate is a gate:** a release fails if it writes more than 5% unsupported sentences on the gold set. With 5% false accepts, 5% false rejects and half the rejects rescued by a re-cite, 7.1% of sentences drop and ≈ 99.7% of shown sentences are supported.
- **SimpleQA is reported, not gated:** search systems score about 0.93 on it, so it no longer separates them.

One failing check blocks the release, however well the others pass.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/release-gate" caption="One failing check blocks the release: citation precision is graded by an independent model, never by the checks that filter sentences." />

**How to read it:**

- The release candidate (a new answer-model version) on the left fans out to seven checks; green passes, red fails, and any red blocks.
- Here citation precision is 88.0% against a 90% bar, so the release is blocked although correctness, recall and latency pass.

**The numbers:**

- Correctness on 1,000 questions at 88% has a ± 2.0-point interval, so the gate is a paired comparison (McNemar, p below 0.05) and never more than 2 points down.
- The high-stakes slice of 400 has ± 3.2 points and must not be lower; freshness on 400 has ± 3.5.
- Followed injections: 0 of 1,000, which bounds the true rate at 0.3% by the rule of three (3 ÷ 1,000).
- Copyright: four consecutive copied sentences must fail at the 2nd, four separate 18-word runs at the 3rd, and a 9-word and a 15-word quote must pass.
- Latency: p50 per path on a replay of 10,000 traced questions; p95 per path on peak-hour live traces of the 1% canary; cost per answer within 10%.

- **Online:** wrong-citation reports per 1,000 answers, low-confidence rate, reformulation within 30 s, research acceptance, and source clicks as a weak signal only.
- **Index sufficiency:** on a 2% shadow sample the API also runs; if it changes the top 12 on more than 20% of questions, grow the index. On routed questions, test 1 against 2 API calls the same way.
- **Coverage:** the routed-fetch skip rate (≤ 10%) and the share of the top 1,000 news hosts with `may_answer_from` true.
- **Rollout:** shadow → 1% → 10% → 50% → 100%, by route. A 3-point drop in graded correctness, a 1-point drop in citation recall or a 200 ms p95 regression rolls back.
- **The gate that blocks a release:** any failing check above; there is no override for citation precision or injections.

## Safety and guardrails

Four kinds of publisher rule become one set of flags per host, checked wherever a page could reach an answer.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/publisher-controls" stepper caption="Robots, Content Signals, RSL licences and snippet rules become one set of per-host flags, checked where pages are crawled, where spans are retrieved and in the answer cache." />

**How to read it:**

- The left column is the four rule sources, the middle box the flags they produce, the right column where the flags are enforced.
- The timing lines below say how fast a publisher's change takes effect at each point.

**The numbers:**

- Rules, refreshed ≤ 24 h per host (hourly for the top 10,000 news hosts): robots.txt for our agent (a 5xx means disallow), `Content-Signal` (`search`, `ai-input`, `ai-train`), an RSL `License:` and its terms, and `nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `X-Robots-Tag`.
- `may_index` = robots allows our agent, RSL does not prohibit `ai-index`, and any crawl price is within budget.
- `may_answer_from` = `may_index`, `ai-input` is not `no`, RSL permits `ai-input` where a licence exists, and the page is not `nosnippet`; `max_snippet` caps quoted characters.
- A missing signal means "no preference": we treat it as allowed if robots allows our agent, and say so in our crawler documentation.
- After a flip: out of retrieval in minutes, cached answers citing the host evicted ≤ 1 h, spans and pages purged ≤ 24 h after the flip and ≤ 48 h from the publisher's change.
- Where coverage of news hosts is low, the remedy is licensing (a per-crawl placeholder of 7,600 USD a day and a per-answer one of 1,730), not routing around the rules through an API.

| Threat | Where it enters | Control | Code or prompt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instructions hidden in a page | Fetched page | Parse-time stripping of hidden text (`display:none`, off-screen, tiny or invisible text, comments, `aria-hidden`); injection classifier at index time, rechecked on the 12 chosen spans; spans quoted with ids | Code + prompt |
| Exfiltration through the answer | Model output | Plain text and chips; model-written URLs and images stripped; links only from `sources` | Code |
| Preference manipulation, "generative engine optimisation", plain false claims | Page content | Quality per host and path; ≤ 3 spans per domain; a key claim supported only by low-prior hosts needs a second independent origin; first-party claims labelled | Code |
| Parasite pages on reputable domains | Index | Quality scored per host and path pattern, not per domain | Code |
| Syndicated copies | Index | MinHash grouping and canonicals; copies inherit the original's flags; independence = a different origin after grouping | Code |
| High-stakes topics (health, money, law, safety) | Answer | Source tiers per topic; ≥ 2 independent origins; "general information, not advice" on dosing and investment, keyed on the planner's topic tag; a crisis template with local helplines for self-harm | Code + prompt |
| Harmful instructions built from benign or poisoned pages | Answer | Harm screen on each held sentence (5–10 ms); drop or refuse | Code |
| Copyright reproduction | Answer, cache, shared links | Copy state per cited origin across the whole answer: running verbatim run ≤ 25 words, ≤ 50 verbatim words per origin and ≤ its `max_snippet`, ≤ 2 copied sentences in a row, ≤ 40% of sentences from one origin; research ≤ 100 words per origin | Code |
| People: defamation, merged identities, delisting | Answer | A `person` topic tag: stricter corroboration, no merging of sources unless they share an identifier; the delisting store applied at retrieval and on cached answers | Code |
| A research agent hijacked to exfiltrate or act | Agent tools | Search and fetch only; URLs only from results or fetched pages; pinned-IP guard against server-side request forgery; ≤ 2 fetches a search | Code |
| Cost attacks | Gateway | Rate limits, quotas and bot detection; per-run caps bound one account at 30 runs × ≤ 2 USD; new-account and per-IP research limits and a daily research-budget alarm | Code |
| Personal data in questions | Query, logs | Vendors see the query only, with zero retention; traces pseudonymised; raw questions kept 30 days (illustrative) | Code |

- **Copy state mechanics:** the longest common token run between shown text and each origin's spans, carried across sentence boundaries, costs ≈ 1 ms with a suffix automaton over 12 spans.
- **A failing sentence:** held, and generation restarts with "paraphrase source n; at most m more quoted words". Lyrics and book text get the run limit with no exceptions.

## Deep dives

### Own index or search API: a price, a volume, a call count and a latency

- **The fair comparator:** an API-only design must find what our index finds from the planner's 3 rewrites, so it sends 3 calls a quick answer; our own API path sends 1, for latency.
- **Same machinery on both sides:** API-only keeps the planner, ranker, checks, GPUs, platform, team and per-answer licensing; it drops the index's 75,800 USD a day and pays per call.
- **Quality is measured, not assumed:** the 2% shadow sample shows whether the API changes our top 12, and whether a second call on routed questions changes graded correctness.
- **Publisher rules cut both ways:** under our rules API results from opted-out hosts are filtered too, so coverage does not favour API-only.

The curve plots a day's cost against the API's price per 1,000 calls for both designs.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/answer-engine/index-vs-api" stepper caption="On cost alone the index wins only above 2.6 to 8.1 USD per 1,000 calls, depending on how many calls the API-only design would send; below that it is bought for latency, control and supplier risk." />

**How to read it:**

- The x axis is the API price per 1,000 calls; the y axis is the cost of a day. The violet line is our index; teal solid and dashed are API-only at 3 and 2 calls; blue dashed is 1 call.
- Where a teal or blue line crosses the violet one, the two designs cost the same; to the right of a crossing, the index is cheaper.
- The markers sit at 1 USD (the fast tier) and 5 USD (list price).

**The numbers:**

- API only: 148,690 USD a day fixed + 13.0M, 22.5M or 32.0M calls × price. Ours: 222,310 fixed (the index included) + 3.88M calls × price.
- Crossings: (222,310 − 148,690) ÷ (calls − 3.88M) = 8.1, 4.0 and 2.6 USD per 1,000 for 1, 2 and 3 calls.
- At 1 USD and 3 calls: 180,700 against 226,200, so the index costs ≈ 45,500 USD a day more. At 5 USD: 308,700 against 241,700; with 1 call, 213,700.
- By volume at 3 calls, the index pays on cost above ≈ 5.3M questions a day at 5 USD, and above ≈ 25M at 1 USD.
- What the 45,500 USD buys: ≈ 860 ms at p50 on 75% of quick answers (430–585 ms against an API that returns page extracts), span control, our own freshness, flag enforcement and no single supplier.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Price the comparator at 1 call and the index never pays; price it at 3 calls and list price and it always does. State the call count and the price, and let the shadow check decide whether 1 call loses recall.
</Callout>

### Citation verification and what it cannot see

- **Definitions (ALCE):** a sentence has citation recall if its cited spans together entail it; a citation has precision unless it is irrelevant (it cannot support the sentence alone, and the sentence stays supported without it).
- **Native citations are pointers:** the provider guarantees each citation points into a span we sent, which removes made-up and mis-numbered citations, not unsupported ones.
- **A per-sentence copy limit bounds nothing:** a sentence is ≈ 19 words, so four copied sentences (76 words) each pass a 25-word check. The running run carried across sentences fails at the 2nd (38 > 25).
- **What sentence checks miss:** merged comparisons, stale dates and orphaned "this is because…" sentences are answer-level errors; the independent grader looks for them.
- **A poisoned span:** a span that supports a false claim passes entailment; corroboration catches it, since high-stakes and low-prior-only claims need a second independent origin.

**The numbers:**

- Drops a sentence: 5% unsupported × 95% caught + 95% supported × 5% false rejects × 50% not rescued by a re-cite ≈ 7.1%.
- Over 12 sentences: no drop 41%, one 38%, two 16%; a 3rd drop ends the answer as low-confidence with a research offer, 4.9% of quick answers; 0.80 restarts an answer on average.
- Shown sentences supported: ≈ 99.7% if the checker rates hold, which is why the live false-accept rate comes from the human audit and wrong-citation reports.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Grade citation quality with the checker that filtered the sentences and every shown sentence passes by construction. Only a grader from another model family, calibrated against people, measures what the filter let through.
</Callout>

### Escalation: quick, multi-hop, research

- **Signals:** dependent hops send a question to multi-hop; 3 or more sub-questions, a comparison of 3 or more entities, or "write a report" send it to a research tier; a low-confidence end makes an offer; the user can press the button.
- **Auto-run:** research starts on its own only on a confident planner label and within quota; otherwise it is offered.
- **Tiers set effort; caps are backstops:** 150,000 / 600,000 / 1.2M tokens and 0.5 / 1 / 2 USD for S / M / L. The L tier uses 1.10M of its 1.2M tokens at 10 calls a subagent.
- **More tokens buy better research** (How real systems do it), so a cap is a quality-for-cost trade, set per tier rather than once.
- **Every route reuses the ranker and the checks:** a research report's sentences pass the same support, copy and harm checks, with a 100-word copy limit per origin.

**How to read it:**

- Left: the signal; middle: the route it picks; right: the research tiers, each with its subagents, calls, cost and time.

**The numbers:**

- Quick: 80% of questions, 0.0063 USD, 1.35 s to the first verified sentence. Multi-hop: 5%, 0.026 USD, ≈ 5.4 s.
- Research: 1% direct + 0.39% accepted offers ≈ 1.4% of questions, 139,100 runs a day at 0.45 USD on average: 62,900 USD a day, 26% of the bill.
- At doubled model prices: S 0.37 USD, M hits its 1 USD cap (1.04 uncapped), L 1.93 against its 2 USD cap.
- Volume is linear in offer acceptance: at 20% instead of 10%, 178,300 runs and ≈ 259,400 USD a day.

### Worked example: one tier-M research run

A user asks to compare four phones on battery life, price and repairability (illustrative); the planner labels it multi-part, tier M.

1. The lead plans: 2,000 tokens in, 500 out; 1 s + 500 tokens at 80 a second = 7.25 s.
2. 4 subagents run 8 calls each in parallel; a call (search, fetch, read, 300 tokens out) takes 5.45 s, so 8 × 5.45 = 43.6 s.
3. 32 searches, half also sent to the API: 16 × 5 USD per 1,000 = 0.08 USD; 2 fetches a search: 64 × 0.0001 = 0.0064 USD.
4. One subagent's context grows from 2,000 by 3,300 a call: 25,100 new tokens written at 2.50 (0.0628) + 83,300 earlier tokens read at 0.10 (0.0083) + 2,400 out at 10.00 (0.024) = 0.0951 USD; × 4 = 0.380.
5. The lead synthesises from 2,000 + 4 × 3,000 summaries + 20,000 selected spans and writes a 1,500-token report: 36,000 in × 2.00 + 2,000 out × 10.00 = 0.092 USD; a small-model repair pass adds 0.003.
6. Time: 7.25 + 43.6 + 1.5 synthesis + 1,500 ÷ 80 = 18.75 s of report ≈ 71 s; tokens: 4 × 108,400 + 58,500 for the lead and repair = 492,100, under the 600,000 cap.
7. Total: 0.380 + 0.092 + 0.003 + 0.08 + 0.0064 ≈ 0.56 USD, against 0.0063 for a quick answer, and under the 1 USD cap until model prices double.

### System design patterns

| Pattern | Where in this design | Why it is needed here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Fan-out](/system-design/patterns/fan-out) | 3 rewrites × 100 shards; 3 live fetches; research subagents | One question becomes hundreds of reads that must come back together |
| [Caching](/system-design/patterns/caching) | Answer cache, page store, host-flag cache, prompt cache | 14% of questions skip the pipeline; flags are read on every candidate |
| [Hot Keys, Skew and Request Coalescing](/system-design/patterns/hot-keys-and-coalescing) | Single-flight per cache key; followers attach to the stream | A news spike would start ≈ 240 pipelines on one key |
| [Tail-Latency Hedging, Deadlines and Degradation](/system-design/patterns/tail-latency-and-degradation) | Per-shard deadline 60 ms, hedge at 40; fetch deadline 500; the vendor race | The slowest of n sets the budget |
| [Circuit Breaker, Retries and Timeouts](/system-design/patterns/circuit-breaker-and-retries) | Vendor breaker at p95 above 1.5 s or 5% errors | A slow vendor must not hold 25% of answers |
| [Sharding and Partitioning](/system-design/patterns/sharding) | 100 shards by `doc_id` hash, 3 replicas in 3 zones | 20B spans and ≈ 1,110 queries a second |
| [Rate Limiting](/system-design/patterns/rate-limiting) | Research quotas; per-host crawl politeness | Cost attacks; publishers' servers |
| [Durable Workflows and Distributed Job Scheduling](/system-design/patterns/workflows-and-job-scheduling) | Research runs; the recrawl schedule | Runs of 1–4 minutes survive deploys |
| [Idempotency](/system-design/patterns/idempotency) | `Idempotency-Key` on answers and research | A retry returns the same stream or job, charged once |
| [Multi-Region, Active-Active and Failover](/system-design/patterns/multi-region-and-failover) | A read-only standby snapshot in a second region | The region-down rung of the ladder |

## Same design, other systems

| If the interviewer asks for… | What stays the same | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An enterprise search assistant | Spans, the funnel, checks, streaming | Permission-aware retrieval; connectors instead of a crawler: [Enterprise AI Assistant](/genai-system-design/case-studies/conversational-assistant-design) |
| A deep research product | Lead and subagents, tiers, checks | Research is the default route; reports with sections; caps per user, not per answer |
| A search API for agents | Index, ranking, spans, publisher flags | No answer model; sell ranked spans per call |
| 10× traffic and a 100B-page index | The funnel and checks | A tiered index (hot head in memory, tail on SSD in a disk graph); cluster-routed retrieval so a query hits few shards |
| News Q&A for one publisher | Freshness, citations | Its own archive only; licensing is moot |

## How real systems do it

- **2025, Perplexity (its own post):** its Search API serves 200M queries a day from an index of over 200B URLs, with ML-scheduled recrawl, LLM-graded parse rules, self-contained spans and staged ranking ending in cross-encoders. Its vendor-run test puts its API at p50 358 / p95 763 ms and Brave's at 513 / 808 ms.
- **2025, Google:** AI Mode fans one question out into many searches at once, and Deep Search runs hundreds of searches to write a cited report.
- **2025, Anthropic's research system:** a lead agent runs 3–5 subagents in parallel at about 15× the tokens of a chat, beat a single agent by 90.2% on an internal eval, and hands citations to a separate agent afterwards. Token use alone explained 80% of the variance on BrowseComp.
- **2025, supplier risk:** Microsoft retired the Bing Search APIs on 11 August 2025, three months after announcing it, and pointed customers to a grounding service whose outputs cannot be reused in other applications.
- **2025, publishers:** Cloudflare began blocking AI crawlers by default on new domains and launched Pay Per Crawl in July; its Content Signals added `ai-input` in September, and RSL 1.0 was published in December. In August Cloudflare reported that Perplexity crawled with undeclared agents when blocked, which Perplexity disputed.
- **2023 → 2025, citation quality:** a 2023 study of four generative search engines found 51.5% of sentences fully supported by their citations and 74.5% of citations supporting their sentence. In 2025 the Tow Center found over 60% of 1,600 source-identification answers from 8 AI search tools wrong (Perplexity 37%), and the EBU and BBC found significant issues in about half of 3,000+ news answers.
- **Where this design differs:** Perplexity indexes 200B+ URLs because search is its product and it sells the API; at 10M questions a day a 2B-page head plus a rented tail meets the latency targets, and the shadow check says when to grow it. Every fetch here, live or crawled, honours host flags, a stricter stance than a user fetcher that "generally ignores robots.txt" in Perplexity's own documentation.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- **A search vendor slows or fails:** the breaker opens at p95 above 1.5 s or 5% errors over a minute and traffic moves to the second vendor. With both down, answers come from the own index only, with "web results may be less fresh" on news.
- **Index trouble at peak (≈ 1,110 queries a second):** a slow shard is dropped; a lost zone leaves ≈ 740 a second, so 2 queries an answer and research paused; a lost region leaves the 6-hour standby at ≈ 370, 1 query an answer. With index and standby down, 1 API call an answer is ≈ 280 a second inside the 400 burst; beyond that, cached answers only and a "busy" message.
- **The model provider throttles (≈ 197M input tokens a minute at peak):** shed research, then multi-hop, then route mid-size answers to the small model with tighter checks; the overflow model family must already pass the gold set.
- **A news spike on one question:** without single-flight a key at 50 requests a second starts ≈ 240 pipelines before its first answer completes in ≈ 4.8 s; followers attach to the in-flight stream instead.
- **An evergreen answer goes stale on news:** users report `outdated` and the changed-this-week score falls; new fast-tier pages tag entities and evict cached answers that name them, and change is detected by span hash, not page bytes.
- **The model writes more unsupported sentences after an upgrade:** the low-confidence rate climbs from 4.9% towards 15.9% (at 10% unsupported) and research offers swell; the 5% gate on the gold set blocks the release, and the canary rolls it back.
- **A takedown, delisting or opt-out:** filtered at retrieval at once, purged ≤ 24 h, answers citing it evicted ≤ 1 h; shared answers are re-rendered without the removed sentences.

## Interview framework

1. **Minutes 0 to 5:** volume, answer shape, freshness, the latency target, index or API, and the constraint order (citations, then latency, then cost).
2. **Minutes 5 to 10:** numbers: 350 a second, 3 searches each, a 4,105-token prompt, 0.0063 USD a quick answer, the breakeven and the 1 USD case.
3. **Minutes 10 to 20:** Steps 1 to 4: baseline, planner and cache, spans, the funnel; the waterfall with max-of-n.
4. **Minutes 20 to 30:** Steps 5 and 6: checks and restarts; routes and research tiers.
5. **Minutes 30 to 36:** Steps 7 and 8: own index and freshness; publisher controls, safety, evaluation.
6. **Minutes 36 to 45:** one deep dive: index or API, citation verification, or escalation.

**What interviewers listen for:**

- **Mid:** fan-out, spans, a reranker, citations as span numbers, a cache with a TTL by freshness.
- **Senior:** per-path budgets with max-of-n, the checks and their error rates, independent grading, routes and caps, cost per answer.
- **Staff:** the index as a price × volume × latency decision, publisher controls and coverage, the degradation ladder with capacities, 100B pages.

- **Red flags:** "give the LLM a browsing tool"; citations attached after writing; grading citations with the filter; whole pages in the prompt; robots.txt as the only publisher rule; "fail over to the API" without its rate limit.
