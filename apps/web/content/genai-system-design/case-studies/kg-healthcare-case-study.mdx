A clinician inside the electronic health record (EHR) asks, "Is it safe to start this patient on apixaban?"
The answer joins what medicine knows about the drug with what this patient's record says, including facts written only in notes.
The hard parts are deciding who decides (rules or the model), mapping free text and outside-lab units to the right code, and saying "I cannot tell" when an input is missing.
The answer must also cite every claim, be about the right patient, and keep patient data inside legal limits that differ by note.

- Interviewers use this question to see whether you keep safety decisions in code, treat "no alert" as different from "safe", know where protected health information may flow, and can explain why this is decision support and not a diagnostic device.
- This page is educational: it describes clinical decision support, not medical advice. The patient is illustrative, and every volume, latency and unit cost is an estimate, because no production system publishes them.
- In the terms of the concept pages, this is [GraphRAG](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/graphrag) local search without community summaries, with templated traversals instead of free neighbourhood expansion ([Knowledge Graph Systems for AI](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/knowledge-graph-systems)).

## Overview

- **Rules decide, the model explains.** Seven drug-centric templates (interaction, allergy, drug–disease, dosing, duplicate therapy, cumulative risk, coverage) run in code over a versioned knowledge snapshot; the alerts card is rendered from their output, so the model cannot drop or invent an alert.
- **Two layers, joined by codes.** A knowledge layer (≈ 1.0M concepts, ≈ 12M edges, no patient data) held in memory, and a per-patient fact store with lifecycle, units, code-system version and security labels on every fact.
- **Notes become coded facts with their context.** An encoder cascade links 3.2M mentions a day to RxNorm, SNOMED CT and LOINC with assertion status (negated, historical, family); every fact keeps its note, span and model version.
- **Right drug, right patient.** The drug comes from the draft order or an exact RxNorm match first; a chip asks for a tap when the match is weak or a look-alike name is close. Every event carries the patient it was computed for.
- **Abstain by rule, not by the model's mood.** A missing indication, a stale kidney result, an unmapped drug, a restricted note or a question no template covers produces a named gap; the answer never says "safe", only "no alert found in these sources for these inputs".
- **Patient data stays inside boundaries.** Everything online runs in the network's own HIPAA-eligible cloud account; the answer model is hosted under the vendor's business associate agreement; restricted notes follow the EHR's own entitlements on every path.

## Estimates and decision drivers

- **Research honesty:** no product or paper publishes volume, latency, cost or in-use accuracy for a clinical GraphRAG assistant. Every volume, rate, latency, salary, licence fee and unit cost below is an estimate for an illustrative network.
- **Sources that are not estimates:** rule text (HIPAA, Part 2), FDA guidance, the CDS Hooks and FHIR specifications, vendor BAA pages, the Eliquis label and named papers.
- **Prices** are list prices as of November 2026: a mid-size model (Claude Sonnet 5.5 class) 2.00 USD per million input tokens, 0.10 cached, 2.50 to write the 5-minute cache, 10.00 output; a small model (Claude Haiku 5.5 class) 0.10 input, 0.01 cached, 0.50 output.
- **Parallel stages** are budgeted as the slowest of their draws, at p50 and p95, not as one call.

## API design

| Call | Who calls it | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/questions` (SSE response) | Side-panel app (SMART on FHIR launch) | Every question; the patient comes from the launch token's context, never from the request body |
| SSE events `context`, `drug`, `alerts`, `gap`, `sources`, `sentence`, `done`, `error` | Gateway → panel | Every event carries `patient`; `alerts` arrives at ≈ 516 ms, rendered by code; one `sentence` per checked sentence |
| `POST /v1/questions/{id}/confirm-drug` | Panel | The clinician taps the resolved drug, dose or indication |
| FHIRcast `patient-open` / `patient-close`, or close-on-switch | Panel and EHR hub | The panel follows the EHR's current patient; an in-flight answer for another patient is discarded |
| `GET /cds-services`, `POST /cds-services/med-safety-notes` on `order-select` and `order-sign` | EHR | Prefetch required: medication requests, statements and administrations (72 h), allergies, conditions, recent labs for the template LOINC codes |
| `POST /cds-services/med-safety-notes/feedback` | EHR | `accepted` or `overridden` with a reason; ignored cards are measured from display logs |
| `POST /v1/answers/{id}/flags` | Panel | A clinician marks an alert, sentence or linked fact wrong; goes to the pharmacist queue |
| `GET /v1/answers/{id}` | Panel, audit, safety review | The stored answer with the stored content of every input and every version |

- **Identity and patient before data:** the gateway validates the SMART token, takes the patient from its context, checks the care-team relationship (a cached table) and computes the clinician's note entitlements before any PHI read. Break-glass access needs a reason, is logged and reviewed daily.
- **Right patient at display:** a persistent banner shows two identifiers (name and date of birth, never sent to the model); the panel drops any event whose `patient` is not the EHR's current one and closes the stream.
- **Without FHIRcast:** close-on-switch relies on the EHR hosting one app instance per patient context; the app is never embedded in a patient-agnostic frame.
- **Right drug, dose and indication:** coded drug from the draft order first, then an exact RxNorm string match, then embedding candidates; a tap is required on a low score, a close look-alike, or a dose or indication only free text supplies.
- **Until the tap:** a free-text indication makes the dosing rule a gap (AF and VTE differ). A free-text dose does not stop the rules, since the criteria dose needs indication, age, weight and creatinine; only the comparison with the intended dose is marked "unconfirmed".
- **Order cards follow the user's entitlements:** the hook authenticates the EHR by its JWT, so the service reads `context.userId`, looks up that user's entitlements (5 ms) and filters note-derived facts by security label before the join.
- **A restricted fact never reaches a card:** where one would change the result, the card says only "A restricted record may be relevant to this order. Ask pharmacy, or open the chart with break-the-glass". Part 2 facts never contribute without consent on file.
- **Alerts by code, no directive words, no writes:** the explanation cannot add, remove or downgrade an alert, never says "safe" or "start", and a suggestion is a CDS Hooks `create` action the EHR applies only on acceptance (Safety and guardrails lists the rest).
- **Transport:** SSE, because alerts, gaps and sentences must arrive in order on one connection, alerts first; polling adds a round trip per event.
- **Idempotency:** `Idempotency-Key` on questions returns the same stream on retry; `hookInstance` deduplicates retries of one hook call. Cards are deduplicated on user, patient, encounter, draft-order ids, rule and inputs hash for the signing session, so an `order-select` card is not repeated at `order-sign`.
- **Model calls** use covered features only; the gateway blocks Batch, Files, code execution, web fetch and external MCP. Under the vendor's BAA (as of November 2026) covered models keep inputs and outputs for 30 days, and caching and structured outputs are covered only on BAA versions accepted after 1 April 2026.

One table holds every patient fact; the columns carry the lifecycle and context the rules need.

```sql
CREATE TABLE patient_fact (
  patient_key   text,         -- pseudonymous; re-keyed on merge (ADT A40), split on unmerge
  fact_id       text,
  kind          text,         -- 'med_request' | 'med_statement' | 'med_admin' | 'allergy' | 'condition' | 'lab' | 'renal_estimate' | 'note_mention'
  code_system   text,         -- 'rxnorm' | 'snomed' | 'loinc' | 'icd10cm'
  code_version  text,         -- release the code was valid in; re-mapped on each snapshot swap
  code          text,         -- '1364430' (apixaban, IN)
  assertion     text,         -- 'present' | 'negated' | 'historical' | 'family' | 'hypothetical' | 'uncertain'
                              -- per kind: a historical allergy reaction stays an active allergy unless refuted
  status        text,         -- 'active' | 'on-hold' | 'stopped' | 'completed' | 'entered-in-error' | 'refuted'
  allergy       jsonb,        -- {"type": "allergy", "criticality": "high", "verification": "confirmed", "reaction": "rash"}; NKDA is its own fact
  quantity      jsonb,        -- UCUM-normalised: {"value": 1.595, "unit": "mg/dL", "original": "141 umol/L"}
  derived       jsonb,        -- renal estimates: {"method": "CrCl_CG", "inputs": [...]} or {"method": "eGFR_CKD-EPI-2021"}
  effective_at  timestamptz,  -- when it was true clinically
  recorded_at   timestamptz,
  valid_to      timestamptz,  -- note-derived medication facts expire into 'historical' unless reconfirmed
  superseded_by text,         -- amendments, corrections, entered-in-error
  security      text[],       -- FHIR meta.security codes: confidentiality, sensitivity (substance use, psychiatry)
  source        jsonb,        -- {"fhir": "MedicationRequest/77", "content": {...}} or
                              -- {"note": "DocumentReference/31", "span": [412, 437], "linker": "lnk-2026.10", "score": 0.91,
                              --  "first_seen_note": "DocumentReference/12"}  (copy-forward: only the first occurrence counts)
  verified_by   text,         -- null, or the clinician who accepted it
  PRIMARY KEY (patient_key, fact_id)
) PARTITION BY HASH (patient_key);
```

- **Access patterns:** expand from the drug on the in-memory snapshot; read one patient's facts filtered by kind, status and security label; exact vector search over one patient's chunks; fetch passages by concept id.
- **Write patterns:** HL7 v2 feeds upsert coded facts within 30 s p50; the note pipeline appends mentions within 15 min p95; merges re-key a patient's rows in one transaction.
- **Units:** labs are converted to UCUM at ingestion (µmol/L ÷ 88.4 = mg/dL); an unknown unit is stored as a gap, never as a number.

## Building the architecture, step by step

- We start from one model call with the chart and add a part only when a requirement or a driver forces it.
- Each figure shows the design after that step; the step's new parts are outlined and the moving dots show its flow.
- The PHI boundary is not drawn as a zone: every zone except the offline knowledge build (no PHI) and the vendor model (under a BAA) runs in the network's own cloud account. Deep dive 1 draws it.

### Step 1: Ask a model with the chart, for the right patient (F1, N6)

The baseline: the clinician's question and recent facts from the chart go to one model, for the patient the EHR has open.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={1} caption="Step 1: the panel asks through a gateway that takes the patient from the SMART token and checks the care team; the orchestrator reads the chart over FHIR and prompts the answer model." />

- **Adds:** the clinician panel, the EHR, the gateway, the orchestrator, the answer model.
- **Flow:**
  1. The panel (with a two-identifier patient banner) sends the question to the gateway.
  2. The gateway validates the SMART token, takes the patient from its context, checks the care team and writes one AuditEvent.
  3. The orchestrator reads recent facts over FHIR and puts them in a prompt.
  4. The answer streams back, every event stamped with its patient.
- **Why this and not that:** identity and patient before any data read (P11); the simplest thing that answers (P1). The patient never comes from the request body, so a bug in the panel cannot ask about someone else.
- **The gap:** models are weakest at ruling interactions out (ChatGPT-4 specificity 0.519 against Micromedex, a 2023 paper), which is what "is it safe?" asks. Answers vary between runs and nothing cites a source.

### Step 2: Rules over a knowledge snapshot decide the alerts (F1, F5, N1)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={2} caption="Step 2: an offline build turns vocabularies, a licensed drug-knowledge base and labels into a versioned snapshot; the rules engine holds it in memory and returns the alerts." />

- **Adds:** the knowledge build, the knowledge snapshot, the rules engine.
- **Flow:**
  1. Vocabularies (RxNorm, SNOMED CT, LOINC), a licensed drug-knowledge base, FDA labels and crosswalks become one versioned snapshot.
  2. Patient facts carry RxNorm, SNOMED CT and LOINC codes and UCUM units.
  3. Seven drug-centric expansions run from the drug; code joins them with the patient's facts.
  4. Phase 1 runs the dosing rules and produces the criteria dose; phase 2 runs dose-dependent rules (inhibitors, inducers) on that dose.
  5. Code renders the alerts card.
- **Why this and not that:** rules in code, not in the prompt (P10), so the same facts and snapshot give the same alerts; dosing rules are multi-fact predicates because labels state doses as combinations of criteria.
- **The gap:** facts written only in notes ("PCN allergy, rash 2019") are invisible to rules that read coded data.

### Step 3: Notes become coded facts with context and lifecycle (F4, N3)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={3} caption="Step 3: signed notes, 80,400 a day, run through note NLP, which writes coded facts with their note, span, model version and assertion to the patient fact store." />

- **Adds:** note NLP (recognition, linking, assertion), the patient fact store.
- **Flow:**
  1. A signed note is split into sections and mentions.
  2. Each mention gets an exact match, or the top 20 candidates from ≈ 3M names.
  3. A cross-encoder disambiguates the ambiguous 25% in context ("PCN" = penicillin).
  4. An assertion classifier sets present, negated, historical, family, hypothetical or uncertain, with per-kind meaning.
  5. The fact is stored with its note id, span, model version, score, security labels and first occurrence; amendments and entered-in-error supersede it.
- **Why this and not that:** provenance on every fact (P14); a cheap encoder cascade over 56M note tokens a day (P4), about 9,050 GPU-seconds. Deep dive 2 draws a note going through it.
- **The gap:** the question's drug may be free text, and the explanation has no source text to quote.

### Step 4: Resolve the drug, expand, then retrieve passages (F1, F2)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={4} caption="Step 4: a drug resolver and planner turns the question into a coded drug, the rules expand from it, and passages attached to the alert paths are fetched and reranked." />

- **Adds:** the drug resolver and planner, the cross-encoder reranker, the passage index.
- **Flow:**
  1. An encoder classifier (a small model on the unsure 15%) runs alongside six FHIR reads.
  2. The drug comes from the draft order or an exact RxNorm match; embedding candidates are a fallback, with a tap to confirm.
  3. Expansions and the join produce the `alerts` event.
  4. Passages attached to the alert paths are retrieved by concept id and reranked; the context is 4,470 tokens.
- **Why this and not that:** exact before similar on the safety path. Similarity seeds survive lower-cased names (KG-RAG 2024: 97% against Cypher's 0%) and, by the same mechanism, misspellings, which can land on a look-alike drug.
- **The gap:** the model can still write past its evidence, and a missing input looks like "no alert".

The graph slice shows the two layers this step joins and the two alert paths for the apixaban example.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/graph-slice" caption="Each edge names its source, and the dosing rule reads several facts at once; the interaction rule runs on the criteria dose 2.5 mg, so it says “avoid”, not “reduce by 50%”." />

- **How to read it:** the top band is the knowledge layer (no PHI), the bottom band this patient's facts, joined by RxNorm and SNOMED CT codes; every edge names its source (the drug-knowledge release, or Eliquis label section 2: 2.1 for the AF dose, 2.6 for inhibitors).

**The numbers:**

- Creatinine 141 µmol/L ÷ 88.4 = 1.595 mg/dL, compared unrounded against ≥ 1.5; with age 82 and 58 kg that is 3 of 3 criteria, and the label's AF dose for at least 2 is 2.5 mg twice daily.
- Itraconazole is a combined P-gp and strong CYP3A4 inhibitor: the label says reduce by 50% at 5 or 10 mg and avoid at 2.5 mg, so phase 2 on the criteria dose gives "avoid".

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Run the inhibitor rule on the intended 5 mg instead of the criteria dose and the card says "reduce by 50%", which lands on the 2.5 mg the label then says to avoid with itraconazole. Rules compose in two phases, and a 32-case dosing grid tests the composition.
</Callout>

### Step 5: Abstain by rule; check every sentence (F6, N2, N4)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={5} caption="Step 5: abstention and checks apply coverage rules per template and check every sentence against its cited rules and passages; the answer is stored with its inputs." />

- **Adds:** abstention and checks; the answers, provenance and audit store.
- **Flow:**
  1. Coverage rules per template check that each input is present, current for the setting, in UCUM units, mapped, not restricted and not contradicted.
  2. Anything that fails becomes a `gap` event, such as "dose check needs the indication".
  3. Each sentence is checked against its cited rules, edges and passages: shown, or dropped with a restart.
  4. The answer is stored with the content of every input and every version.
- **Why this and not that:** constrain the output (P9) and carry provenance (P14). Models abstain poorly on their own (MetaMedQA, 2025), so the gap comes from rules, not from model confidence. Deep dive 3 draws the inputs per template.
- **The gap:** clinicians decide at order entry, not in a side panel.

### Step 6: Order cards in the EHR (F3, N5)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={6} caption="Step 6: the EHR’s CDS Hooks calls reach a hook service that runs the same templates on the rules engine and returns cards only for what notes add." />

- **Adds:** the CDS Hooks service.
- **Flow:**
  1. `order-select` warms the cache and returns only cards with the CDS Hooks critical indicator; `order-sign` arrives with the required prefetch.
  2. The user's entitlements come from `context.userId`; note facts are filtered by security label before the join.
  3. The templates run on coded facts with the EHR's local configuration, then again with the permitted note facts.
  4. Only the difference becomes a card, labelled "found only in notes", with a suggestion to code the fact; feedback is recorded.
- **Why this and not that:** the same rules on every path (P10); the clinician stays in charge, and a suggestion changes the chart only if they accept it (P15).
- **The gap:** drug knowledge and vocabularies change, an edge can be wrong, and an urgent warning cannot wait a week.

The order-card figure shows the two runs and the difference that becomes a card.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/order-cards" caption="The rules run twice, on coded facts with the EHR’s own configuration and then with the note facts this user may see; only the difference interrupts the prescriber, about 1.5 cards per 100 orders." />

- **How to read it:** step 1 filters note facts by the user's entitlements; step 2 runs the rules on what the EHR already shows (A) and with permitted note facts (B); step 3 keeps B minus A and the restricted-record card; step 4 is the budget and feedback.

**The numbers:**

- Interruptive means the CDS Hooks critical indicator: ≈ 10% of 768 delta cards, 77 a day (0.15 per 100 orders); the card is ready at ≈ 64 ms p50, 87 p95.
- The shift budget (3 a prescriber, illustrative) applies to interruptive types other than high-criticality allergies and contraindicated pairs, such as a major interaction tiered critical by local policy; hitting it flags the type for pharmacy review, never demotes a card.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Show every alert the rules find and the prescriber sees a second copy of each EHR alert, in a different tier. Clinicians already override 49% to 96% of drug alerts (a 2006 review), so duplicates train them to dismiss the one card that matters.
</Callout>

### Step 7: Keep knowledge current and versioned (F5, N7)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={7} caption="Step 7: a release gate sits between the knowledge build and the snapshot, so every vendor or vocabulary release passes regression before the atomic swap." />

- **Adds:** the release gate and regression suite.
- **Flow:**
  1. A vendor or NLM release arrives and is diffed against the live snapshot.
  2. Patient facts are re-mapped through the release history (inactive code → replacement); unmapped counts are reported.
  3. Every weekly or monthly release runs the deterministic gates: 150 high-priority cases, every pair as orderable products, 2,000 contexts, the grid and the slices. The 600-answer clinician grade runs monthly and on any model, prompt or template change.
  4. The snapshot is swapped atomically. Urgent path: a pharmacist-authored local edge with committee provenance goes live within 24 h after a reduced regression.
- **Why this and not that:** evaluate before release (P16). A release that retires a code would otherwise silence every rule that used it, without an error.
- **The gap:** nobody yet knows whether answers are right, for whom, or which links fail.

### Step 8: Evaluate, review, close the loop, inside the boundary (F7)

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/architecture" step={8} caption="Step 8: evaluation and review read answers, flags and overrides from the answers store and feed the release gate; sets are annotated inside the PHI zone." />

- **Adds:** evaluation and review.
- **Flow:**
  1. Sets are annotated inside the PHI zone by workforce annotators: linking, interactions, slices, abstention and 600 clinician-graded questions.
  2. A 12-month retrospective replay and subgroup slices feed the gates.
  3. Flags, overrides and ignore rates go to a pharmacist queue.
  4. Fixes become link corrections, knowledge change requests and new eval cases.
- **Why this and not that:** close the loop (P15); keep PHI inside a boundary even for evaluation (P17), because de-identifying free text misses some identifiers.
- **The gap:** none; the gate decides each release.

## Component choices and why

- Every box in the final diagram: what we picked, why, and why the usual alternatives lose. Driver ids (G) refer to the drivers table; F and N to the requirements.

### Build or buy: buy the reference answer, build the delta

- **Choice:** use the EHR vendor's assistant with licensed reference content for reference answers (we assume chart summaries too); build only the note-derived delta at order entry and the network's own rules and tiers.
- **Why:** the reference answer is a commodity the content owner sells (G4); the checks of facts written only in notes, against this network's local alert configuration, are what no product publishes (G1).
- **Why not build everything:** a team rebuilds a licensed product and still has to evaluate it.
- **Why not buy everything:** no vendor publishes checks of note-only facts against local tiers; revisit when one does.

### Who decides: rules over a curated snapshot; the model explains

- **Choice:** seven templates in the rules engine decide every alert; the model writes only the explanation.
- **Why:** the same facts and snapshot give the same alerts, and each alert names its rule and source (G1, G2, G8). FDA lists interaction and allergy notifications based on current labelling as non-device CDS examples.
- **Evidence (papers):** against Micromedex (2023) ChatGPT-4 had sensitivity 0.915 but specificity 0.519; a 2025 preprint on 750 scenarios found accuracy and self-consistency fall as drugs are added. A 2025 study reports models "cannot reliably exclude" interactions (read from its abstract record).
- **The templates:** interaction (clinical-drug level, so topical ketoconazole is not oral), allergy and cross-reactivity (by reaction type), drug–disease, dosing (multi-fact predicates), duplicate therapy, cumulative risk (QT with the latest QTc and potassium, bleeding, serotonergic, CNS depression) and coverage.
- **Why not the model with a "check interactions" tool:** it may not call it, and "no tool call" reads as "no alert".
- **Why not the model alone with the chart:** ruling out is exactly what it cannot do reliably, and its answer changes between runs.

### Drug knowledge: a licensed curated base, diffed against the EHR

- **Choice:** license a curated drug-knowledge base; vocabularies from NLM and SNOMED International; diff every card against the EHR's knowledge-base release and local suppressions.
- **Why:** NLM's free interaction API was discontinued on 2 January 2024; the 15-pair high-priority list is a floor, not a base; DrugBank is non-commercial unless licensed (terms read November 2026) (G1, G6).
- **Licence terms:** sending monograph text to a model vendor may need an AI-use rider, so until then only FDA labels and openly licensed guidelines are passages; only structured fields of the licensed base reach the model.
- **Why not extract interactions from labels with an LLM:** hallucinated edges, and thousands to validate by hand.
- **Why not public research graphs (SPOKE, Hetionet, PrimeKG):** research use and text-mined edges.

### Knowledge store: an in-memory graph snapshot in the rules service

- **Choice:** the ≈ 6 GB snapshot (compact layout) is loaded whole into each of three rules-service instances and swapped atomically per release.
- **Why:** read-only and versioned; seven expansions in ≈ 30 ms p50; paths with per-hop provenance for the explanation (G3, G8). Templates are drug-centric, so expansion runs alongside the fact read and caches per drug per snapshot.
- **Why not a graph database server:** one more store and a network hop for a dataset that fits in memory.
- **Why not PostgreSQL closure tables:** the vendor's native shape and the honest runner-up; paths with provenance per hop become multi-join queries. Kept as the fallback if memory grows past one instance.
- **Why not RDF with a reasoner:** SNOMED ships its classification as inferred relationships, so no reasoner needs to run.

### Patient store: PostgreSQL facts and pgvector chunks, per patient

- **Choice:** <TechIcon id="oss/postgresql" /> PostgreSQL partitioned by `patient_key`, holding coded facts, note-derived facts and note chunks with INT8 vectors; exact search inside one patient.
- **Why:** every read is one patient; ≈ 412 chunks on average (≈ 10,000 in the tail) make exact search single-digit milliseconds; embeddings are PHI, so one PHI store, not two (G6, G9).
- **Why not all patients in a graph store:** ≈ 11B more rows as edges, PHI mixed with public knowledge, and access control per node.
- **Why not a separate vector search engine for chunks:** a second PHI store and approximate recall, for no gain.
- **Why not FHIR reads only:** note-derived facts and lifecycle do not exist in the EHR's API.

### Note linking: an encoder cascade, tuned locally

- **Choice:** exact and dictionary match → SapBERT-class candidates (top 20 of ≈ 3M names, filtered by semantic type) → a cross-encoder on the ambiguous 25% → an assertion classifier.
- **Why:** ≈ 9,050 GPU-seconds a day and ≈ 19,300 USD for the backfill (G4, G9); locally tuned MedCAT reached F1 above 0.94 against 0.448–0.738 on open data (paper, 2021).
- **Why not a hosted LLM extractor:** ≈ 116,100 USD for the backfill, and ten years of notes go to a vendor.
- **Why not a small open LLM inside the PHI zone:** a fair option (≈ 60,900 USD backfill, one GPU a day), chosen against on hallucinated facts and per-slice accuracy; revisit if it wins the linking slices.
- **Why not MetaMap or cTAKES as they are:** 59.0% and 47.1% end to end on n2c2 2019.

### Drug resolution: exact first, embedding as fallback, a tap on doubt

- **Choice:** the coded draft order or the EHR's medication picker → exact and normalised RxNorm match → embedding candidates; the chip always shows; a tap is required on a low score or a close look-alike (≈ 3% of safety questions, illustrative).
- **Why:** look-alike names (for example hydroxyzine and hydralazine, on ISMP's list) turn a silent mis-link into a confident answer about the wrong drug (G2).
- **Why not embedding seeds first:** they survive misspelling, which is the problem: "hydroxyzine" misspelt can land nearest to "hydralazine".
- **Why not free text-to-Cypher on the safety path:** an empty result looks like "no alert".

### Planner: an encoder classifier first, the small model on 15%

- **Choice:** an encoder intent and mention classifier (≈ 20 ms) on 85% of questions; a small model with a PHI-free JSON schema on the multi-intent or low-confidence 15%.
- **Why:** it removes a vendor call from the critical path for most questions (G3); the head is 374 ms instead of 431 at p50.
- **Why not the small model always:** + 57 ms at p50 on the head and one more vendor dependency on the safety path.
- **Why not rules only:** "why was warfarin stopped and can she restart it?" carries two intents a keyword rule splits badly.

### Answer model: a hosted mid-size model under the vendor's BAA

- **Choice:** a mid-size model (Claude Sonnet 5.5 class, as of November 2026) on the vendor's API under its BAA, in the network's HIPAA-eligible cloud setup.
- **Why:** ≈ 74 USD a day for every question; the BAA covers the API, and prompt caching and structured outputs on BAA versions accepted after 1 April 2026 (as of November 2026) (G4, G9).
- **Why not self-hosting a 70B-class model:** ≈ 480 USD a day (6.5×), a lower quality tier that must pass the same clinician gate, and an operations team.
- **Why not de-identify the prompt and use any model:** prompts still hold dates and rare combinations; 87% of the US population was likely unique on 5-digit ZIP, sex and date of birth (Sweeney, 2000, on 1990 census data).
- **Standby:** a second model family on a HIPAA-eligible service, used only if it passes the same gate; otherwise alerts without explanation.

### Checks and abstention: coverage rules, then sentence checks

- **Choice:** each template names its inputs and their recency, units and mapping; a failure is a named gap. Each sentence then passes rules (its drugs, doses and values appear in a cited rule, edge or passage; nothing contradicts an alert) and a 0.8B entailment checker.
- **Why:** FDA asks the output to show missing inputs (G2, N4); models abstain poorly (MetaMedQA, 2025; AbstentionBench, 2025: reasoning tuning made it 24% worse). Checks cost 60 ms a sentence (G3); ≈ 0.16% shown unsupported is optimistic, so the gate measures it.
- **Conflicts:** sources that disagree on severity show both, and the more severe drives the card. Values that disagree in a dose criterion take the later valid specimen by collection time; if they cannot be ordered, the criterion is a gap, because a lower anticoagulant dose has its own harm.
- **Why not model confidence:** it is not calibrated to "an input is missing", and it differs between runs.
- **Why not an LLM judge per sentence:** ≈ 200–300 ms each, ten times a question.

## Evaluation and release gates

| Offline set | Size | Source | What it checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High-priority interactions | 150 (15 ONC pairs × 10 orderable variants) | The 2012 high-priority list | Every case alerts |
| Every interaction pair as orderable products | ≈ 300,000 pairs × 10 products = 3M | The licensed base, through the crosswalks | 100% alert: ≈ 25 CPU-hours, ≈ 23 minutes on 64 cores |
| Patient contexts (dose, route, renal state) | 2,000 | Stratified by class, weighted by 12-month order frequency | 2,000 of 2,000 |
| Dosing grid | 32 (0–3 criteria × inhibitor yes or no × intended 5 or 2.5 mg × AF or VTE) | Authored | Two-phase composition |
| Rule slices | Drug–disease 500, allergy cross-reactivity 300, cumulative risk 100, route and form 100, negatives 1,000 | Authored | Each at target; negatives measure alert burden |
| Data-quality slices | Outside-lab units 200, allergy semantics 150, unmapped codes 100, conflicts 50, untemplated questions 50, restricted notes 50, wrong patient and merges 20 | Authored on real charts in the PHI zone | Each must produce the right gap, never silence |
| Linking and assertion | 200 notes, ≈ 8,000 mentions (1,600 medication, 3,000 problem) | Network notes, double-annotated by workforce annotators | N3 by slice |
| Clinician-graded answers | 600 (300 safety, 200 record, 100 reference) | Real charts in the PHI zone | Two clinicians each, a pharmacist adjudicates |
| Abstention | 150 + 150 answerable controls | Missing, stale, restricted, unmapped, conflicting inputs | N4 |
| Checker | 2,000 sentences, 500 unsupported | Labelled | False accept 5% ± 1.9 points |

- **Why the pair set is exhaustive:** a deterministic test this cheap is run in full, not sampled; a broken crosswalk silences a whole class, which a sample can miss. A pair the network suppresses by committee is excluded with its record.
- **What each set proves:** 150 of 150 is a must-pass list (15 pairs × 10 products), not a sample; the exhaustive gate is the coverage claim, and it cannot find a pair the base lacks (pharmacist flags and the replay cover that). 0 misses in 2,000 contexts bounds misses below 0.15% per order at observed frequencies.
- **Clinician rubric:** correctness, omission (major or minor), possible harm, citation support, gap handling, no directive, right patient; kappa ≥ 0.6 before a grade counts; acceptable ≥ 95% (± 1.7 points).
- **Checker rates (illustrative):** 3% unsupported, 5% false accepts, 3% false rejects give 5.76% drops a sentence; 55.3% of answers have no drop, 33.8% one, 9.3% two, 1.7% three or more (the explanation is replaced by the rules card).
- **Replay and subgroups:** 12 months of orders (≈ 18.7M) with known pharmacist interventions, replayed in the PHI zone; linking accuracy and alert rates by age band, sex, race and ethnicity, note language and outside-lab patients.

## Deep dives

### Where PHI may flow, and which model runs where

- **Minimum necessary:** it does not bind a disclosure to a provider for treatment (the clinician reading the answer), but it does shape what goes to a business associate and what workforce roles may read. The care-team check is the role-based limit; the 4,470-token prompt is the minimum for the vendor.
- **The BAA chain:** every processor that sees PHI (cloud account, model vendor, log sink) needs a business associate agreement, and only the vendor's covered features may be used.
- **Pseudonymised is not de-identified:** structured facts say "the patient" with relative dates, but dates and rare combinations still identify; note text in record prompts is not pseudonymised at all. Both are PHI under the BAA.
- **Embeddings are PHI:** vec2text (paper, 2023) recovered 92% of 32-token inputs exactly, so the chunk vectors live in the PHI store.

<AnimatedDiagram spec="animated/kg-health/phi-boundary" caption="Patient data stays in the network’s account except the prompt, which goes to a vendor model under a BAA with covered features only; the knowledge build holds no PHI, and only de-identified copies ever leave." />

- **How to read it:** violet is the PHI zone in the network's account, green the vendor model under the BAA, blue the knowledge build with no PHI, the bottom strip de-identified copies; step 4 adds restricted notes and ids-only logs.

**The numbers:**

- One flow crosses to the vendor: the 4,470-token prompt. Under the BAA the vendor keeps covered-model inputs and outputs for 30 days, plus the KV cache for its time to live (vendor BAA page, as of November 2026); both are PHI stores under the BAA, and caching is covered only on BAA versions accepted after 1 April 2026: check ours.
- Self-hosting the answer model would keep that flow inside, at ≈ 480 USD a day against ≈ 74 hosted (6.5×), for a lower quality tier; it wins only if policy forbids the vendor or the BAA drops a feature we need.
- De-identified copies use Expert Determination with surrogates and per-patient date shifts; the best tool in a vendor-authored 2025 comparison scored 96% F1 on 48 documents, so some identifiers are missed and a measured residual goes in the determination.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  "We de-identified by removing names" fails on the first note: dates, places and rare combinations remain, and Safe Harbor keeps only the year of dates, which breaks drug timelines. Evaluate inside the PHI zone with workforce annotators instead.
</Callout>

### From a note to a coded fact, and its lifecycle

- **Lifecycle:** copy-forward text counts only at its first note; amendments and entered-in-error supersede; note-derived medication facts expire into "historical" unless reconfirmed.
- **Low-confidence facts do not interrupt;** they show as "possible, from note, unverified". A low-confidence high-criticality allergy gets a non-interruptive "possible allergy in notes: verify" card, and the allergy-semantics slice measures how often.

**How to read it:**

- Three mentions: warfarin and GI bleed match exactly or by synonym; "PCN" takes the top 20 candidates, and the cross-encoder reads "allergy" and "rash" and picks penicillin.

### Abstention and provenance: "no alert" is not "safe"

- **Rates (illustrative):** 8% of safety questions carry at least one named gap, 2% cannot run the rules at all, and 1.7% of explanations are replaced by the rules card.
- **Conflicts:** when the base says major and the label says monitor, both show and the more severe drives the card. Two creatinines that disagree take the later valid specimen by collection time; if they cannot be ordered, both show with lab and units and the gap is "creatinine results disagree", since no dose is the safe side.
- **Untemplated questions** (peri-procedural anticoagulation) get "no rule covers this question type; no safety check run", with a reference answer marked as not patient-checked.

### Worked example: no kidney result in 90 days

1. An outpatient on 17 November 2026 asks about apixaban for AF; her last creatinine is 140 days old, outside the 90-day outpatient window.
2. Age 82 (≥ 80) and weight 58 kg (≤ 60) are current, so 2 of 3 criteria are met without creatinine; the rule still reaches the label's 2.5 mg twice daily.
3. Had she been 75 and 58 kg, only 1 known criterion is met and creatinine decides between 5 and 2.5 mg, so the rule returns the gap "renal input not current: creatinine needed for the AF dose criteria".
4. In that case the interaction alert with itraconazole still shows, worded for both doses (reduce by 50% at 5 or 10 mg; avoid at 2.5 mg), because phase 2 has no criteria dose.
5. The answer cites the label for each criterion and never says "safe"; the gap event costs nothing extra, and the model call is still ≈ 0.011 USD.

## How real systems do it

- **No product or paper publishes latency, volume or in-use accuracy** for a clinical GraphRAG assistant; products exist, and their makers' announcements are labelled as such.
- **2026 (September), Epic and Wolters Kluwer:** UpToDate content is embedded in Epic's Art clinician assistant ("Evidence in Art") and will help power Epic Agent Factory; UpToDate Expert AI launched in 2025 (vendor announcements).
- **2026 (as of November), model hosting:** Anthropic signs a BAA for its first-party API with listed covered features (prompt caching and structured outputs on BAA versions accepted after 1 April 2026, but not Batch, Files or code execution), and covered models require 30-day retention; AWS lists Bedrock among HIPAA-eligible services.
- **2025, benchmarks:** MedHELM (121 clinical tasks, 29 clinicians) found models scored lower on clinical decision support (0.56–0.72) than on note generation; a 750-scenario interaction benchmark found "uneven" capabilities that fall as drugs are added.
- **2024, research graphs and retrieval:** KG-RAG over SPOKE (41 sources, ≈ 42M nodes) linked entities by embedding similarity and cut prompt tokens by 53.9% against Cypher retrieval; MedRAG's MIRAGE (7,663 questions) showed gains of up to 18%. NLM discontinued its free interaction API on 2 January 2024.
- **2021, MedCAT:** trained on ≈ 17M records and evaluated at three London hospitals, with F1 above 0.94 after local tuning.
- **Where this design differs:** research systems let the model reason over the graph; here the graph is read by fixed templates in code, because a prescriber's "is it safe?" needs the same answer every time and a named source.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- **Vendor model slow or down:** p95 over 3 s or 5% errors in a minute trips the breaker; the standby family if it passed the gate, else the alerts card and evidence with "explanation unavailable". The order path has no model.
- **Rules service down:** the other two instances hold the whole snapshot; all down, questions say "rules unavailable; no safety check run" and hooks return no cards, so the EHR's own checks remain (fail open).
- **FHIR slow:** 600 ms deadline per read (lab 900); on timeout the projection with its time ("medications as of 10:37"); a failed allergy read is a gap, never "no allergies".
- **A release retires codes:** facts re-mapped through the release history, unmapped counts reported; an unmapped drug is a gap, never silence. You notice it in the release gate's unmapped slice.
- **A wrong knowledge edge reported:** a pharmacist confirms it, an urgent local edge or suppression goes live within 24 h, and affected answers are found by edge id.
- **Note pipeline backlog:** order cards fall back to coded-only and answers say "notes after 09:10 not yet read".
- **Alert fatigue rising:** acceptance below 30% for an interruptive type, or the shift budget hit often; downgrade the type after pharmacist review.

## Interview framework

1. **Minutes 0 to 5:** users, population and setting; question types; "the EHR already checks coded interactions"; build or buy; the order of constraints.
2. **Minutes 5 to 10:** numbers: 7,200 questions, 51,200 orders and ≈ 123,000 hook calls a day; a 4,470-token context; 0.011 USD a question; people are 86% of 14,200 USD a day.
3. **Minutes 10 to 20:** steps 1 to 4: the baseline and why it fails; rules over the snapshot with dosing predicates; notes to coded facts; exact-first drug resolution; the waterfall with max-of-n.
4. **Minutes 20 to 30:** steps 5 and 6: abstention by coverage rules, sentence checks, provenance; delta cards with an interruption budget.
5. **Minutes 30 to 36:** steps 7 and 8: versioned knowledge with an urgent path; evaluation inside the PHI zone.
6. **Minutes 36 to 45:** one deep dive: PHI flow and model placement, note linking, or abstention.

**What interviewers listen for:**

- **Mid:** two layers joined by codes, rules for safety, negation, FHIR, citations, units.
- **Senior:** coverage-based abstention, delta cards and alert fatigue, linking accuracy by slice, allergy semantics, right patient, the BAA chain and embeddings as PHI, per-type latency with max-of-n.
- **Staff:** FDA criteria and what breaks them, Part 2 and segmentation, knowledge-release regression and code mapping, multi-EHR growth, build or buy, a cost that is mostly people.

**Red flags:**

- "The LLM checks for interactions"; "it's safe" as an output; a single-lab dosing edge; units ignored.
- PHI sent to an API without a BAA; "we de-identified by removing names"; text-to-Cypher deciding safety.
- A second copy of every EHR alert; an answer not stamped with its patient.
