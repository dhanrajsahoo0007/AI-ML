Vector RAG answers questions from a few chunks, missing whole-corpus themes. GraphRAG builds a knowledge graph with an LLM, groups entities into communities, and answers global questions from their summaries.

## Overview

- **GraphRAG** (Edge et al., 2024, Microsoft) uses an LLM to extract **entities and relations** from every chunk, finds **communities** of closely linked entities, and writes a **summary per community** ahead of time.
- **Local search** starts at the entities a question names and gathers their neighbours, linked chunks and community summary. **Global search** map-reduces over all community summaries to answer whole-corpus questions.
- In the paper, global search beat vector RAG on **comprehensiveness (72–83% win rate)** and **diversity (62–82%)** for corpus-wide questions over about 1 million tokens of podcast transcripts and 1.7 million tokens of news.
- The price is **indexing**: an LLM reads every token at least once and writes every summary. Plain vector RAG embeds each chunk once.
- Use it when users ask global or relationship questions over a slow-changing corpus. For "what does article X say?", vector RAG is cheaper and as good.

## Mental model

- **The 6,000-ticket table:** Imagine 6,000 support tickets on a table; vector search is a librarian who pulls the five tickets that sound most like your question.
- **The sampling trap:** Ask "why do customers get locked out?" and vector search gives five lockout tickets—true, but not a picture of the whole pile.
- **Topic briefs (GraphRAG):** GraphRAG first has an analyst read every ticket, sort them into piles by topic, and write a one-page brief on each pile.
- **Corpus-wide reading:** A question about the whole table is then answered by reading the briefs, not by sampling five tickets.

## Core concepts

- **Running example:** Brightpost's support tickets for one quarter—**6,000 tickets** of about 500 tokens each, 3 million tokens in all (illustrative).
- **Articles vs. tickets:** The help-centre articles used on the other RAG pages say how things should work; the tickets say what actually goes wrong.

### Why vector RAG misses global questions

- **Top-k chunk limits:** Vector search returns the top k chunks most similar to the question (top 5 on these pages), which works only when the answer lives in those chunks.
- **Count and ranking questions:** "What are the most common reasons customers get locked out?" has no single chunk that answers it; the answer is a count and a ranking across thousands of tickets, whereas the top 5 lockout tickets are a tiny sample of 5 out of 6,000.
- **Query-focused summarisation:** Edge et al. call this query-focused summarisation—summarising the corpus with a question in mind—where retrieving a few chunks cannot see the whole corpus.
- **Multi-hop gap:** The [vector-vs-graph figure on Knowledge Graph Systems](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/knowledge-graph-systems) shows the other gap, multi-hop questions, where the answer is a link between two facts in different documents.

    - The groups have no text yet that a model could read.

    For each community an LLM writes a report from its entities, relations and claims, bottom up, so higher levels build

  </StepCard>

**The numbers:**

- **Extraction and gleaning calls (illustrative):** 6,000 chunks give 6,000 extraction calls; the paper also runs "gleaning" passes that ask the model whether it missed entities, so one extra pass each makes 12,000 calls that read about 6 million tokens.
- **Vector RAG comparison:** Vector RAG over the same tickets runs the 3 million tokens once through an embedding model.
- **Measured paper figure:** Indexing the 1-million-token podcast set took 281 minutes with GPT-4 Turbo.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Extraction is a model's reading, not a fact. If one ticket says "2FA", another "two-factor" and a third
  "authenticator", the graph gets three nodes and three weak communities instead of one strong one. Fix: give the
  extractor a short list of entity types and canonical names, and merge near-duplicate entities before community
  detection, as in [entity resolution](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/knowledge-graph-systems).
</Callout>

### Local search versus global search

A question that names something starts from that thing. A question about the whole corpus starts from all the summaries.

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/rag/graphrag-local-vs-global"
  stepper
  caption="Local search starts from the entities a question names; global search reads every community summary, so it can answer questions no single chunk or entity holds."
/>

**How to read it:**

- **Step 1 (local search):** "backup codes" matches the entity Backup code; the search walks one hop out and collects related entities, the tickets that mention it, and its community's summary, then ranks them to fit one context window.
- **Step 2 (global search map step):** Community summaries at the chosen level are shuffled into batches that fill a context window (8,000 tokens in the paper), and each batch goes to the model with the question (the figure draws one summary per call), returning a partial answer and a helpfulness score from 0 to 100.
- **Global search reduce step:** Answers scored 0 are dropped; the rest are sorted by score and packed into one context for the final answer (the scores in the figure are illustrative).

**The numbers:**

- **Token reduction vs. source text:** In the paper, root-level summaries (C0) needed over 97% fewer context tokens than map-reducing over the source text: 26,657 against 1,014,611 tokens for the podcast set.
- **Efficiency and win rate:** Across the conditions the paper compared (lower levels and source-text summarisation), C0 needed 9 to 43 times fewer tokens per query, and it still won 72% of comparisons with vector RAG on comprehensiveness.

## Architecture

A GraphRAG system has an offline indexer and an online query router.

- **Indexer:** chunker → extraction LLM → entity merge → graph store → community detection → summary LLM. Its outputs are tables of entities, relations, communities, community reports and the source chunks, plus embeddings of entity descriptions so a question can find its entities.
- **Router:** decides per question. Questions that name entities go to local search; whole-corpus questions go to global search; plain lookups ("what does the reset article say?") go to ordinary vector search. Microsoft's open-source library exposes these as `local`, `global`, `basic` and a fourth mode, DRIFT, that starts global and then follows up locally.
- **Generator:** the same LLM call as in [RAG Fundamentals](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/rag-fundamentals), with graph context instead of, or as well as, chunks. Citations point at the community reports and through them at the source chunks.

The graph, the summaries and the chunk index are three stores with one lifecycle: rebuilding one without the others leaves summaries that describe a graph that no longer exists.

## Technical mechanics

### Indexing cost and incremental updates

- **Corpus-scaled LLM cost:** The cost is in LLM tokens and scales with the corpus, not traffic—every chunk is read at least once by a large model, every community gets a written report, and changing the extraction prompt means doing it again.
- **Three levers to cut cost:** Use a cheaper model for extraction, use larger chunks (fewer calls, lower recall, as the paper's chunk-size test shows), and index only the part of the corpus that global questions need.
- **Update propagation:** A new ticket adds entities and edges, which can move community boundaries and make some summaries stale; Microsoft's library has an `update` command that updates an existing index, so check what it recomputes against your own corpus before relying on it.
- **Scheduled batch rebuilds:** Append new chunks to the vector index immediately, and re-run extraction, communities, and summaries on a schedule (nightly or weekly); global answers are then a day or a week behind, which is fine for "common reasons" and wrong for "what broke this morning".
- **LazyGraphRAG (Microsoft Research, 2024):** Moves work to query time by extracting concepts with cheap noun-phrase extraction instead of an LLM and writing no summaries at indexing time, so indexing costs the same as vector RAG (0.1% of full GraphRAG, by their account).

### Hybrid graph and vector retrieval

- **Complementary stores:** GraphRAG is not graph instead of vectors; local search finds its starting entities by embedding similarity between the question and the entity descriptions, uses the graph to decide what else belongs in the context, and adds the raw chunks for detail.
- **Three-path router:** The common production shape is a router in front of three paths—vector search for single-passage lookups, local graph search for entity questions, and global search for corpus-wide ones ([Hybrid Search and Reranking](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/hybrid-search-and-reranking) covers the vector side).

### When GraphRAG is worth it

- **Good fit:** Users ask questions about themes, patterns, or connections across many documents; the corpus changes slowly enough that a weekly rebuild is fine; and you can afford an LLM pass over every token (such as analysts reading incident reports, research notes, or customer feedback).
- **Poor fit:** Not worth it for help-centre lookups, for corpora that change by the minute, or before checking whether users ask global questions at all (log a month of questions and count).

## Trade-offs

| Decision                      | Option A                              | Option B                              | Guidance                                                       |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| GraphRAG vs vector RAG        | Answers whole-corpus questions        | Cheap to index, fresh in minutes      | GraphRAG only for the global or multi-hop share of questions   |
| Community level               | Root level (C0): few summaries, cheap | Low level (C3): detailed, more tokens | Start at a high level; go lower if answers are too vague       |
| Extraction model              | Large model: more entities found      | Small model: cheaper                  | Measure entity recall on a labelled sample before downgrading  |
| Rebuild schedule              | Frequent: fresh summaries             | Infrequent: cheap                     | Match it to how fast the answers to global questions change    |
| Full GraphRAG vs LazyGraphRAG | Summaries ready before questions      | No indexing LLM cost                  | Lazy when the corpus changes often or most questions are local |

## Production systems

- Microsoft Research published GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) and an open-source library with local, global, DRIFT and basic search.
- The paper's evaluation used two corpora of about 1 million and 1.7 million tokens and compared answers head to head with an LLM judge on comprehensiveness, diversity, empowerment and directness; vector RAG won on directness, its intended control.
- Graph databases and RAG frameworks now ship GraphRAG-style pipelines that extract entities with an LLM and store them next to vector embeddings.
- **Used in our case studies:** [Clinical Knowledge-Graph Assistant](/genai-system-design/case-studies/kg-healthcare-case-study) · [Yield Root-Cause Assistant on a Fab Knowledge Graph](/genai-system-design/case-studies/kg-semiconductor-case-study)

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- Building a graph before checking that users ask global or multi-hop questions.
- Skipping entity merging, so one concept is spread over several weak communities.
- Letting summaries drift out of date while the vector index stays fresh, so the two disagree.
- Sending every question to global search, which costs a map call per summary for questions one chunk could answer.
- Reporting the paper's win rates as if they were accuracy: they are head-to-head preferences on summarisation questions.

## Interview framework

1. Separate question types: single-passage lookups, multi-hop, and whole-corpus questions. Say which ones vector RAG handles.
2. Describe indexing: chunk, extract entities and relations with an LLM, merge duplicates, detect communities (Leiden), summarise each.
3. Describe querying: local search from named entities, global map-reduce over summaries, a router choosing per question.
4. Put a number on indexing cost (LLM tokens per corpus token) and a plan for updates.
5. Say how you would evaluate it: a set of global questions, judged against vector RAG on coverage, with cost per query tracked.

**What interviewers listen for:** a clear reason to pay for the graph, and a router that keeps cheap questions on the cheap path.

**Red flags:** "GraphRAG is better RAG" with no question types, no indexing cost, and no update plan.
