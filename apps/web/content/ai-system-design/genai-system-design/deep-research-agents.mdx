A deep research agent takes one open question, plans it, searches many sources over minutes, and writes a report in which every claim names its source.

- Interviewers use it to test planning, parallel work, stopping rules and citations in one design.
- This page builds on [Multi-Agent Systems](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/multi-agent-systems), which covers the lead-and-workers pattern itself, and on [Agentic RAG](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/agentic-rag), which covers one agent searching in a loop.

## Overview

- **Plan first.** The lead agent pins down what the question means, splits it into sub-questions, and saves the plan outside its context window.
- **Search wide, then narrow.** Each worker starts with short, broad queries, reads what comes back, and narrows. Workers run in parallel, each in its own context window.
- **Carry the source with the claim.** A worker returns notes, and each note keeps the id of the page or record it came from. A sentence in the report is only as good as the note under it.
- **Stop on a rule, not a feeling.** A budget, saturation (a search adds nothing new) and confidence (independent sources agree) each end a search. The report says which one fired.
- **Check before sending.** A citation pass matches every sentence to a source and sends back what has none.
- **It is expensive.** Anthropic measured its multi-agent research system at about 15 times the tokens of a chat (June 2025). Products run for minutes, so they run as background jobs.
- **Evaluate the report, not the path.** Grade factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness and source quality, and keep a benchmark with short, checkable answers such as BrowseComp.

## Mental model

- Think of a research lead with three analysts and a fact-checker.
- The lead writes the brief: what exactly we are asking, who looks where, and when each analyst should stop.
- The analysts work at the same time and hand back index cards, each with one finding and where it came from.
- The lead writes the report from the cards. The fact-checker refuses any sentence that has no card behind it.

- A search engine answers "which pages mention this?". A deep research agent answers "what is true, and how do you know?".
- The second needs a plan, many reads, and a paper trail.

### Search, browse and read loops

Each worker runs its own [agent loop](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/agent-architectures#architecture). For the reviews worker, one turn of that loop looks like this:

1. **Search.** A short query first, such as "brightpost kettle review". Anthropic's prompts tell sub-agents to "start wide, then narrow down".
2. **Read the snippets.** The result list costs a few hundred tokens. The worker decides which pages are worth opening.
3. **Fetch and read one page.** A full page can cost thousands of tokens, so it opens only the best new one.
4. **Extract notes.** It writes down each finding with the page's URL and a short quote, then drops the raw page from its working set.
5. **Decide.** Anthropic's sub-agents think between tool calls to judge result quality, find gaps and refine the next query. The worker searches again, or a stopping rule ends the loop.

- Web pages are untrusted text. A page can contain instructions aimed at the agent, so treat every fetched page as data. See [Computer Use & Browser Agents](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/computer-use-and-browser-agents) for the injection path.
- Retrieval from your own documents follows the same loop with a search tool over an index. [Agentic RAG](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/agentic-rag#stopping-rules-and-budget-control) covers that case with one agent.

### A lead agent and parallel sub-agents

- Research splits well: the three sub-questions above share no state, so three workers can search at once.
- Each worker spends its own context window on raw pages and hands back a short summary. The [context-isolation figure](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/multi-agent-systems#core-concepts) counts what that saves the lead.
- How many workers, and how much effort each, should scale with the question. Anthropic's prompts give the scale, and the numbers are on [Multi-Agent Systems](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/multi-agent-systems#architecture).
- Anthropic's lead agents run sub-agents **synchronously**: the lead waits for the whole set before it continues. One slow worker blocks the run, and the lead cannot redirect a worker mid-search.
- A worker's task must say what it must not do. Without boundaries, the refund-records worker could also read reviews, and the lead would count one finding as two sources.

```json
{
  "id": "c4",
  "claim": "7 reviews since 3 Nov mention a cracked spout or a thin box",
  "source": {
    "id": "S3",
    "url": "https://reviews.example/brightpost/kettle",
```

- Model APIs return the same kind of link. OpenAI's web search tool attaches a `url_citation` annotation to the answer text, with the URL, the title and the `start_index` and `end_index` of the words it supports.
- Anthropic's research system runs a separate **CitationAgent** over the report and the documents, so that every claim is attributed to its source.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  A citation that does not support its sentence. The lead merges two notes into one sentence and keeps one citation, or
  rounds "26 of 31" up to "almost all". The link is there, so a check that only looks for links passes it. Check the
  sentence against the quote, not only that a citation exists.
</Callout>

## Architecture

- The research system is orchestrator-workers with research parts added. The figure is the one on [Multi-Agent Systems](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/multi-agent-systems#architecture), where its five steps are explained in turn. Here, read it for what is specific to research.

  spec="animated/agents/orchestrator-workers"
  stepper
  minWidth={620}
  caption="The lead plans and writes; workers search in parallel and send back short summaries; a reviewer checks every claim before the report goes out."
/>

**How to read it:**

- Step 1 is the [research plan](#planning-and-decomposing-the-question): the lead fixes the scope and saves the plan before any worker starts.
- Steps 2 and 3 are three [search loops](#search-browse-and-read-loops) at once. "Findings" holds notes with source ids, not transcripts.
- Steps 4 and 5 are the [citation check](#citations-and-source-tracking) and the second round it causes.

Around the figure, a production system adds four parts:

1. **A job runner.** Research takes minutes. Run it as a background job with a job id, report progress, and notify the user when the report is ready. OpenAI's API docs recommend background mode for requests that take several minutes.
2. **A notes store.** Notes and fetched pages live outside the context windows, keyed by source id, so the citation pass and the reviewer can reread any quote.
3. **A tool layer with limits.** Search and fetch go through the harness, which counts calls against the budget, caches pages, and blocks domains you do not trust.
4. **Traces.** Every worker's queries, pages and notes go into one trace per run, so a wrong sentence can be followed back to the page that caused it.

## Technical mechanics

### Worked example: why are kettle refunds up?

This follows the orchestrator-workers figure through the whole run, from the manager's question to the report. Tool-call counts are illustrative.

| Step | Who                   | What happens                                                                                     | Tool calls |
| ---- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| 1    | Lead                  | Fixes the scope (1–15 Nov vs 1–15 Oct, per 100 sold), writes three sub-questions, saves the plan | 0          |
| 2–3  | Refund records worker | October: 9 refunds, 600 sold. November: 31 refunds, 620 sold. 26 of 31 "arrived cracked" (S1)    | 3          |
| 2–3  | Help centre worker    | `search_help` finds "Damaged on arrival"; its edit history shows no change since March (S2)      | 2          |
| 2–3  | Reviews worker        | 4 searches, 3 fetches; 7 reviews on one site mention a cracked spout or a thin box (S3)          | 7          |
| 4    | Lead, then reviewer   | Draft 1 has five sentences; the courier sentence has no source and goes back                     | 0          |
| 5    | Refund records worker | Joins refunds to shipments: 24 of 26 cracked kettles went with the new courier from 1 Nov (S4)   | 2          |
| 5    | Lead, then reviewer   | Draft 2 cites S4; every sentence is supported; the report goes to the manager                    | 0          |

**The numbers:**

- Rates: 9 ÷ 600 = **1.5** refunds per 100 sold in October; 31 ÷ 620 = **5.0** in November. The rate more than tripled, while sales rose only 20 kettles.
- Tool calls: 3 + 2 + 7 in round 1, then 2 in round 2 = **14**, against a cap of 3 workers × 10 = 30.
- Time is set by the slowest worker. Round 1 takes as long as the reviews worker's 7 calls, not 3 + 2 + 7 = 12 in a row: 7 ÷ 12 ≈ 58 % of the sequential time, before the lead's own calls.

The report the manager gets:

- Kettle refunds rose from 1.5 to 5.0 per 100 sold [S1]. 26 of 31 arrived cracked [S1]. The refund rule did not change [S2].
- Reviews blame the packaging [S3, one site, medium confidence]. The rise began with a new courier on 1 Nov: 24 of 26 cracked kettles went with it [S4].

### Stopping rules, cost and latency

A worker that never stops burns the budget; one that stops after one search misses the answer. Write the rules into the task, and let the harness enforce the hard ones.

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/agents/stopping-rules"
  stepper
  minWidth={620}
  caption="Saturation stops the search before the budget does, and before confidence is reached, so the worker says how sure it is instead of searching on. Counts are illustrative."
/>

<StepCards>
  <StepCard n={1} title="Search 1 finds 4 new sources">
    The reviews worker's first, broad query returns 4 review pages it has not seen. It fetches the best one. The three
    rules on the right are part of its task from the start.
  </StepCard>
  <StepCard n={2} title="Searches 2 and 3 find 2, then 1">
    Narrower queries ("cracked", then "cracked spout november") find fewer new pages. Falling returns are the signal
    that the topic is nearly covered.
  </StepCard>
  <StepCard n={3} title="Search 4 finds nothing new: saturation fires">
    "brightpost courier damaged kettle" returns only pages it already has. Saturation stops the loop. The budget is at 7
    of 10 calls. Confidence is not met: all seven reviews are on one site.
  </StepCard>
  <StepCard n={4} title="The worker stops and reports its confidence">
    It stops with 3 calls unspent and marks its claim "medium confidence, one site". The lead decides whether that is
    good enough, instead of the worker searching until the budget runs out.
  </StepCard>
</StepCards>

The three rules, and who enforces each:

- **Budget** (harness): tool calls, tokens and wall-clock time per worker, and a cap on rounds for the lead. Here 10 calls per worker and 2 rounds. At the cap the worker reports what it has and what it could not find.
- **Saturation** (harness or model): stop when a search adds no source you have not seen. Count by URL after normalising it, and by site, so two copies of one press release count once.
- **Confidence** (model, checked by the harness): stop when two independent sources agree. Do not trust the model's own sense of certainty: on BrowseComp, OpenAI measured Deep Research's calibration error at 91 %.

On cost and latency:

- Anthropic measured agents at about 4 times the tokens of a chat and its multi-agent system at about 15 times (June 2025). Token usage alone explained 80 % of the variance in its BrowseComp results.
- Running sub-agents in parallel cut research time by up to 90 % for complex queries in the same write-up.
- OpenAI said its deep research takes 5 to 30 minutes per task (February 2025). Design for a job the user leaves and comes back to, not a chat reply.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Effort that ignores the question. Anthropic's early agents spawned 50 sub-agents for simple queries and kept searching
  after they already had enough. Its fix was explicit scaling rules in the prompt. Back them with a hard budget in the
  harness, and log which rule stopped every run.
</Callout>

### Checking claims and source quality

- **Check sentences against quotes.** For each sentence, a grader reads the cited quote and answers "supported, partly, or not". This catches citations that exist but do not support the sentence.
- **Prefer primary sources.** Brightpost's own refund records beat a blog post about them. Anthropic's human testers found early agents "consistently chose SEO-optimized content farms" over authoritative but lower-ranked sources; source-quality heuristics in the prompt fixed it.
- **Count independent sources, not links.** Seven reviews on one site are one source for "confidence". Ten news stories rewriting one press release are one source too.
- **Show conflicts.** When sources disagree, the report says so and names both, instead of picking one silently.
- **Keep dates.** Every note carries when it was retrieved and, where known, when it was published. "The rule did not change" is only true up to the date of the edit history read.
- **Separate public and private data.** A fetched page can carry instructions. OpenAI's docs advise running public web search and private MCP lookups in separate requests, so a page cannot steer a tool that reads private records. See [Guardrails & Safety](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/guardrails-and-safety) for the lethal trifecta.

### Evaluating research reports

- **Grade the end state.** Agents take "completely different valid paths" to the same goal, so Anthropic judges the final result, not each turn.
- **Use a rubric judge.** Anthropic's judge scores factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality and tool efficiency. One model call with one prompt, giving a score from 0.0 to 1.0 and a pass or fail, matched human judgement best. See [LLM-as-Judge Evaluation](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/llm-as-judge-evaluation).
- **Start small.** Anthropic began with about 20 queries from real usage. Early on, a prompt change can move success from 30 % to 80 %, and a few cases show it.
- **Keep checkable questions.** BrowseComp (OpenAI, April 2025) has 1,266 questions whose answers are hard to find and short to check. Google's DeepSearchQA (December 2025) has 900 tasks across 17 fields.
- **Keep people in the loop.** Anthropic found that people testing agents catch edge cases evals miss: its human testers found the content-farm problem.

The published BrowseComp results show how hard the task is:

| System (as reported)                          | BrowseComp accuracy |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| GPT-4o with browsing (OpenAI, Apr 2025)       | 1.9 %               |
| OpenAI o1, no browsing (Apr 2025)             | 9.9 %               |
| OpenAI Deep Research (Apr 2025)               | 51.5 %              |
| Gemini Deep Research agent (Google, Dec 2025) | 59.2 %              |

- OpenAI's human trainers also tried the questions. They solved 29.2 % and gave up on 70.8 % after two hours.
- Vendor scores come from different dates and set-ups. Compare systems on your own questions before choosing one.

## Trade-offs

| Decision                         | Option A                         | Option B                                      | Guidance                                                                       |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| One agent vs lead and workers    | Cheaper, simpler to trace        | Parallel, isolated contexts, faster           | Workers when sub-questions are independent and the answer is worth ~15× tokens |
| Ask the user vs plan alone       | Right scope, one more round trip | Starts at once, may answer the wrong question | Show the plan for long or costly runs                                          |
| Fixed budget vs adaptive stop    | Predictable cost                 | Stops early on easy questions                 | Both: saturation and confidence inside a hard budget                           |
| Cite while reading vs cite after | Every claim has a real source    | Simpler pipeline                              | Carry sources with notes; add a citation pass on top                           |
| Web vs your own sources          | Wide coverage                    | Trusted, current, private                     | Your records first for facts about you; the web for outside views              |

- **OpenAI deep research** launched in ChatGPT on 2 February 2025, built on o3. OpenAI said a task takes 5 to 30 minutes and reported 26.6 % on Humanity's Last Exam at launch.
- **Gemini Deep Research** launched on 11 December 2024. It shows a multi-step plan for the user to revise or approve, then writes a report with links to its sources. On 11 December 2025 Google opened a Deep Research agent to developers through its Interactions API, built on Gemini 3 Pro, reporting 46.4 % on Humanity's Last Exam and 59.2 % on BrowseComp.
- **Anthropic's Research feature** uses a lead agent, parallel sub-agents and a citation agent. Its June 2025 engineering write-up is the most detailed public account of how such a system is built and evaluated.
- Products and models in this space change every few months. The facts above are as of November 2026; check the vendors' current docs before quoting a model name or score.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- Searching before fixing what the question means.
- Overlapping sub-questions, so two workers find the same fact and the lead counts it twice.
- Adding citations after writing, by searching for a page that seems to match.
- Treating many pages from one site, or one press release, as many sources.
- Trusting content farms because they rank well.
- No stopping rule except the budget, or a budget no harness enforces.
- Treating fetched pages as instructions.
- Reporting high confidence on one source.

## Interview framework

1. Clarify the question and the reader: scope, time window, which sources count, how long the user will wait.
2. Plan: sub-questions, one worker each, with sources, boundaries and a stop.
3. Search loops in parallel, each returning notes with source ids.
4. Write from notes, then a citation pass and a review; allow one more round for gaps.
5. Budgets and stopping rules; background jobs; traces.
6. Evaluate with a rubric judge, a checkable benchmark and human review.

**What interviewers listen for:** a plan before search, sources carried with claims, explicit stopping rules, and an honest cost.

**Red flags:** "the model adds citations at the end", or no answer to "when does it stop?".
