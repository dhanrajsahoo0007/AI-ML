A fine-tuned model is only as good as the examples it trains on, and most teams have too few clean examples for rare edge cases.

- Interviewers ask where training examples come from, how a curation funnel enforces diversity and quality, and how to prevent evaluation leakage and model collapse.

## Overview

- **Data quality beats data volume:** LIMA fine-tuned a 65B model on only **1,000 curated examples** and produced strong alignment (Zhou et al., 2023), showing that what you filter out matters as much as what you add.
- **Three complementary sources:** Combine production **logs** (high volume, messy), **human annotations** (gold standard for edge cases and evals), and **synthetic generation** (targeted gap-filling anchored on real seeds).
- **Diversity requires explicit constraints:** Self-Instruct starts from **175 seed tasks** and retains a generated instruction only when its ROUGE-L similarity to every existing instruction is **below 0.7** (Wang et al., 2022).
- **Raw synthetic output is noisy:** In the Self-Instruct authors' human audit of 200 generated samples, only **54%** had a valid instruction, input, and output simultaneously—making multi-stage filtering and human spot-checks mandatory.
- **Train/eval leakage inflates benchmarks:** Splitting the golden evaluation set off **before** selecting synthetic seeds and running near-duplicate checks prevents synthetic variants of test tickets from leaking into training.
- **Avoid recursive model collapse:** Repeatedly training only on synthetic outputs erases tail distributions (Shumailov et al., Nature, 2024); always accumulate synthetic data alongside real human/logged data (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024).

## Mental model

- **Expanding a culinary textbook:** A cooking school starts with 150 master recipes written by head chefs (seed data) and asks a junior cook to draft thousands of variations (synthetic generation).
- **The curation funnel:** Because many drafts repeat themselves, invent non-existent pans, or break food-safety rules, the school strips near-duplicates, runs rule checks, and has a senior chef taste-test a 100-recipe sample.
- **Keeping the final exam locked away:** The recipes used for the students' graduation exam (the golden eval set) are locked in a safe before any variations are written so no exam dish leaks into the practice book.

## Core concepts

### Sources: logs, human labels and synthetic generation

- **Production logs:** Resolved support tickets with agent-approved replies provide authentic user phrasing and real failure modes in high volume (Brightpost's 5,000-ticket baseline), but require heavy PII scrubbing and noise filtering.
- **Human expert labels:** Support leads write or edit gold-standard responses for high-risk slices and the held-out evaluation benchmark; highest quality, highest cost, and lowest throughput.
- **Synthetic generation:** A frontier teacher model expands real seed tickets into underrepresented edge cases (such as refunds over £30 across different customer personas), trading generator bias for speed and coverage.
- **Blending rule:** Use logs for baseline distribution, humans for the golden set and policy corner cases, and synthetic data strictly to fill measured slice gaps.

### Generating diverse synthetic examples

- **The Self-Instruct loop:** Start from a curated seed pool (175 tasks in Wang et al., 2022), sample a few seeds per prompt to generate new instructions and responses, filter by ROUGE-L $< 0.7$, and append survivors back into the pool (yielding 52K instructions / 82,439 instances and a +33% absolute gain on Super-NaturalInstructions; Stanford Alpaca generated 52K pairs from `text-davinci-003` for under \$500).
- **Parameterized diversity prompts:** Unconstrained prompts collapse onto the teacher model's favorite phrasings; inject explicit axes into every generation call (`topic`, `customer_persona`, `tone`, `complexity`, `policy_branch`).

### Filtering and quality checks

Run filters in ascending order of cost so cheap deterministic checks reject bad rows before calling an LLM judge:

- **Stage 1 — Format & schema:** Verify valid JSON structure, length bounds, and target language.
- **Stage 2 — PII & privacy scrubbing:** Strip or placeholder-mask emails, phone numbers, and Luhn-matched card numbers before writing to disk (see [Data Privacy & PII Handling](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/data-privacy-pii-handling)).
- **Stage 3 — Deterministic business rules:** Reject any synthetic reply that violates hard policy (e.g., promising a £42 refund without required human approval over the £30 limit).
- **Stage 4 — Near-duplicate removal:** Drop exact hashes and high-overlap candidates (ROUGE-L, MinHash, or cosine similarity).
- **Stage 5 — LLM-as-judge rubric:** Score surviving candidates on tone, factual grounding, and policy compliance (see [LLM-as-Judge Evaluation](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/llm-as-judge-evaluation)).

### De-duplication and train/test leakage

- **Why deduplicate training data:** Lee et al. (2021) showed that web and LM training corpora contain massive near-duplication (one 61-word sentence appeared > 60,000 times in C4) and that deduplicating cuts verbatim memorization by ~10× while improving validation perplexity.
- **Synthetic amplification of test leaks:** If even a handful of golden evaluation tickets enter the synthetic seed pool, the teacher model generates dozens of paraphrases per leaked seed, contaminating the training set with near-copies of the exam.

**The numbers:**

- **Leak amplification:** If 20 of 150 seeds come from the 200-ticket golden set and the teacher generates $8,000 \div 150 = 53.3$ candidates per seed ($1,067$ candidates) with a 52.5% keep rate, **~560 leaked paraphrases** land in the 4,200 kept examples (**13.3%** of the synthetic set).
- **Score inflation:** Scoring 20/20 on those memorized tickets instead of the true 88% ($17.6/20$) inflates the 200-ticket benchmark by $2.4 \div 200 = \mathbf{+1.2\text{ percentage points}}$—enough to mask a real regression.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Train/eval leakage never throws a runtime error: offline metrics jump, the model ships, and production resolution drops. Split and freeze the golden evaluation set before selecting any seed pool, and run an n-gram + embedding similarity gate against the golden set on every training build.
</Callout>

## Technical mechanics

### How much data is enough

- **Alignment vs new knowledge:** LIMA (Zhou et al., 2023) demonstrated with 1,000 curated examples on a 65B model that supervised fine-tuning primarily teaches output format, tone, and policy routing rather than new world facts (see [Fine-Tuning LLMs](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/fine-tuning-llms)).
- **Iterative slice scaling:** Start with 500–1,000 high-purity examples, evaluate on the frozen golden set by topic slice, and generate targeted synthetic batches only for the slices where error rates remain high until the learning curve plateaus.

### Model collapse and synthetic-data bias

- **Recursive tail erasure (model collapse):** Shumailov et al. (Nature, 2024) proved that training generation $n$ exclusively on synthetic outputs from generation $n-1$ causes variance to shrink and rare tail events (like complex £42 refund escalations) to vanish.
- **Data accumulation fix:** Gerstgrasser et al. (2024) showed that **accumulating** synthetic data alongside the original real dataset (rather than replacing real data) keeps test error bounded across iterations.
- **Cross-family judging:** Because a generator and judge from the same model family share stylistic biases (verbosity, phrasing), use a distinct model family or deterministic rule verifiers to grade synthetic candidates.

### Dataset versioning and data cards

- **Reproducible dataset manifests:** Version every training build with the exact seed IDs, generator model snapshot, prompt version, deduplication threshold, judge rubric, stage counts, and golden-set hash.
- **Datasheets / data cards (Gebru et al., 2018):** Document dataset motivation, source breakdown (% real vs % synthetic), known slice gaps, PII scrubbing rules, and intended scope so any model regression can be traced back to its exact data lineage.

### Worked example: Brightpost's rare cases

Brightpost's 5,000 logged tickets contain too few examples of refunds over £30 being escalated properly:

1. **Seed selection:** Support leads pull 150 resolved damaged-goods and refund tickets, verified disjoint from the 200-ticket golden set.
2. **Conditioned generation:** The teacher model generates 8,000 candidates across product types (kettle, toaster, lost parcel), customer tones (calm, angry, non-native speaker), and amounts above/below £30.
3. **Deduplicate and filter:** Deduplication drops 1,800 ($6,200$ left); deterministic policy rules (rejecting any unapproved > £30 refund promise) and the judge drop 2,000 ($4,200$ kept).
4. **Human audit:** A support lead inspects 100 samples, catches 3 subtle policy errors, tightens the generator prompt, and re-runs the batch.
5. **Blend with real data:** The 4,200 curated synthetic tickets are merged with the 5,000 real logged tickets ($4,200 \div 9,200 \approx \mathbf{46\%\text{ synthetic}}$), keeping real customer language as the anchor.
6. **Final leak gate:** Every training row is checked against the 200 golden tickets (dropping any row with $\text{ROUGE-L} \ge 0.7$) before kicking off fine-tuning.

## Trade-offs

| Choice | Pros | Cons | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Production logs only | Authentic user phrasing; low collection cost | Sparse on rare edge cases; noisy; contains PII | High-volume flows with clean outcome signals |
| Human-written examples | Highest accuracy and nuance | Slow and expensive per row | Golden evaluation sets and high-risk policy slices |
| Synthetic generation from teacher LLM | Fast, cheap, targets specific slice gaps | Generator bias, collapse risk, provider licensing constraints | Filling tail coverage gaps alongside real data |
| Strict curation funnel | High signal per training step; smaller training runs | Can over-prune rare phrasing if thresholds are too tight | Production fine-tuning; calibrate drop rates on human samples |
| Loose filtering | Maximizes raw token volume | Teaches hallucinations and policy violations | Never for alignment/SFT; noisy pre-training experiments only |

## Production systems

- **Self-Instruct (Wang et al., 2022):** Bootstrapped 175 human seed tasks into 52,000 instructions with ROUGE-L $< 0.7$ deduplication, lifting GPT-3 by +33% absolute on Super-NaturalInstructions.
- **Stanford Alpaca (2023):** Generated 52,000 instruction-following examples from `text-davinci-003` using the Self-Instruct recipe for under \$500 in API spend.
- **LIMA (Zhou et al., 2023):** Showed that 1,000 carefully curated examples suffice to align a 65B model, establishing the "Superficial Alignment Hypothesis".
- **Deduplicating Training Data (Lee et al., 2021):** Demonstrated across C4 and Wiki-40B that suffix-array and MinHash deduplication cuts memorization 10× and detects > 4% train-validation leakage.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- **Contaminating seeds with golden-set examples:** Even 20 leaked evaluation tickets expand into hundreds of paraphrased training rows that invalidate offline benchmarks.
- **Single-prompt homogeneous generation:** Calling the teacher model 8,000 times with the same unconditioned prompt produces repetitive phrasing clustered in a tiny corner of topic space.
- **Same-family generator and judge bias:** Using the exact same model to generate and grade candidates reinforces verbosity and blind spots.
- **Replacing real data with synthetic data across iterations:** Induces model collapse and erases rare edge cases.
- **Skipping human sample inspection:** Raw synthetic pipelines frequently pass subtle policy violations (46% invalid rate in raw Self-Instruct before filtering).
- **Unversioned data builds:** Training on ad-hoc JSONL files without recording seed IDs, filter thresholds, and golden-set hashes makes regressions impossible to debug.

## Interview framework

1. **Combine the three sources:** Real logs for baseline distribution, human labels for the golden eval set and hard policies, and synthetic data to fill specific slice gaps.
2. **Walk the 5-stage curation funnel:** Seed selection → parameterized generation (topic/persona/tone axes) → exact + near deduplication (MinHash / ROUGE-L $< 0.7$) → deterministic rules + LLM judge → human sample audit.
3. **Quantify train/eval leakage:** Explain how 20 leaked seeds amplify into ~560 training paraphrases (+1.2% benchmark inflation) and enforce a pre-training similarity gate against the frozen golden set.
4. **Address model collapse:** Cite Shumailov et al. (2024) on recursive tail loss and Gerstgrasser et al. (2024) on accumulating synthetic data alongside real data.
5. **Close with governance:** Dataset lineage manifests, datasheets/data cards, PII scrubbing before storage, and checking provider terms of service for model distillation (see [Distillation & Small Models](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/distillation-and-small-models)).

**What interviewers listen for:** Multi-stage filtering ordered by cost, parameterized diversity, strict train/eval leakage prevention, and keeping real data in the training mix.

**Red flags:** Generating thousands of rows from one prompt with no deduplication, no held-out golden set check, or zero human inspection.
