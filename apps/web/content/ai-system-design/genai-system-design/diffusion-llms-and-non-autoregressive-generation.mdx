Almost every production LLM writes one token at a time, left to right. Diffusion language models start from a fully masked canvas and fill in many tokens per step, trading step count against quality and caching.

## Overview

- An autoregressive model needs **one forward pass per output token**, so a 1,000-token answer is 1,000 passes in a row, each one bound by memory bandwidth.
- Non-autoregressive models predict **many tokens per pass**. Predicting them independently causes the **multimodality problem** (pieces of different valid answers mixed together); **iterative refinement** fixes it by remasking low-confidence tokens and predicting again.
- **Masked diffusion** trains a Transformer without a causal mask to recover tokens hidden at a **random mask ratio t**; the loss is a weighted masked-LM loss on the masked positions only. **LLaDA 8B** (2025) showed this scales to an 8B model that is competitive with LLaMA3 8B on many reported benchmarks.
- Generation starts from all-`[MASK]` and runs **T denoising steps**; fewer steps means more tokens per pass and more speed, but lower quality.
- Bidirectional attention gives **infilling and a smaller reversal-curse gap**, but breaks the exact **KV cache**. **Block diffusion** (left to right between blocks, diffusion inside a block) restores caching and variable length.
- Commercial systems report **over 1,000 tokens per second** (Inception's Mercury Coder, Google's experimental Gemini Diffusion), mostly on code; on hard reasoning and knowledge benchmarks they still lag comparable autoregressive models.

<KeyIdea>A diffusion LLM fills a masked canvas in T parallel passes instead of one pass per token, trading step count against quality and giving up the exact KV cache unless it decodes in blocks.</KeyIdea>

## Mental model

- **Sequential pen vs. crossword grid:** An autoregressive model writes like a person with a pen who may never go back—one word after another—whereas a diffusion model solves the answer like a crossword grid all at once.
- **Confidence-first filling:** On the first pass it looks at all empty squares, pencils in the answers it is surest of, and leaves the rest blank.
- **Iterative clues:** Filled squares provide context clues for the harder blanks, so after a few passes the grid is full.
- **Speed vs. contradiction trade-off:** The crossword solver writes several words per pass, but every pass means rereading the whole grid, and penciling in too many guesses at once can cause crossing answers to contradict each other.

## Core concepts

1. **The autoregressive decode bottleneck:**
   - **Sequential factorization:** An autoregressive (AR) model factorizes text as $p(x) = \prod_i p(x_i \mid x_{<i})$, so generating $N$ tokens takes $N$ sequential forward passes (see [Decoding & Sampling](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/decoding-and-sampling)).
   - **Memory-bandwidth wall:** Each pass reads every weight and the whole KV cache from GPU memory to produce one token per sequence, making decoding memory-bandwidth bound rather than compute bound (see [Efficient Attention](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/efficient-attention)).
   - **Alternatives:** Batching helps throughput but not single-answer latency; [speculative decoding](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/speculative-decoding) and [multi-token prediction](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/multi-token-prediction) attack the same wall while keeping left-to-right order, whereas diffusion drops the order altogether.

2. **Non-autoregressive generation and the multimodality problem:**
   - **One-pass parallel prediction:** Gu et al. (2017) built a non-autoregressive translation model that predicts every target token in one pass conditioned on the source, achieving an order of magnitude lower latency within 2.0 BLEU of its AR teacher.
   - **The multimodality trap:** When several outputs are valid ("Paris is the capital" or "The capital is Paris"), independent per-position choices can mix them ("Paris is the Paris").
   - **Distillation mitigation:** Gu et al. eased this by training on a teacher model's outputs (knowledge distillation), which are less varied than human references.

3. **Iterative refinement (Mask-Predict):**
   - **Linear remasking schedule:** Ghazvininejad et al. (2019) trained a conditional masked language model and decoded it in $T$ passes—pass 1 predicts every token, and each later pass masks the $n = N \cdot (T - t)/T$ lowest-probability tokens and predicts them again conditioned on the rest.
   - **Measured quality jump:** On WMT'14 English–German, 1 pass scored 18.05 BLEU with 16.72% repeated tokens (multimodality); 4 passes reached 25.94 BLEU with 1.07% repeats, and 10 passes reached 27.03 against 27.74 for their base Transformer.
   - **Legacy:** This mask, predict, and remask loop is the exact decoding loop modern diffusion LLMs reuse.

4. **Discrete diffusion for text:**
   - **Discrete transition matrices:** Because text consists of discrete tokens rather than continuous pixels, D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) corrupts tokens via a transition matrix: uniform random swaps, embedding nearest neighbors, or an **absorbing state** where a token turns into `[MASK]` and stays masked.
   - **Link to masked LMs:** The absorbing-state formulation connects diffusion directly to masked language models like BERT (see [encoder-decoder families](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/encoder-decoder-families))—denoising simply means predicting masked tokens.

5. **The masked diffusion objective:**
   - **Weighted masked-LM loss:** MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024) showed that absorbing-state diffusion simplifies to a weighted average of masked-LM losses; LLaDA draws $t \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1]$, masks each token of clean text $x_0$ independently with probability $t$ to get $x_t$, and minimizes:

   $$
   \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\,\mathbb{E}_{t,\,x_0,\,x_t}\left[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{L} \mathbf{1}[x_t^i = \text{M}]\,\log p_\theta(x_0^i \mid x_t)\right]
   $$

   - **Variable mask ratio and NLL bound:** Unlike BERT's fixed 15% mask, $t$ varies per example so the model learns to fill both nearly empty and nearly complete text, and the $1/t$ weight makes the loss an upper bound on negative log-likelihood for perplexity comparisons.

6. **Score entropy (SEDD):**
   - **Probability ratios:** Lou et al. (2023) learned ratios between the probabilities of neighboring sequences (a discrete analogue of the continuous diffusion score) using a score entropy loss.
   - **Results:** Reported 25–75% lower perplexity than earlier language diffusion models, competitive quality with GPT-2 at similar sizes, and matching sample quality with 32× fewer network evaluations.

7. **Parallel token refinement and remasking steps:**
   - **Reverse masking loop:** Generation starts with the prompt followed by $L$ `[MASK]` tokens; at each of $T$ steps the model predicts every masked position at once, keeps a subset, and remasks the rest.
   - **Low-confidence remasking:** Borrowed from MaskGIT, LLaDA's default sampler keeps the most confident predictions (about $L/T$ tokens per step on a linear schedule) and remasks the least confident ones before they can mislead future steps.

8. **Bidirectional context:**
   - **No causal mask:** Every position attends to every other position—including those to its right—allowing the model to finish the end of a sentence before its middle.
   - **Smaller reversal curse:** LLaDA treats "what comes before X" and "what comes after X" more symmetrically, beating GPT-4o on completing the previous line of a poem (45.6 vs. 34.3) though trailing in the forward direction (51.8 vs. 82.7).

9. **Conditioning, infilling and controllability:**
   - **Unmasked conditioning:** Prompt tokens are never masked so every denoising step attends to them.
   - **Native infilling and constraints:** Keeping a code prefix and suffix while masking the middle achieves infilling without special fill-in-the-middle training, and fixed tokens (keywords, brackets, JSON keys) can be pinned at any position.

## Architecture

A diffusion LLM is usually a standard Transformer with the causal mask removed; the difference is the decoding loop around it.

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/llm/diffusion-ar-vs-parallel"

  caption="The autoregressive model needs one forward pass per token; masked diffusion predicts every masked position at once, keeps the 2 most confident and remasks the rest, so 8 tokens take 4 passes."
/>

1. **Lay out the canvas:** Append $L$ `[MASK]` tokens to the prompt, where $L$ is chosen before generation (LLaDA's base-model evaluations use 1,024).
2. **Predict everything:** Run one forward pass of the bidirectional Transformer to get a vocabulary distribution at every masked position.
3. **Commit and remask:** Pick a token at each masked position (greedy or sampled), score by confidence, keep the top $L/T$, and reset the rest to `[MASK]`.
4. **Repeat:** Run for $T$ steps with the prompt and all committed tokens visible to every position.
5. **Finish:** Drop everything after the first end-of-sequence token; in **block diffusion**, steps 1–4 run on one block of tokens at a time from left to right.

## Technical mechanics

### Diffusion step count vs. quality and latency

- **The step-count dial:** Latency is roughly $T$ forward passes over the prompt plus all $L$ positions (versus $N$ cheaper cached passes for an AR model); $T = L$ commits 1 token per pass with no speed gain, whereas $T = L/4$ commits 4 per pass.
- **Diminishing returns curve:** In Mask-Predict, going from 1 to 2 passes raised BLEU from 18.05 to 22.91, while going from 4 to 5 gained only 0.36.
- **Benchmark vs. efficiency settings:** LLaDA sets $T = L$ (e.g., 512 on GSM8K) for peak benchmark scores, but in throughput tests LLaDA 8B Base matched LLaMA3 8B Base on GSM8K and Math at 1.5× and 1.8× higher throughput (without a KV cache), tied on HumanEval, and lagged on MBPP.
- **Adaptive confidence thresholds:** Fixed schedules waste passes on easy spans, so samplers like Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025) commit every token whose confidence exceeds a threshold, speeding through easy spans and spending passes on hard ones.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Fewer steps force more independent simultaneous token choices and revive multimodality: repeated or mixed tokens. The fix is to spend passes where they are needed, with more steps or a confidence-threshold sampler.
</Callout>

### KV caching challenges in non-causal bidirectional models

- **Why exact caching breaks:** An AR model caches keys and values because token representations depend only on unchanging left context; in a bidirectional diffusion model, every position attends to masked positions that change every step, forcing full-sequence recomputation.
- **Compute vs. memory:** LLaDA notes it is incompatible with a standard KV cache—its memory footprint is similar to cached AR (17.03 GB for LLaDA 8B vs. 16.32 GB for LLaMA3 8B at 512 input + 512 output tokens), but compute per step is much higher.
- **Block diffusion:** Arriola et al. (2025) generate blocks left to right and run diffusion only inside the current block; causal attention between blocks lets finished blocks be cached like an AR prefix and removes the fixed-length limit by stopping at any block with an EOS token.
- **Approximate caching:** Fast-dLLM reuses keys and values that barely change between steps and refreshes them once per block, reporting up to 27.6× higher throughput on LLaDA and Dream with minimal accuracy loss when paired with confidence-threshold decoding.

- **Block length trade-off:** A block of 1 token is an AR model, while a single block of length $L$ is plain diffusion; LLaDA uses this semi-autoregressive order at sampling time without retraining to prevent early EOS padding artifacts in its instruction-tuned model.

### Where diffusion LLMs beat or lag autoregressive models

**Where they win:**

- **Latency per answer at low step counts**, especially for code, where outputs are structured and many tokens are predictable. Inception and Google both report over 1,000 tokens per second (see Production systems).
- **Infilling and editing**, because the prefix and suffix are both visible at every step.
- **Reversal-style tasks**, as in LLaDA's poem results.
- **Math, in one report:** LLaDA 8B Base scored 70.3 on GSM8K against 48.7 for LLaMA3 8B Base, though the training data differ, so this does not isolate the architecture.

**Where they lag:**

- **Likelihood.** On OpenWebText MDLM's perplexity bound is 23.21 against 17.54 for an AR model trained the same way.
- **Knowledge and hard reasoning.** Gemini Diffusion trails Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite on GPQA Diamond (40.4% vs 56.5%) and Global MMLU Lite (69.1% vs 79.0%), as reported by Google.
- **Serving.** Engines such as vLLM and TensorRT-LLM, along with PagedAttention, speculative decoding and prefix caching, were built for AR models. Diffusion serving needs custom kernels and samplers, and post-training methods such as RLHF and RL for reasoning are less mature for diffusion models.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  **Length.** Plain masked diffusion must fix $L$ in advance; too short truncates the answer, too long wastes compute on padding. Block diffusion removes the limit by stopping at any block with an EOS token.
</Callout>

## Trade-offs

<Compare caption="Decoding choices: how many tokens each pass commits, and what that costs in caching, length and quality.">

| Choice | Pros | Cons | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autoregressive decoding | Exact KV cache, mature serving stack, best likelihood and reasoning results | One pass per token; latency grows with output length | The default for chat, reasoning and long outputs |
| AR + speculative decoding | Keeps AR quality exactly; several tokens per target pass | Needs a draft model or extra heads; speedup depends on acceptance rate | Lower latency without changing the model |
| Full-sequence masked diffusion | Many tokens per pass; bidirectional context; infilling | No exact KV cache; fixed output length; quality drops at low step counts | Infilling, editing, short structured outputs |
| Block diffusion | Exact cache across blocks; variable length; parallel inside a block | Less parallelism than full diffusion; block size is a new hyperparameter | Diffusion serving at scale, long answers |
| Many denoising steps | Quality close to the model's best | Little or no speed gain over AR | Benchmarks, quality-critical answers |
| Few steps / confidence-threshold sampling | Large speedups | Multimodality errors such as repeated or mixed tokens | Latency-critical code completion and edits |

</Compare>

## Production systems

- **LLaDA 8B** (Nie et al., 2025): a masked diffusion model trained from scratch on 2.3 trillion tokens with 0.13 million H800 GPU hours, then fine-tuned on 4.5 million prompt-response pairs. The paper reports MMLU 65.9 vs 65.4 and HumanEval 35.4 vs 34.8 against LLaMA3 8B Base (figures from the October 2025 revision).
- **Mercury Coder** (Inception Labs, technical report June 2025): diffusion models built on a Transformer, with parameter counts not disclosed. Artificial Analysis measured 1,109 tokens per second for Mercury Coder Mini and 737 for Small on H100 GPUs, as reported; HumanEval scores were 88.0 and 90.0.
- **Gemini Diffusion** (Google DeepMind, announced May 2025): an experimental demo, not a general release. Google reports 1,479 tokens per second sampling speed (excluding 0.84 s of overhead) and HumanEval 89.6% vs 90.2% for Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite.
- **Mask-Predict** (2019) in translation: 27.03 BLEU with 10 refinement passes against 27.74 for a base Transformer on WMT'14 English–German, the clearest early sign that a few parallel passes can approach AR quality.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- Comparing tokens per second without matching quality, hardware, batch size and step count. A diffusion model run with $T = L$ makes as many passes as an AR model.
- Assuming one pass of diffusion costs the same as one AR decode step. A diffusion pass reprocesses the whole prompt and canvas without a cache.
- Forgetting the fixed output length of plain masked diffusion, and the padding or truncation it causes.
- Committing too many tokens per step and getting repeated or mixed phrases, the multimodality problem in a new form.
- Treating "bidirectional" as free: it is exactly what breaks the KV cache.
- Quoting vendor speed numbers as independent measurements. Say "as reported" and name who measured them.

## Interview framework

1. Explain the bottleneck: AR needs one memory-bound forward pass per token, and batching helps throughput, not single-request latency.
2. Introduce non-autoregressive generation, name the multimodality problem with a small example, and show how iterative refinement (Mask-Predict) fixes it.
3. Describe masked diffusion: random mask ratio $t$, a bidirectional Transformer, loss on masked tokens weighted by $1/t$; then the sampling loop with low-confidence remasking over $T$ steps.
4. Give the costs: no exact KV cache, fixed length, quality falling as $T$ shrinks, and immature serving and post-training. Explain block diffusion as the middle ground.
5. Ground it with evidence: LLaDA 8B against LLaMA3 8B, Mercury and Gemini Diffusion speeds as reported, and where they still trail.

**What interviewers listen for:** the step-count trade-off stated in passes, why bidirectional attention breaks caching, the multimodality problem, and a balanced view of vendor claims.

**Red flags:** "diffusion LLMs generate everything in one step", "they are strictly faster than autoregressive models", or not knowing that the output length must be chosen up front.
