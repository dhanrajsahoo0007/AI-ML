Brightpost wants a clean product image for every new listing without a photo shoot. Interviewers ask about image and video generation because a good answer connects four things: how noise becomes a picture, why the work happens in a small latent space, what guidance does, and why video costs so much more.

## TL;DR

- A **diffusion model** learns to **predict the noise** in a picture that was mixed with noise. To generate, it starts from pure noise and removes a little at each step.
- **Latent diffusion** runs that loop on a compressed latent, not on pixels: **512 × 512 × 3 = 786,432 values become 64 × 64 × 4 = 16,384**, 48 times fewer. A VAE decoder turns the final latent into pixels.
- The text prompt steers the denoiser through **cross-attention**. **Classifier-free guidance** runs the denoiser with and without the prompt and extrapolates: **ε = ε_uncond + w (ε_cond − ε_uncond)**.
- A step costs two denoiser passes with guidance, and a picture needs many steps. **Fewer steps** come from better samplers and **distillation** (some models need 1 to 4).
- **Video** adds time. A video latent cut into spacetime patches gives **tens of thousands of tokens**; attention cost grows with their square.
- Generated media needs **safety checks and provenance**, not only quality.

## The idea in plain English

Picture a sculptor who starts with a rough block, not a blank canvas. Each pass with the chisel removes a little stone, guided by a note that says "kettle on a shelf". After a few dozen passes the block is a kettle. Training works the other way round: take a finished sculpture, add random stone back in measured amounts, and teach the sculptor to tell which part is the added stone. A sculptor who can do that at every stage of roughness can start from a plain block and finish a new kettle.

This page covers images and video. For diffusion applied to text, which uses masking instead of noise, see [Diffusion LLMs & Non-Autoregressive Generation](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/diffusion-llms-and-non-autoregressive-generation).

## Core concepts

### Diffusion and flow matching in plain terms

The **forward process** mixes a picture with noise over T steps. In DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) T = 1000 and the noise level rises on a fixed schedule, so after step t the picture is `x_t = √ᾱ_t · x_0 + √(1 − ᾱ_t) · ε`, a blend of the clean picture and standard noise ε. A network is trained to **predict ε** from `x_t` and t. To generate, a **sampler** starts at pure noise and repeats: predict the noise, remove some. **Flow matching** (Lipman et al., 2022) and rectified flow trains a network to predict the velocity along a path from noise to data, often a straight line, and generates by solving an ordinary differential equation. Diffusion paths are a special case, and the paper reports more stable training. Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al., 2024) uses rectified flow with a transformer.

### Latent diffusion and the VAE

Denoising pixels is expensive. A **variational autoencoder (VAE)** learns to compress an image into a small **latent** and decode it back. Latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) runs the loop on the latent. Stable Diffusion v1 uses a downsampling factor of 8 with 4 latent channels. The encoder is needed in training only; at generation time you start from latent noise and run the decoder once at the end.

### Text conditioning and classifier-free guidance

A **text encoder** turns the prompt into one vector per token. The denoiser reads them through **cross-attention**: queries come from the image features and keys and values come from the text (see [Self-Attention Explained](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/self-attention-explained) for the mechanism). **Classifier-free guidance** (Ho and Salimans, 2022) trains one network both with and without the prompt, by replacing the prompt with a null token a fraction of the time (0.1 and 0.2 worked about equally well in the paper). At sampling it runs both and extrapolates. A **guidance scale** w above 1 buys closer prompt matching at the cost of variety.

### U-Nets versus diffusion transformers

Early latent diffusion used a **U-Net**, a convolutional network with attention layers. The **diffusion transformer (DiT)** (Peebles and Xie, 2022) swaps it for a transformer on latent patches. In DiT a 256 × 256 × 3 image becomes a 32 × 32 × 4 latent; with patch size 2 that is (32 ÷ 2)² = 256 tokens. Halving the patch size quadruples the tokens. DiT-XL/2 reached an FID of 2.27 on class-conditional ImageNet at 256 × 256, and the same design carries over to video.

**The numbers.** 512 × 512 × 3 = 786,432 values. With f = 8 and 4 channels the latent is 64 × 64 × 4 = 16,384 values. 786,432 ÷ 16,384 = 48, so each step handles 48 times fewer values.

## Deep dive

### What a step does

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/multimodal/diffusion-denoise-steps"
  stepper

**How to read it.** Each panel is `x_t` from the formula above, computed for a kettle-shaped toy picture and one fixed noise sample. Signal is √ᾱ and noise is √(1 − ᾱ). At t = 1000 the signal share is 0.01, so the panel is noise. At t = 300 it is 0.63 and the shape shows. At t = 0 the picture is clean. Real samplers take far fewer than 1,000 steps and jump between noise levels; the panels are snapshots.

### Guidance, computed

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/multimodal/guidance-scale"
  stepper
  caption="Four latent cells with illustrative numbers. The guided prediction starts at the unconditional value and moves w times the prompt's pull."
/>

**The numbers.** The first cell has ε_uncond = 0.10 and ε_cond = 0.30, so the difference is 0.20. At w = 1 the result is 0.30. At w = 7.5 it is 0.10 + 7.5 × 0.20 = 1.60. The paper writes the same rule as (1 + w′) ε_cond − w′ ε_uncond, so w = 1 + w′. Each step needs two denoiser passes, one without and one with the prompt, so guidance roughly doubles the compute of a step unless the passes are batched on spare GPU capacity or the model is distilled to skip it. ### Faster sampling: distillation and fewer steps

Quality needs many small steps, and every step is a full denoiser pass. Two routes cut the count. Better **samplers** solve the same equation in fewer, larger steps. **Distillation** trains a student to reach in a few steps what the teacher reaches in many; adversarial diffusion distillation, for example, generates in 1 to 4 steps by combining score distillation with an adversarial loss. The price is some variety and fine detail, and some distilled models give up the guidance scale, so test quality again after distilling. Measure by prompt sets and human review, as on [Quality Metrics](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/quality-metrics).

### Video: temporal consistency and cost

<AnimatedDiagram
  spec="animated/multimodal/video-spacetime-patches"
  stepper
  caption="A video VAE compresses time and space; patches that span time become tokens. The clip size is illustrative; the compression rules are from the Wan 2.1 report."
/>

**How to read it.** Video models extend the latent idea. OpenAI's Sora report (February 2024) describes a transformer over **spacetime patches** of a compressed video. The Wan 2.1 report gives the arithmetic: its video VAE compresses time, height and width by 4 × 8 × 8 into 16 channels, and its transformer cuts 1 × 2 × 2 patches. An illustrative clip of 81 frames of 832 × 480 gives 1 + 80 ÷ 4 = 21 latent frames of 60 × 104, then 21 × 30 × 52 = 32,760 tokens.

**The numbers.** One 256 × 256 image in DiT is 256 tokens. The clip has 32,760 ÷ 256 = 128 times as many tokens, and attention compares every pair, so about 128² ≈ 16,000 times as many pairs. That is why video models compress hard, attend over limited windows or use efficient attention, and cost far more per second of output than per image.

**Temporal consistency.** Frames must agree with each other: the kettle must keep its shape and the shelf must not drift. Attending across frames helps, but flicker, object changes and impossible motion remain common failures. OpenAI's report itself says its model does not accurately model the physics of many basic interactions.

### Safety, provenance and watermarking

Generated images need the same care as generated text. Screen prompts and outputs for disallowed content (see [Guardrails & Safety](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/guardrails-and-safety)). Add **provenance**: signed metadata such as C2PA Content Credentials records where a file came from and how it changed, and an invisible watermark can survive some edits that strip metadata. Neither is proof on its own; metadata can be removed and watermarks can be damaged. For a shop, the rule is simple: label generated product images, and never present a generated image as a photo of the real item.

### Worked example: a listing image for the kettle

Brightpost generates images for new listings: a stainless steel kettle on a white shelf, 512 × 512. The request above asks for 30 steps with guidance 7.5 (both illustrative).

The denoiser works on a 64 × 64 × 4 latent. With guidance each step is two passes, so one image costs 30 × 2 = 60 denoiser passes and one decoder pass. If a pass takes an illustrative 20 ms, one image takes 60 × 20 = 1,200 ms. A batch of 2,000 listing images a day is 2,000 × 1.2 s = 2,400 GPU-seconds, 40 minutes of one GPU, so the work fits a small queue and a nightly job, not a real-time path. A distilled model at 4 steps with no guidance pass would need 4 passes, 80 ms.

Quality control matters more than speed. Brightpost fixes the seed in the log so a bad image can be reproduced, blocks prompts that name brands it does not sell, and compares each image with the listing text for a wrong colour or an extra spout. Generated images are labelled, and the customer's own cracked-kettle photo from the [vision-language page](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/vision-language-models) is never altered or replaced. The two directions are separate: a VLM reads a customer photo, and a diffusion model writes a catalogue image. See [Multimodal & Voice AI](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/multimodal-and-voice-ai) for both in one picture and [Multimodal RAG](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/multimodal-rag) for retrieving images.

## Trade-offs

| Choice | Pros | Cons | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pixel-space diffusion | No decoder error | Far more values per step; slow | Small images and research |
| Latent diffusion | 48 times fewer values at 512 px; fast enough to serve | VAE limits fine detail such as small text | Default for images and video |
| U-Net denoiser | Mature tooling; efficient at modest sizes | Harder to scale; convolution-based | Existing pipelines |
| Diffusion transformer | Scales with size and tokens; reuses LLM tools | Token count grows with resolution | New large models and video |
| High guidance scale | Closer prompt match | Less variety; over-saturated results | Product shots that must match the prompt |
| Distilled few-step model | 1 to 4 steps; low latency | Less variety; some lose the guidance scale | Interactive tools, high volume |

## Real-world examples

- **DDPM (2020).** Reported an Inception score of 9.46 and an FID of 3.17 on unconditional CIFAR-10, with T = 1000 steps.
- **Latent diffusion and Stable Diffusion (2022).** Rombach et al. found downsampling factors of 4 to 8 gave the best balance; Stable Diffusion v1 uses f = 8 and 4 channels, so 512 px becomes 64 × 64 × 4.
- **DiT (2022).** A transformer on latent patches; DiT-XL/2 reported an FID of 2.27 on ImageNet at 256 × 256.
- **Sora (February 2024) and Wan 2.1 (2025).** Video transformers on spacetime patches; Wan 2.1 is an open model in 1.3 B and 14 B parameter sizes with a 4 × 8 × 8 video VAE.
- **Adversarial diffusion distillation.** Generates images in 1 to 4 steps from a diffusion teacher.

## Common pitfalls

- Describing diffusion as "drawing from a prompt". It denoises; the prompt only steers.
- Forgetting that guidance doubles the passes per step.
- Quoting a step count or pass time as a fact. Both depend on the model, the sampler and the GPU; label them illustrative.
- Treating a distilled model as free. Re-check variety, detail and prompt match.
- Ignoring video token counts. Compression and patching, not the frame count alone, set the cost.
- Shipping generated images without labels, logs or a way to reproduce a bad one.

## How to answer in an interview

1. Say what training does: add noise on a schedule, train a network to predict it. Generation reverses it step by step.
2. Draw the stack: text encoder, denoiser in latent space, VAE decoder, with the size arithmetic (786,432 to 16,384 values).
3. Explain guidance with the formula and its cost: two passes per step, w trades variety for prompt match.
4. Name the cost levers: fewer steps (samplers, distillation), smaller latents, batching, and for video the token count.
5. Close with safety and provenance: filtering, logging seeds, labelling, content credentials.

**What interviewers listen for:** the noise-prediction idea, why latent space is cheaper, the guidance formula and its compute cost, and the token arithmetic for video.

**Red flags:** "the model paints the image from the prompt", no mention of steps or cost, and treating watermarks as proof.
