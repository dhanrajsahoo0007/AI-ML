Real documents combine screenshots, wiring diagrams, tables, charts, and video alongside text. Multimodal RAG indexes each modality into a searchable form and passes the raw evidence to a model that can read it.

## Overview

- A text-only pipeline drops everything that is not text. A screenshot of the sign-in screen is invisible until you give it **words or a vector**.
- Three ways to index an image: a **caption** written by a vision model, **OCR** text, or an **image embedding** in a space shared with text.
- **Multimodal embeddings** (CLIP and its successors) put pictures and text in one space, so a typed question can retrieve a picture directly.
- **Page-image retrieval** (ColPali) skips parsing: it embeds each page as an image and matches the question against patches of the page.
- Tables become rows that keep their header; audio and video become **timestamped transcripts**. The answer cites the page, the region or the timestamp so a person can check it.

## Mental model

- **Pointing at visual evidence:** Ask a colleague "where is the backup code button?" and they will point at the screenshot rather than quote a paragraph; a text search engine cannot point because it never saw the screenshot.
- **Two routes to searchability:** Either describe every picture in words so a text engine can find the words, or teach the engine to compare a question with a picture directly.
- **Seeing the original:** Multimodal RAG uses one or both indexing routes, then hands the picture itself to a vision-language model that can look at it.

## Core concepts

- In [RAG Fundamentals](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/rag-fundamentals), Brightpost has **1,200 articles** (3,600 text chunks).
- Here we add screenshots and tables: say 900 articles carry one screenshot each (illustrative).

### Parsing complex documents: layout, tables and figures

- **Typed page regions:** A layout-aware parser reads a page as typed regions—title, paragraph, list, table, figure, caption, header, and footer (see [Document Processing & Chunking](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/document-processing-chunking)).
- **Preserving figure context:** Instead of dropping figures, the parser keeps each figure as its own element with its heading, alt text, and position before turning it into an index entry.

**The numbers:**

- **Captioning calls:** Captioning 900 screenshots requires 900 vision-model calls once at ingestion (illustrative).
- **Token overhead:** At about 40 tokens per caption, that adds 36,000 tokens to embed—under 3% of the 1,440,000 tokens the text chunks already cost.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  A caption describes what the captioner noticed. "A sign-in screen" without the button label means the question "where is the backup code button?" never matches. Fix: prompt the captioner for the visible labels and the purpose of the screen, store OCR text beside the caption, and spot-check a sample of captions.
</Callout>

### Multimodal embeddings

- **Dual-encoder alignment:** A multimodal embedding model trains an image encoder and a text encoder together so a picture and its sentence land close together.
- **Contrastive training (CLIP):** CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) trained on 400 million internet image-text pairs to predict which caption matches which image in a batch, pulling matching pairs together and pushing others apart.
- **Shared similarity space:** Both modalities map into one space where text and images are compared by cosine similarity (see [Embeddings & Representation Space](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/embeddings-and-representation-space)).

**How to read it:**

- **Step 2 (Text clusters):** The help centre's text chunks cluster by topic in the shared space.
- **Step 3 (Adding images):** The sign-in screenshot lands among sign-in articles, and the parcel tracker lands among delivery articles.
- **Step 4 (Cross-modal query):** Embedding "Where is the backup code button?" with the text encoder places it nearest to both the sign-in screenshot and the chunk "Lost phone: use a backup code", so retrieval returns both.

**The numbers:**

- **Index growth:** At one vector per image, 900 screenshots add 900 vectors to the 3,600 chunk vectors.
- **Model constraints:** Google's `multimodalembedding@001` returns 1,408 dimensions by default and reads at most 32 tokens of text, suiting questions and captions rather than 400-token chunks.

<Callout tone="red" title="What breaks">
  Vectors from different models do not share a space. An image vector from a CLIP-style model cannot be compared with Brightpost's 1,536-dimension text vectors. Fix: embed the question twice, once per model, search each index, and fuse the two lists (see [Hybrid Search & Re-ranking](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/hybrid-search-and-reranking)); or embed everything with one multimodal model whose text side handles long passages.
</Callout>

### Page-image retrieval

- **Bypassing brittle PDF parsing:** Complex PDF parsing is slow and brittle (columns merge, tables break, charts vanish); page-image retrieval skips extraction altogether.
- **Patch-level late interaction:** ColPali (Faysse et al., 2024) feeds each rendered page to a vision-language model that outputs one vector per image patch, matching query tokens against patches with ColBERT-style late interaction (see [Embedding Models & Selection](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/embedding-models-and-selection)).
- **Quality vs storage trade-off:** ColPali beats text-extraction pipelines on the ViDoRe benchmark while being simpler and faster, at the cost of storing many vectors per page instead of a few per chunk.

### Captioning versus native multimodal retrieval

- **Captioning to text:** Converts every non-text item into text to keep one text index, one embedding model, and one retrieval path; it is easy to debug and works with keyword search.
- **Native multimodal retrieval:** Preserves visual signal (layout, colour, shapes, and uncaptioned details) but requires a second model/index or a unified multimodal embedder.
- **Hybrid middle path:** Use captions and OCR for search, then pass the original image to a vision-language model at answer time so the generator sees the real picture.

## Architecture

The pipeline is the one from [RAG Fundamentals](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/rag-fundamentals), with each stage widened.

1. **Parse** with a layout-aware parser. Emit typed elements: text, table rows, images, page renders, audio and video segments, each with source, page or timestamp, and region.
2. **Represent** each element: text and captions through the text embedder; OCR text into the keyword index; images through the multimodal embedder; whole pages through a page-image model if layout matters.
3. **Retrieve** from each index in parallel and fuse the lists with reciprocal rank fusion, then rerank the top candidates.
4. **Generate** with a vision-language model that receives the retrieved text and the retrieved images themselves, not only their captions (see [Vision-Language Models](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/vision-language-models)).
5. **Cite** each claim with its element: article and section, page and region, or timestamp.

## Technical mechanics

### Tables and charts

- **Row-level context:** Keep each table row with its header or serialize it as a sentence ("Backup code: takes about 2 minutes") so it stands alone, while keeping the whole table as a parent for cross-row comparisons.
- **Structured SQL routing:** Large numeric tables belong in a database where queries are answered with SQL (see [Text-to-SQL & Structured Data](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/text-to-sql-and-structured-data)).
- **Chart indexing:** Vision models can describe a chart's trend but misread exact bar or line values; index the caption, axis labels, and description—or index the underlying data table when available.

### Audio and video retrieval

- **Timestamped transcription:** An ASR model such as Whisper (Radford et al., 2022; trained on 680,000 hours of audio) produces a timestamped transcript that is chunked by time (30–60 seconds, illustrative) or speaker turn with start and end times preserved.
- **Video keyframes:** Add sampled keyframes or scene changes, embedded as images or captioned and aligned with the transcript.
- **Combined retrieval:** A query like "how do I add a backup phone?" retrieves both the transcript chunk ("video 4, 01:42 to 02:15") and the on-screen frame at that moment (see [Speech: ASR & TTS](/ai-system-design/genai-system-design/speech-asr-and-tts)).

### Citing visual evidence

- **Modality-specific locators:** Cite article and section for text, page number and bounding box for PDFs (for UI highlighting), thumbnail for images, and a timestamp playback link for audio or video.
- **Ingest-time capture:** Store all locators at ingestion because they cannot be recovered at answer time.
- **Cite the source, not the caption:** When retrieval matches a caption, cite the original image rather than the generated caption, which could be wrong.

## Trade-offs

| Approach | Strength | Cost | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Caption with a vision model | One text index; readable; works with BM25 | One vision call per image; loses what it omits | Screenshots and photos with a clear subject |
| OCR text | Cheap; exact labels and codes | No layout or meaning | Screens, forms, scanned text |
| Multimodal embedding (CLIP-style) | Matches pictures directly | Second model and index; short text input | Image-heavy corpora, visual search |
| Page-image retrieval (ColPali) | No parsing; keeps layout | Many vectors per page | PDFs with complex layouts, charts, slides |
| Transcribe audio and video | Reuses the text pipeline | ASR errors; loses tone and visuals | Calls, meetings, tutorials |

## Production systems

- Google Vertex AI's multimodal embedding model puts image, text and video embeddings in one space with the same dimensions, so images can be searched with text; the default is 1,408 dimensions and text input is capped at 32 tokens.
- ColPali's authors released their models and the ViDoRe benchmark of page-level retrieval tasks across domains and languages, which makes page-image retrieval testable on your own documents.
- Whisper is widely used to turn meeting recordings and support calls into timestamped transcripts that a text RAG pipeline can index.

## Failure modes & pitfalls

- Dropping images and tables at parsing, then wondering why "where is the button?" questions fail.
- Comparing vectors from two different embedding models as if they shared a space.
- Indexing captions and citing the caption, so a captioning mistake becomes a confident wrong answer.
- Sending only captions to the generator when a vision-language model could read the picture.
- Losing page numbers, regions and timestamps at ingestion, so answers cannot point at their evidence.

## Interview framework

1. Ask which modalities matter and how questions refer to them: by words, by layout, or by what something looks like.
2. Start with the cheapest path: layout-aware parsing, captions plus OCR for images, rows with headers for tables, transcripts for audio.
3. Add a multimodal embedding index or page-image retrieval only where evaluation shows text-only retrieval misses.
4. Fuse the indexes, rerank, and pass the original images to a vision-language model for the answer.
5. Cite pages, regions and timestamps, and evaluate per modality.

**What interviewers listen for:** knowing that each modality needs its own route into the index, and that vectors from different models are not comparable.

**Red flags:** "a multimodal model will handle it" with no plan for parsing, citations or evaluation.
